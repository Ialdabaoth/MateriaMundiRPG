## Vehicles and Mounts

Wagons and horses make overland travel much more convenient in some ways, and less so in others. A wagon pulled by horses or oxen travels at one league per two hours, and restricts the party’s movements to maintained roads, but gives the party a mobile ‘base of operations’ to rest, store supplies, and use as makeshift cover during an encounter.

A well-built wagon pulled by an ox or two horses can typically carry up to a thousand pounds of supplies while still leaving room for up to four medium-sized passengers. A smaller cart, usually pulled by a mule, can carry up to 500 pounds of supplies, or half that while also comfortably carrying a single passenger.

A good quality mount, on the other hand, will often both be faster than walking and able to manage some amount of off-road travel; a set of good unencumbered horses might double the party’s travel speed along the road, while more exotic mounts might negate specific kinds of difficult terrain if the mount is native to that terrain. Mounts tend to need more frequent rest than humans walking on foot, and obviously their food and water needs must be taken into account.

### Mounts

A good riding mount allows a party to carry more gear without encumbrance and ignore some kinds of rough terrain, but does not otherwise increase travel speed. A fast horse might be able to double its overland speed for up to an hour – traveling up to 2 leagues at a full gallop - but would then need a full hour of rest. Pushing a horse past this limit is likely to wound or even kill it.

### Carts

The simplest option for a land-vehicle is a cart: a driver's seat, a box, two wheels, and a hitch for a horse or a pair of mules. A cart will generally have one to six structure points, depending on materials and craftsmanship, and can generally carry up to 500 lb worth of gear. Room can be made for up to two medium-sized passengers or five small passengers; each medium passenger reduces its load capacity by 250 lbs, while each small passenger reduces its load capacity by 100 lb.

### Wagons

The next step up is a wagon, with four wheels and a covered box, an extra seat next to the driver's, and a hitch for four horses or a pair of oxen. A wagon will have between two and twelve structure points, and can carry up to 2,000 lbs worth of gear, with passengers taking up room as above.

### Carriages

Carriages are essentially fancy wagons designed for passenger comfort. A carriage  is usually hitched to four horses; it has between five and fifteen structure points, and has room for 500 lbs worth of gear plus six dedicated seats for medium passengers.

## Mounts and Beasts of Burden

Domesticated animals can be acquired by the party and used for various purposes. Genuine animal companions, such as a trusty warhorse or a mastiff, can be trained to obey commands and even fight alongside their master in battle. Other animals will behave, or not, according to their nature. Survival can be used both to predict how an animal is about to behave, and to try to get it to do what you want from moment to moment.

When an animal is acquired, the referee might make a few D6 rolls to give it a rudimentary personality. These traits will influence how easy, or hard, it is to get the animal to perform specific actions.
