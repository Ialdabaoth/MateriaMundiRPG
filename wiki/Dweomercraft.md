## Dweomercraft

At its heart, dweomercraft is the design and creation of arcane magic items and alchemical formulae. Dwarven dweomersmiths dedicate their lives to the pursuit of this craft, hoping to craft legacies that will last for generations.

Dwarven magic is most similar to human arcane magic, and in fact the first human wizards discovered its principles by studying dwarven dweomercraft. Much like arcane spellcraft, dweomercraft involves the precise alignment of arcana and materia to channel an elemental or aetheric effect, and this practice requires similar levels of intelligence, precision, and experience to master.

But in other respects, dweomercraft is similar to human divine magic. Dwarven craft-priests teach that the Divine Principle crafted the world, and then crafted mortals in Its own image, and as such “divine miracles” and "arcane spells" are simply an imitation of that original process of creation – an imitation which should be performed with utmost care, pride, and humble reverence.

### Magic Item Creation

Dwarves were the first mortal beings to learn to craft magic items. Dweomercraft treats magic as a natural force, to be understood and shaped like any other. Magic which does not fit into this paradigm is avoided as "unstable" or "dangerous", while magic which can be properly understood and tamed becomes part of dwarven engineering.

Dwarves have developed tools, rituals and practices over the millennia to make arcane magic item creation faster, easier, and more reliable. Dwarven magic items usually begin as prepared *enchantments*, which are modular prototypes that require regular maintenance to function. Once a Dwarf is satisfied with an enchantment, they can construct a permanent magic item with the same powers, usually by modifying and refining the prototype itself.

### Prepared Dweomers

A dwarf crafting a dweomer creates a volatile alchemical formula or temporary magic item out of raw materia and any other available materials. Preparing a dweomer uses the same alchemical formula or magic item blueprint as creating a permanent version of that object, but is much faster.

Every dwarf is taught from childhood how to assist with the preparation of enchantments and formulae; even those who go on to become stalwarts or delvers know how to assist dweomersmiths at the forge or brewers at the distillery. Those who show real talent are apprenticed at a young age to a master crafter, who teaches them to improvise various alchemies and enchantments with astonishing speed.

#### Volatile Alchemical Formulae

A volatile alchemical formula is identical to a normal formula, but loses its potency at the next sunrise or sunset. It must be periodically maintained during the day by the alchemist that brewed it; inspecting it for a few seconds once per hour is sufficient to keep its energies stable. The net result is a potion that uses and occupies a preparation of the appropriate circle until imbibed or otherwise used.

Once an alchemical formula is used, attention is freed up for the brewer to prepare another one, or to prepare any other formula of the same rarity or magic of the same circle.

#### Magic Item Enchantments

Similarly, a magic item enchantment uses arcane ink and materia to temporarily enchant any common object with a magic item effect.

The enchantment lasts until the next sunrise or sunset, and must be maintained during the day by the enchanter that made it; touching the enchantment and inspecting it for a few moments once per hour is sufficient to keep its energies stable. The net result is effectively a functional magic item, which occupies a preparation of the appropriate circle.

Magic item enchantments may be recharged using the same process as constructing them, and may be disenchanted during the day to free up their preparation, allowing the dwarf or wizard to prepare a new enchantment or other magic instead.

### Dweomersmith Rituals

Dweomersmiths also learn more elaborate rituals as part of their class training. A dweomersmith may analyze or recharge a magic item in two exploration turns; at third level, they may perform the unbind cursed item ritual in four turns; and at fifth level, they may perform the hallow ritual dweomer in six turns.

| | |
|---|---|
| Analyze Magic Item | *First Circle* |

You perform this ritual while holding an object or item with an empty hand, or while touching a piece of a larger structure. You learn its magical properties, how many charges or uses it has, and how to activate it. You also learn whether any active spells or other supernatural effects are affecting it, their approximate power levels, and the rough nature of those effects.

| | |
|---|---|
| Mend | *First Circle* |

As an exploration action, you repair up to 2d6 breaks or tears in a single damaged non-living object, such as broken chain links, two halves of a broken key, a tear in a cloak, or a leak in a wineskin. As long as each break or tear is no larger than 1 foot in any dimension, you mend it, leaving no trace of the former damage. You may also repair all of a weapon or armor’s reduction in performance from a sundering effect. This dweomer can physically repair a magic item or construct, but the blessing can’t restore magic to such an object.

| | |
|---|---|
| Recharge Magic Item | *Second Circle* |

You can perform this ritual while holding any arcane magic item with an expended charge, then make a Science check and a Finesse check, with a difficulty based on the item's rarity; if both rolls succeed, the item regains one charge. Each time you attempt this ritual, you use one charge of materia of an element appropriate to the magic item.

| | |
|---|---|
| Unbind Cursed Item | *Second Circle* |

You touch a cursed object that is bonded to a mortal and offer a prayer of deliverance, then make a Science check. If you succeed, the item relinquishes its hold. Your Science check is easy for a common cursed item, or hard for a rare item. This ritual will not un-curse an item, but only un-bonds it from a mortal.

| | |
|---|---|
| Consecrate Workspace | *Third Circle* |

You bless a workspace up to 20 paces in diameter and all of its tools and equipment, while tidying and organizing and tending to any necessary minor repairs. Until the next sunrise or sunset, all rolls that you make to design or craft an item or alchemical formula within the space are easier, and all alchemy and crafting tasks to produce a permanent item take half as much time as normal.

If multiple dwarves assist with the ritual, they can increase the size of the workspace, and all participants gain the effect's bonuses. Most dwarven forges are consecrated daily at each sunrise and sunset; after one lunar month of this, the effects become permanent until the workspace is damaged or the blessing is dispelled.
