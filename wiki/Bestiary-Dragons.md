## Dragons

Before the world belonged to mortals, it was the domain of the dragons.

When the Starfall destroyed their civilization, a precious few used draconic magic to escape into the Elemental Chaos, where they waited until they could return to the Prime Material. Those that survived found the world changed, host to the mammalian mortal races and their fey echoes. A great schism formed within Dragonkind, over whether to wipe out these furry upstarts and restore the world to its former glory, or aid the new mortal races as they reached for heights that only the elder dragons fully understood.

The so-called Celestial Dragons began guiding human civilization from its earliest beginnings. Indeed, some of them claim that it was they who directly shaped humanity from the small, furry creatures inhabiting the savannas deep to the south of Khemet’s Itru river. Regardless of the truth, the celestial dragons aided mortalkind in the discovery of agriculture, medicine, and magic.

The Primordial Dragons, on the other hand, set about to restore their civilization. Enough of their lizardfolk servants survived with them to begin rebuilding their civilization, and so the primordials and their kobold servants began to colonize the tropical jungles that most reminded them of their homes. The Primordial Dragons of the Jambud jungles even welcomed serpentfolk centaurs into their ranks, bolstering their numbers in the early days of their new empire.

**Dragonscale —** In addition to hit points, all dragons’ hides are protected by a thick layer of dragonscale. Before any hit point damage can be dealt to a dragon, this armor must be penetrated.

## Draconic Lizardfolk and Serpentfolk

Lizardfolk and serpentfolk under the sway of a dragon are not technically orcs, but the distinction is often lost on civilized mortals. They still practice a form of ritualized cannibalism, and often perform gruesome blood sacrifices for their dragon masters.

## Cockatrice and Basilisk

Cockatrices and Basilisks are a form of devolved Earth-aspected Primordial Dragon.

While millennia of inbreeding and mutation have resulted in a degenerate species of dragon with only animalistic intelligence, a cockatrice still poses a significant danger to an adventuring party. All cockatrices are male, while all basilisks are female.

At first glance, a cockatrice appears to be a scaly cross between a lizard, a bat, and a rooster, with four bird-like legs, a long serpentine tail, and a cockscomb. Adult cockatrices rarely grow larger than three feet in length from beak to tail.

Basilisks, on the other hand, appear to be massive six-legged crocodiles with a grey, stone-like hide. They often possess a crown of short, thick horns adorning the backs of their heads.

A cockatrice or basilisk’s Earth Pearl is visible on its forehead, and both its Pearl and its milky-white eyes glow with an eerie light.

Any creature that catches the cockatrice or basilisk’s gaze is stunned, and hobbled until they save to shake it off. Any creature that continues to meet its gaze is turned to stone over several seconds. Even a partial glance is excruciatingly painful, as thin slivers of stone begin growing and spreading within the victim’s muscles and other tissues.

### Celestial Dragon

*(Level 8)*  

While smaller and weaker than their Primordial kin, a Celestial dragon is still a terrifying force of nature. Many Celestial dragons set themselves up as local gods and protectors, much like the Fair Folk; some of these beings have even become Numina under the aegis of the holy Church, taking their place within the Faith as incarnate protectors of a place or people. A Celestial Dragon numen’s various lizardfolk and serpentfolk servants find themselves in an awkward position, as does the Church; so long as the cannibalistic fertility rites necessary for their reproduction are performed willingly, posthumously, and only upon their own kind, the representatives of the Church in most Successor Kingdoms will do their best to look the other way.

While Primordial Dragons are colored based on the elemental energies they have infused into their bodies, the scales of Celestial Dragons are typically a brilliant array of metallic hues and lustrous gem-like inclusions. No two Celestial Dragons have the same coloration, but translucent scales of ruby-red, jade-green, amethyst-purple, and turquoise-blue are common, usually in alternating stripes against metallic shades of bronze, copper, silver, gold, or rainbow metallic patinas.

All Celestial dragons are made, not born. A dragonborn lizardfolk that has lived for a century and proven itself worthy undergoes a primal ritual, where they consume and merge with a Dragon Pearl over an arduous monthlong process of transformation. Once its mortality has been fully burned away by the materia’s power, the new Celestial dragon takes its place as a leader of its tribe.

Celestial Dragons are much less inimical to humankind than their Primordial cousins.

Some celestial dragons claim to have been the source of the myth of the fabled Serpents of Paradise - a corrupted memory of their original role, they claim, of uplifting the ancestors of human and dwarfkind from simple apes into true thinking beings.

Certainly, many celestial dragons show credible linkages to ancient serpent-cults in prehistoric Europa and Asia.

### Primordial Wyrmling

*(Level 9)*  

While Celestial dragons are impressively powerful beings, they are not the pinnacle of draconic potential. Before the Starfall, Celestial dragons discovered a means to transform themselves into a new class of being, the Primordial dragon, through a dark and bloody ritual.

A Celestial dragon must tear out its own Dragon Pearl, alchemically refine it, and then bathe it in the heartsblood of thousands of worthy mortals over the course of a century. Once the pearl is sufficiently anointed it is reimplanted, and the transformation into a Primordial wyrm is rapid and spectacular.

Primordial wyrmlings are ravenously hungry both for meat and precious metals, which their bodies alchemically smelt into orichalc and feysilver. These materials are incorporated into the wyrmling’s scales as it grows, giving it nearly impenetrable armor and greatly enhancing its magical abilities.

**Dragonscale -** In addition to hit points, a dragon’s hide is protected by a thick layer of dragonscale. Before any hit point damage can be dealt to the dragon, this armor must be penetrated. If the line is stopped short by a barrier, roll each damage die one at a time against the barrier until it gives way; if it does, the line continues past the barrier reduced by the number of damage dice rolled to penetrate it.
