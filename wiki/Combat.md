## Combat Encounters

When combat begins, each creature that isn't surprised gets to act in the first round, which is also called the *surprise round*. After the surprise round is over, all creatures in the encounter who are capable of action may do so.

### Combat Actions

Each creature that can act declares their action at the start of the round. Possible actions are:

Attack (speed: per weapon) – move up to 5 paces and make a

weapon attack. Making a ranged weapon attack or attempting

to move out of melee will provoke attacks of opportunity.

Dash (speed: normal) – move up to 20 paces if unencumbered,

or up to 10 paces if encumbered.

Evade (speed: fast) – move up to 5 paces, and it is harder to

hit you for the rest of this round. You must be unencumbered

and have a Dexterity of +1 or better to evade.

Guard (speed: fast) - You can perform one opportunity attack

each phase, and you may move up to 2 paces between each

phase. Only fighter classes can guard.

Magic (speed: slow) - Attempt to cast a spell, glamour, or

miracle, or use a magic item. You must declare what you are

attempting to cast, and you cannot move while casting.

Task (speed: normal) – perform some other action (such as

attempting to hide, or interacting with the environment in some

way). The referee will determine whether you can also move.

### Order of Actions

After each creature declares their action, combat proceeds in three phases. First, all fast actions occur; then, all normal actions occur, and finally, all slow actions occur. Actions will normally happen simultaneously; if the order of two actions in a single phase matters, each acting creature can make a Dexterity check, with the winner choosing whether to act first or last.

**Evading** and **Guarding** are special actions, in that they focus more on reacting to other creatures and events rather than performing a single action. Evading lets you move during the fast phase, and then you are harder to hit for the round. Guarding skips your action, but lets you move 2 paces and make an opportunity attack during each phase of the round.

### Action and Movement

When it's your turn to act, you may move as part of your action. Each character has a natural movement rate of 5 paces (25 feet) over even ground; difficult terrain or a heavy burden halves this movement speed, to three paces per round. Dashing increases your movement to 20 paces per round while you are unencumbered, at the cost of not being able to do anything else that round. If you are within reach of another creature’s melee attacks, you provoke an opportunity attack from it if you attempt to move out of its reach.

### Encumbrance

You are encumbered if you are wearing too much gear or carrying too heavy a load. Your maximum carrying capacity is equal to 6 + your Strength ability. In addition to armor and weapons, each 25 lb of carried gear or treasure adds +1 to encumbrance. An oversized weapon or gear such as a polearm will also add an additional +1.

As long as your carried armor and gear isn't more than half your carrying capacity, you can move and act normally. Past this, up to your carrying capacity, you are encumbered; the dash rate of an encumbered character is halved to 5 paces per phase, and you can't evade. Past your full carrying capacity, you are burdened – your speed is halved (to 3 paces per combat round), your actions are always slow, and you can't dash or evade.

### Magic in Combat

Casting in a combat encounter is slower and riskier than other combat actions. You must declare to the referee what effect you intend to cast. If another creature hits you with an attack after you declare your effect but before you can cast it, you must save vs. Doom or your magic is disrupted.

#### Fast Magics

Some magics are fast to perform. A fast magic can be performed in reaction to another action in combat, even if the caster hadn't originally declared that they were casting magic this round. They simply switch their action to casting the magic, then do so.

### Reacting to Other Creatures

Each creature that can act can also react to other creatures' actions even if it isn't their turn to act yet, as long as the acting creature’s action affects the reacting creature.

Usually, you only get to either act or react to one thing in a round; once you've reacted, you can't perform any more actions or reactions for the rest of the round. This means that if you react to something before it's your turn to act, you will lose your action.

The most straightforward reaction is the **opportunity attack**. If you've declared the attack or guard action and haven't made your attack yet, you can immediately make your attack against it if it attempts to perform a **provoking** action while within reach of your melee weapon. Since you're attacking before your attack would normally happen, you don't also get to move.

Your own attack interrupts its action; if you hit, the creature you hit loses the rest of its action. The most common provoking actions that a creature can make are attempting to step away from you, attempting to make a ranged attack, or attempting to cast magic.

If you're a fighter class (Warrior, Stalwart or Champion), the **guard** action lets a you give up you own attack action to allow you to make multiple opportunity attacks in a round instead. You can make an opportunity attack in response to one fast action, one normal action, and one slow action, and you can move up to 2 paces in between the fast and normal phases of the round, and again in between the normal and slow phases.

Whenever you successfully save vs. Blast against an area effect that might harm you, you can perform another reaction, called a **dodge**. To dodge, you make a hard Dexterity check, then move that many paces before the blast goes off. If you manage to get out of the blast area, it doesn't affect you at all.

Another kind of reaction that you can make is a **parry**, in response to an attack that's about to hit you. You need to be wielding a weapon that has the parry property in order to parry; if you are wielding such a weapon, you can give up your own attack to force the attacker to re-roll their attack.

### Stunts and Maneuvers

The following options are a common, but by no means exhaustive, list of combat maneuvers that may be performed if the circumstances favor them. The referee and players are also welcome to come up with new maneuvers to add to their own game.

#### Brace

If you are wielding a polearm with the piercing property, you may brace it into the ground as you choose the guard action. You choose a direction as you brace your weapon; if a creature charges you from that direction, you may make an opportunity attack against it which gains +1d6 damage for each 5 full paces the target moved towards you, and which ignores even heavy armor.

#### Charge

A charge combines a dash and attack into a single action. Charging is always a slow action, happening in the last phase of the round. For every 5 full paces that you move directly towards your target, your attack gains +1d6 damage. If you hit, your target is shoved backwards, and you end your movement in the target's space.

#### Disarm or Pilfer

You can attempt to disarm a target with your attack, but doing so makes your attack throw harder. If you hit, and one or more damage dice exceed your Finesse threshold, your opponent must make a save vs. Hold or drop their weapon. A Scout can attempt to disarm or pick a pocket by making a Finesse check instead of a weapon damage roll after hitting with a melee attack. If you succeed, you pilfer or plant one reachable item of your choice.

#### Grab or Shove

You can attempt to shove or grab a target by making a melee attack roll with a difficulty based on the relative size of the target. A grab attack must be made with a free hand or a flail weapon, while a shove can be made with any melee attack. If you hit, the target makes a save vs. Hold to halve the damage and avoid being grabbed, moved, or knocked prone.

#### Wait

If you want to wait for a specific event to occur before performing your action, you can do so. For example, you might want to attack an enemy as it moves past you.

### Combat Conditions

Concealed – a creature can be partially or totally concealed by

cover. A partially concealed creature is harder to hit, while a

fully concealed creature cannot be attacked unless the attacker

has some way of knowing where they are.

Hobbled / Prone – Various attacks or other circumstances

might hobble a creature. A hobbled creature can only move 2

paces per round in an encounter, and can’t dash or evade. A

creature might sometimes be able to end this condition with a

successful save vs. Hold. Being knocked prone is similar to

being hobbled, but can stand up during their next action unless

they're somehow restrained from doing so.

Panicked – A panicked creature can’t attack, and must attempt

to move as far away from the source of its fear (dashing if

possible). If it can’t move, it cowers in place as far away as it

can get.

Stunned – A stunned creature loses its action for the rest of

the combat round, causing it to be unable to act, react, or

move. However, it isn’t considered helpless.

Unconscious / Helpless – A helpless creature can’t move, act,

or react, and all attacks against it are easy. An unconscious

creature is helpless, and also completely unaware of its

surroundings. At the end of the round, an unconscious creature

makes an Alertness check to attempt to wake up.

Injured – A creature that has lost half of its hit points is

injured, and will need either magic, or time and medical

attention, in order to heal.

Dying – A creature with zero hp is dying. A dying creature is

also unconscious, and whenever it suffers further  damage, it

must save vs. Doom or die.

At the end of each round, after all other creatures have acted,

each dying creature must save vs. Doom or die. If its saving

throw rolls a ‘20’, it stabilizes to one hit point and is no longer

dying, but it’s still unconscious and helpless until it wakes up.

### Critical Hits

If an attack throw rolls a ‘20’, it’s a critical hit. Scouts and hunters can also sneak attack to cause a critical hit if the damage dice roll high enough, and all fighters achieve a critical hit whenever their attack roll beats its threshold by 10 or more.

When a creature suffers a critical hit, it immediately saves vs. Doom after the main attack damage is applied. If it successfully saves, it loses half its current hit points (round the loss down, so a successful save will never drop a creature to zero hit points); if it fails, its hit points drop to zero and it is dying.

### Damage and Ongoing Conditions

Whenever you inflict damage with an attack, if any of the damage dice roll a ‘6’, an additional effect is inflicted on the target. This effect depends on the type of damage inflicted.

The attacker can also force the affected target to make a save vs. Hold to avoid being knocked down or pushed back (attacker’s choice) by the force of the blow, if they wish. This saving throw is easy if only one ‘6’ was rolled in the damage roll, or hard if two or more ‘6’s were rolled.

#### Weapon Damage

Physical attacks that roll a '6' usually inflict ongoing **bleeding**. A bleeding creature loses hit points equal to half its level (round up) at the end of each round until it saves vs. Doom to stop bleeding; it or an ally can use its action to make a Medicine check to attempt to staunch the flow.

Physical attacks with the **blunt** property stun instead of dealing ongoing damage, causing the target to lose their next action when any damage die rolls a '6'.

Physical attacks with the **piercing** property ignore light or medium armor when inflicting their damage.

Attacks with the **sundering** property reduce the quality of the target’s armor by 1 per ‘6’ rolled until repaired. If the target has a shield, each ‘6’ rolled affects the shield instead.

#### Elemental Damage

Elemental damage effects depend on the element. Many elemental attacks, such as acid, poison, or fire, can inflict ongoing damage. Many others, such as psychic, cold, thunder, lightning, or radiant, might stun the target. Thunder and radiant damage can also affect the target's senses, deafening or blinding them for a round. Cold or petrification might hobble or restrain the target until they break free, and acid can sunder armor or weapons.

Psychic damage might frighten or confuse the target for a round.
