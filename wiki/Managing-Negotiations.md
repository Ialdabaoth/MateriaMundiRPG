## Managing Negotiations

The player characters will often want to try talking their way out of (or into) situations. This should be encouraged whenever possible; combat is a hazard that sane adventurers should wish to avoid whenever possible.

## During Exploration

Most social interactions operate at roughly the same scale as exploration - each round is approximately 10 minutes, and involves a single task check made by each participant.

This allows some characters to engage socially while other members of the party perform non-social exploration actions.

As such, each task check performed during a social interaction should determine the consequences of an entire 10-minute conversation. The simplest procedure for the referee is to have the characters performing non-social actions roll their task checks first, and then roleplay out any conversation that’s occurring during the same exploration turn.

At the end of that conversation (or at a more relevant point during the exploration turn, if you decide that a non-social interaction interrupts that conversation), you should appraise the goals of each participant, as well as “how the conversation is going”, to determine which talents are relevant and whether any rolls should be easy or hard.

## During Encounters

It is also possible to use social talents during an encounter. A single encounter round is only six seconds, so each character is limited to a few words or gestures in their attempts to communicate. This is still enough to allow for various attempts to persuade, deceive, intimidate, or otherwise sway the course of the encounter through words rather than bloodshed.

## Which Talent to Use

Direct, non-deceptive persuasion typically involves a Charm or Wit check, with the target’s current attitude (Helpful, Friendly, Indifferent, Unfriendly, or Hostile) combining with the details of the conversation to determine whether rolls are easy or hard.

Charm is generally used to influence attitude, while Wit is generally used to directly influence behavior, although there might be exceptions based on the actual conversation. Above all, the actual conversation that is being roleplayed should always take precedence over any mere mechanical considerations.

Attempts to directly coerce or intimidate will typically involve a Bearing check, while attempts to lie or mislead via “fast-talk”

tactics will typically involve a Wit check.

Flattery, flirtation and other forms of deceptive manipulation might instead use a Charm check. A direct debate over issues will likely involve both sides rolling Wit, with one side “gaining ground” only if they succeed and the other side fails simultaneously.

Discussions over knowledge or technical minutiae might even invoke a Lore, Medicine, Science, or Survival roll.

Characters who do not share each other’s language will often be at a disadvantage when attempting to communicate with each other.

A character’s Lore is useful for attempting to learn a new language, and might indicate some basic familiarity with some of those language’s words and concepts. Language is typically less of a barrier when using Charm than when using Wit, as non-verbal cues become more relevant. Different cultural rules of etiquette and behavior can also come into play, making various rolls easy or hard as appropriate. As with languages, Lore is useful for attempting to pick up the local customs, and for determining whether a character might already be aware of some of them.

Actual familiarity with local languages and customs cuts through all of this, allowing characters to converse and persuade without difficulty.
