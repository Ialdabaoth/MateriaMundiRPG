Creating a character takes four steps. Each step links to the page with the full rules.

## Step 1: Roll Origin

Roll a die to determine your origin, based on a table that the referee will provide to you; or, the referee might instead decide to allow each player to choose their origin. See [[Origins|E9-Origins]].

The most common origin for most characters will usually be human; humans typically represent the common baseline that other races are measured against. Most non-humans have two enhanced abilities and one deficient ability (at least, enhanced or deficient compared to humans). Note these; you'll use them in the next step.

## Step 2: Roll Abilities

Roll 3d6 for each of your abilities, in this order: **Constitution, Strength, Dexterity, Intelligence, Wisdom, Charisma**. The [ability table](E9-Abilities#ability-table) converts each roll into a number between −3 and +3, which measures how good (or bad) you are at actions related to that ability.

Characters with a non-human origin will have one or two enhanced abilities, and possibly one deficient ability. If the ability you are rolling is an **enhanced** ability for your origin, reroll the lowest die and choose the better roll before adding the dice together. If it is a **deficient** ability, reroll the highest die and choose the worse roll. Human characters do not have any enhanced or deficient abilities, but the referee might allow the player of a human character to swap one pair of abilities, to represent human versatility.

### Step 2a: Attack and Saving Throws

Your character has two attack throw thresholds and six saving throw thresholds. Your melee attack throw is adjusted by your Strength, while your ranged attack throw is adjusted by your Dexterity. Each of your six saving throw thresholds is adjusted by a different ability (see [Saving Throws](E9-Abilities#saving-throws)).

Each attack throw or saving throw threshold starts at 14+ if your ability is average (+0). A positive ability lowers your threshold, while a negative ability raises it. The result is the threshold you compare to a d20 roll whenever you make that attack throw or saving throw.

| Ability | −3 | −2 | −1 | +0 | +1 | +2 | +3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Attack or saving throw | 17+ | 16+ | 15+ | 14+ | 13+ | 12+ | 11+ |

### Step 2b: Adjust Talents

Each character has twelve [[Talents|E9-Talents]], each of which relates to different adventuring tasks. Each talent has a threshold from 6+ to 2+; whenever you perform a task related to that talent, you roll a d6 and compare it to your threshold for that talent.

By default, you have a 5+ threshold in each talent. If you have any abilities above or below +0, some talents will begin at a 6+ or 4+ threshold instead. Each talent is linked to an ability; for each ability that is below or above +0, determine which of its linked talents begin better or worse than normal.

## Step 3: Choose Your Character Class

Each origin has a number of [[Classes|E9-Classes]] to choose from: at least one fighter class, one expert class, and one magic-user class. Each class relies on abilities in different ways. Fighters benefit from higher Strength and Constitution, experts from higher Dexterity and Wisdom, and magic-users from higher Intelligence, Wisdom, or Charisma, depending on the nature of the magic that they wield.

### Step 3a: Set Hit Points

Your starting maximum hit points are equal to **8 + your Constitution** if you are a fighter, or **6 + your Constitution** if you are an expert or magic-user.

### Step 3b: Note Feats

Each class gains one or more feats at various levels. Note any feats gained at level 1.

## Step 4: Choose Starting Equipment

Choose any starting equipment that is reasonable for your character to have. Each class has a list of recommended starting equipment, or the referee might give you a list of options to choose from. See [[Weapons|E9-Weapons]] and [[Armor and Shields|E9-Armor-and-Shields]].
