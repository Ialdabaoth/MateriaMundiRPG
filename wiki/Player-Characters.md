## Player Characters

## Origin

Every character has an origin, representing their cultural and ethnic background. This is much broader than our world's ethnicities - this is a world with dwarves, elves, and even stranger creatures! Some origins even have a few abilities that tend to be higher or lower, on average, than a human’s.

Your origin will determine which character classes you can take. Each origin always has one martial class, one expert class, and one magic-user class to choose from, and any origin can choose to become a Priest. Within those choices, your abilities determine which classes you would be better or worse at.

## Abilities

Your character has six primary abilities, which define how tough, strong, nimble, smart, perceptive, and outgoing they are compared to the average person. Each of these abilities ranges from -3 (severely disabled) to +3 (world-class prodigy), with most humans typically having abilities of +0. An ability of -1 or lower is noticeably worse than the average human's, while an ability of +1 or higher is noticeably better.

### Rolling Abilities

When you create a character, you will roll 3d6 and add the dice together for each of these abilities, generating a number from 3 to 18 which will then convert into a -3 to +3 ability value. Certain origins will provide you with enhanced or deficient abilities; you roll 4d6 for those abilities, then ignore either the highest or lowest die and add the rest together to determine that ability.

You always roll your abilities in order; this might result in a character with exceptionally high intelligence but abysmally low wisdom, but such is the way of things sometimes. As an option, the referee might decide to allow anyone playing a human character to choose and swap a single pair of abilities after rolling. Barring serious injury or miraculous blessing, these numbers will be your character’s innate abilities throughout your career — you can increase your skill at anything with sufficient training and effort, but raw inborn ability will always be what it is.

### What Abilities Do

Here are each of the six abilities, in order, along with a brief description:

#### Strength

*is your character’s raw physical*  

power, including their muscle strength and speed. It determines how well (and how hard) they can hit with melee weapons, how much they can lift and carry, and their general athleticism.

#### Constitution

*is your character’s toughness,*  

endurance, and health. It determines how long they can stay in a fight, as well as how well they resist poisons and diseases.

#### Dexterity

*is your character’s hand-eye*  

coordination, balance, and agility (although some facets of agility are a matter of strength rather than dexterity). It determines how well you can avoid getting hit, and gives you an edge with various trainable physical talents.

#### Intelligence

*is your character’s logical thinking*  

and deduction capability. It determines how well you notice out-of-place details, and gives you an edge with various mental talents.

#### Wisdom

*is your character’s intuitive awareness*  

and common sense. It determines how well you resist various magical or mind-controlling effects, and gives you an edge with various trainable intuitive talents.

#### Charisma

*is your character’s courage, spirit,*  

and likeability. It determines how well you resist being frightened or discouraged, and gives you an edge with various social talents.

## Character Class

This game tracks your character’s talents and experience through a concept called a *character*

*class*. Your character class is an abstraction

that represents the general focus of your character’s talents as an adventurer; each class focuses on a different aspect of the game — fighter classes focus on combat prowess, expert classes focus on skills and tricks, and magic-user classes focus on magic and the supernatural. As you advance, your character will gradually get better at the talents that your character class focuses on, while also improving their general ability to survive hardship and danger.
