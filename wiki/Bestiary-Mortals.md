## Natural Mortals

The only two species of mortal known to be fully native to the Material Plane are humans and dwarves, who evolved together before the Flood.

## Humans

*Enhanced Abilities: None*

Humans are one of the two original species of mortal on the Prime Material, seemingly unaffected by its collision with the Aether. Originating from the savannas of the Southern continent, humans spread north during the last ice age to eke out a living in every environment imaginable. If not for the coming of magic, it is likely that humans would have out-competed the dwarves and driven them to extinction.

Because they conquered the surface world while dwarves built their underground empires, humans are something like the ‘default’ species of the world. In some human cultures, non-human prejudice is almost nonexistent, while in others it borders on rabid xenophobia. Most civilized places, though, find the rewards of diversity too rich to pay the costs of racism.

By the time of the Old Empire, humans and dwarves had largely set aside their differences. The combination of Dwarven technological know-how and Human ambition led to a rapid expansion of the Old Empire across the known world; even today, the Successor States live fully in its historic shadow.

Humans and dwarves can interbreed, and their children will typically take after both parents - although usually with a stronger resemblance for one or the other.

## Dwarves

| *Enhanced Abilities:* | *Strength, Constitution* |
|---|---|
| *Deficient Abilities:* | *Dexterity* |

Dwarves, like humans, are an original species of mortal from the Prime Material. Humans and dwarves share a distant ancestry that diverged approximately 400,000 years ago in the Southern continent, when the ancestors of the dwarves first spread north and east into the larger world. The species that became the dwarves was almost extinct when the Aether re-joined the Material 12,000 years ago, driven deep into the wilds by human competitors and the harsh climate of the last ice age.

The discovery of magic changed all that.

Dweomercraft allowed the dwarven race to survive, and thrive, in conditions where humans could not gain a foothold. Dwarves built vast empires underground, where they studied the arcane sciences and unlocked the secrets of the universe, largely sheltered from the predation of humans or the Fair Folk.

When they re-emerged onto the surface world over 8,000 years ago, much had changed - both within them, and within the world that greeted them.

Dwarven medicine and technology, combined with their natural hardiness, have resulted in a species with almost twice the longevity of humans - although a dwarf subjected to the same conditions that a “civilized” human normally lives in would likely only live a few meager decades longer than their neighbors.

Dwarven temperament seems biologically predisposed to a certain kind of obsessive focus more often than in other species, giving them a distinct advantage in learning new tasks related to knowledge and attention to detail, but at the cost of some amount of warmth and connection when relating to those who do not think as they do. As a result, most social skill checks made between a dwarf and a non-dwarf are harder.

**Small Size -** Dwarves are smaller than most mortals; as a result, it’s harder for them to wield Heavy weapons, and they must wield Medium weapons with both hands. It’s easier for them to attack medium or larger creatures, and it’s harder for medium or larger creatures to attack them.

## Fey Mortals

There are a myriad of diverse types of fey. Each type of fey is either the product of wild fey energies interacting directly with mortal bloodlines, or the descendant of interbreeding between those original fey mutations and ‘natural’ humans or dwarves.

**Iron Vulnerability -** Each of the fey is vulnerable to cold iron – unalloyed lodestone, telluric iron, or meteoric iron that has never been heated enough to glow by mortal means.

Damage to a fey creature from any cold iron weapon also stuns them, and causes all saving throws to be harder until it heals.

## Elves

| *Enhanced Abilities:* | *Dexterity, Wisdom* |
|---|---|
| *Deficient Abilities:* | *Constitution* |

Millennia of humans living side-by-side with the fey has resulted in a species that combines attributes of both. Elves are slender, delicate beings with large, brightly-colored eyes and large ears, often reminiscent of a deer’s or a rabbit’s. Elves find themselves most at home within the beauty of wild nature, although longer-established elven civilizations will likely have cultivated that nature into a vast patchwork of parks and groves which blend seamlessly into living and working spaces, merchant stalls, and military strongholds.

An elf’s apparent age is almost entirely dependent on the emotional hardships they have experienced. Elves have inherited some of the magical resilience of their fey ancestors, often living for hundreds of years in bodies that appear to be in their mid to late teens before they show any signs of age at all. Once they do begin to age, they typically settle into a middle-aged appearance that lasts for several more centuries, before becoming wizened and frail for the remainder of their lives. Unlike humans, who tend to fear aging, elves see middle-age and even the frailty of the old as badges of honor, signs of a rich life filled with meaning and stories.

Many elves can tap into the primal magic of their fey ancestry if they choose, but most forgo this easy road to power in favor of exploring their other faculties. Their culture teaches elves to take pride in the breadth of their experience and expertise, and magic often provides too many ‘easy outs’ to truly learn how to manage one’s problems the hard way. Thus, most elves avoid magic until they settle into middle age, however long that might take. An elven druid who took up training as a child is often seen as impulsive or undisciplined, although when they reach old age, their sheer depth of knowledge and power is appreciated by their community.

**Elven Senses -** elves can see in dim light as well as most other races can see in bright light, and they can hear and distinguish sounds more easily than other races. In general, any Alertness check related to raw sensory acuity is easier for an elf.

## Gnomes

| *Enhanced Abilities:* | *Dexterity, Charisma* |
|---|---|
| *Deficient Abilities:* | *Strength* |

Gnomes, like elves, originated from unions between mortals and the fey. While elves are tall, slender, feral beings, hobs most often resemble small dwarven children, with wide eyes and a ready smile. Their small stature has led to their colloquial nickname of halfling among humans. Dwarves, on the other hand, call their halfling cousins gnomes, and rely on them for most of their above-ground dealings. Much of gnome society is distinctly agrarian, favoring comfort and tradition over spectacle and innovation. Those gnomes that choose to adventure among the big folk are often seen by their kin as somewhat queer.

Elusive - Most gnomes retain some of their fey heritage, possessing a natural glamour that seeks to subtly camouflage them wherever they go. Their hair and skin color often shift visibly as they shift moods or enter a new environment, brightening to match their emotions or forming near-perfect camouflage patterns when they become frightened. As a result, gnomes find all Stealth checks easier while in any natural environment.

**Small Size -** Gnomes are smaller than most mortals; as a result, it’s harder for them to wield Heavy weapons, and they must wield Medium weapons with both hands. It’s easier for them to attack medium or larger creatures, and it’s harder for medium or larger creatures to attack them.

## Trolls

*Enhanced Ability: per Elemental Affinity*

Trolls are often formed through the union of a mortal and a nymph or djinn lover, although some are created through rarer natural fluctuations of primal energies. All trolls have an inborn talent for elemental magic that matches the element of their birthright.

Most trolls typically display striking colors and patterns which reveal their elemental heritage. Stone-trolls show deep browns and greys mottled with the metallic hues of precious metals and the slight glitter of gemstones, while water-trolls display deep blues, greens and purples with a slick and often translucent lustre, wood-trolls have deep browns and brilliant greens with flashes of every flower-color imaginable, air-trolls show pale blues, greys and yellows that shift and billow with their moods, and fire-trolls show dark blacks and ash-greys with flashes of brilliant red, orange and gold. An individual troll might be able to hide some of their elemental birthright with magic or makeup, but any action that taps into that birthright will put their true colors out on display.

**Elemental Affinity -** Each troll is aspected from birth to one of the five elements: stone, water, wood, air, or flame. They halve all damage that they receive from that element, and troll druids can perform magics that correspond to their birthright aspect without needing materia to focus the magic. Each troll has a single enhanced ability based on their subtype.

If your origin indicates a troll, roll a d20 to determine your elemental affinity:

## Beastfolk

Beastfolk are intelligent fey mortals that combine traits of humans or dwarves with the traits of natural beasts. They are a dream of Faerie, produced as echoes of the human fairy-tales of animals whose lives and politics are as complex as their own.

Most beastfolk are champions of their animal kin, tapping into their animal nature in combat. Pucca champions whose animal form matches their patron can battle-frenzy when in animal form, and any beastfolk champion whose animal form matches their patron gains experience faster, regardless of their prime requisite abilities. These creatures have given rise to all the legends of werewolves and other shapeshifting monsters.

Beastfolk druids and hunters are uncommon, but not unheard of. Fox pucca are the most notable exception; reyngarou almost invariably choose the path of the druid.

## Fauns

Fauns have the appearance of feral elves with pronounced animal features - their ears, eyes, and lower legs are almost entirely those of some species of wild animal; they typically possess a tail, a slight muzzle, and horns or antlers if appropriate to the animal species (and gender) that they resemble.

## Pucca

**Shapeshift -** All pucca have the ability to transform between a humanoid form and a single kind of natural beast. Unlike a druid’s ‘Wild Shape’ magic, a pucca’s magic – and their animal form – is innate to their fey nature, and their animal form is considered their ‘natural’ form. In their humanoid form, a pucca often resembles an elf or human with some faun-like features. Fox and owl pucca have enhanced Dexterity and no impaired abilities, while other pucca species have enhanced Dexterity and Strength, and impaired Intelligence.

Pucca feel just as at home in either of their forms, but often have difficulty maintaining their human form when injured or startled.

Most pucca have a ‘tell’ — such as a long, luxurious fox’s tail, or a cat’s reflective eyes — that can only be partially hidden under clothing or illusion magic, but that discerning mortals might still spot. Noticing a pucca’s tell if you have never seen it in animal form is a hard Alertness check, while noticing its tell if you have seen its animal form (even if you didn’t know it was a pucca) is an easy Alertness check. The referee makes this check for you in secret each time you cross paths. Pucca shapeshifting follows all the rules for a druid’s wild shape feat, but it is their animal form rather than their human form that is considered natural. A pucca in human form does not need to maintain focus on their form, but must still make a saving throw vs. Doom when injured to avoid reverting back into their animal form. This saving throw is hard if the damage is from a cold iron weapon or projectile.

## Centaurs

Centaurs are fey chimera, possessing the upper body of a faun and the lower body of some form of natural beast. Horse, deer, seal, dolphin, snake, spider, and cheetah centaurs are all known to exist in populations large enough to maintain a tribal presence in the wilderness, while more unique specimens occasionally prowl the deep forests.

The most common form of centaur encountered by humans are hippocentaurs, who possess a lower body that resembles a small pony. Wild hippocentaur tribes typically live in close proximity to elven and human barbarians, and share many of their habits and beliefs. Cervitaurs, or deer centaur, are more elusive creatures, keeping mostly to their own kind in small tribal herds.

In coastal regions, dolphin and seal centaurs called merfolk are most commonly encountered. While merfolk cannot breathe underwater by nature, they can hold their breath for nearly an hour at a time, and often craft intricate magic items allowing themselves to transform into two-legged forms and walk on dry land.

Other centaur types are more often seen as a threat than an ally, although there are always exceptions. Tribes of serpentfolk dot the wild jungles of the world, still offering mortal sacrifices to the Fair Folk. Ettercap are typically proud, solitary creatures, but will often enslave tribes of elves or goblins in the deep underground caverns of Faerie. Leotaurs are most often found as solitary predators, waylaying the unwary and devouring them.

**Enhanced Movement -** The animal lower half of a centaur gives them the movement rates of the animal species they most resemble, as well as determining their enhanced and deficient abilities. Each particular body plan will also make specific task checks and saving throws hard or easy depending on the environment - for example, a quadruped will find it easier to save vs. Hold in many circumstances, but will find Agility checks to climb a ladder or a rope harder.

If your origin indicates a centaur, roll a d20 to determine the shape of your lower half:

**1-6. Hippocentaurs** (+Str, +Dex, -Int) possess a lower body that resembles a small pony.

They may dash up to 30 paces, or up to 20 paces while encumbered.

**7-10. Cervitaurs** (+Str, +Dex, -Int) possess a lower body that resembles a deer or small elk. Lighter and more nimble than other centaur, they may dash 40 paces when unencumbered, and find it easy to navigate difficult terrain. **11-14. Leotaurs** (+Str, +Dex, -Int) possess a lower body that resembles a cheetah. Like cervitaur, they may dash up to 30 paces when unencumbered, and find it easy to navigate difficult terrain.

**15-16. Merfolk** (+Con, +Wis, -Dex) possess a lower body that resembles a dolphin or porpoise, but with elaborate fins and bright colors. Merfolk are hobbled on land, but may dash 30 paces underwater.

**17-18. Serpentfolk** (+Str, +Dex, -Int) possess a lower body that resembles a massive snake – often 15 ft long or longer. Serpentfolk are not hobbled while prone, and may even dash 20 paces while prone. They may make a grapple attack with their tail with a reach of 2 paces. Serpentfolk find it harder than other creatures to save vs. Hold to prevent being moved, unless they can grab or wrap around something while making their saving throw.

**19. Ettercaps** (+Con, +Dex, -Int) possess a lower body that resembles a gigantic spider. An ettercap may climb reliably without needing an Agility check, and may dash 20 paces while climbing. They find it easy to avoid being knocked over or moved.

**20. Garda** (+Dex, +Int, -Con) are unusual in that it is their lower body that is humanoid, while their head and shoulders are those of a large owl, magpie, crow, or raven. Garda are small creatures with hollow bones and enormous wings, allowing them to fly 20 paces as a dash action when unencumbered.

As **small** creatures, it’s harder for garda to wield Heavy weapons, and they must wield Medium weapons with both hands. It’s easier for them to attack medium or larger creatures, and it’s harder for medium or larger creatures to attack them.

## Orcs

In addition to natural animals and fey beasts, there are plenty of intelligent enemies that threaten civilization. The most common threats that an adventuring party is likely to face are called orcs.

Orcs are barbarian cannibal-warriors that follow Orcus, a dark god of terror and blood. While some human and fey barbarian tribes can be orcs, by far the most common orcs are goblins and lizardfolk.

Orcs are built as fey/barbarian non-player characters, with character classes and levels as normal. Goblin and lizardfolk orcs can come in small, medium, large, or huge size, with the largest typically also having the most hit dice. Small goblin-orcs are called redcaps, while medium goblin-orcs are called hobgoblins, large goblin-orcs are called bugbears, and huge goblin-orcs are called ogres. Small lizardfolk-orcs are called kobolds, medium lizardfolk-orcs are called troglodytes, and large lizardfolk-orcs are called dragonborn. While there are technically no huge lizardfolkorcs, lizardfolk tribes are often led by huge primordial dragons.

The statistics for small, medium, and large goblin and lizardfolk orcs are presented here for player and referee alike to familiarize themselves with, while the statistics for huge creatures are presented later in this book.

## Goblin Orcs

Goblin physiology seems particularly suited to orc marauding, as the consumption of the flesh of intelligent creatures allows them to increase in size almost without limit. As they grow, a goblin orc's hunger for manflesh increases faster than their height and weight, leaving bugbears and ogres thinking of little but their next succulent meal. Orc tribal hierarchy is almost entirely based on size, with the smaller redcaps living in constant fear of being caught and eaten by their ravenous larger cousins.

## Beastman Orcs

Many beastman tribes form marauding bands of orc champions and hunters, tapping into their animal natures to sow terror as they raid civilization. To create an orc beastman, choose a faun, pucca, or centaur subtype and set each of its enhanced abilities to +1 and its deficient ability (if any) to -1.

Orcish centaur warbands will typically be comprised exclusively of centaurs, while pucca often work together as a single pack, which might also include members of their associated animal species. Orc faun tribes, called "gnoles" by peasant villagers, might even organize into entire clans, conquering large swaths of territory and enslaving mortals for labor and food.

A pack will typically be led by a champion, who will have the highest level among the marauders. For each champion in the pack, there will typically be 2-3 hunters, serving as archers and skirmishers to support the champions' direct assault. Some warbands will also include a druid for magical support. Some beastmen types, such as ettercaps, prefer to be solitary hunters or a single mated pair. The only known large tribe of orcish ettercaps is the vast subterranean empire of Svartalfheim, where eerily pale dwarf, elf, and ettercap cannibals have managed to form a brutal but effective civilization within the Hyperborean ruins of an ancient Atlantean city-state that was buried and forgotten beneath the northernmost wastes of the Danemarch by the Great Flood.
