## Beasts

Animals typically have an armor rating between 1 and 2, representing a leathery or thick hide. A few animals or fey beasts might have even thicker hides. A beast’s armor never encumbers it. Many animals have unusual movement modes. A fast animal can dash 30 paces per round when unencumbered, while a very fast animal can dash 40 paces per round. Animals with a swim or climb speed never have to roll to swim or climb, and can dash while using that movement. A slow animal, on the other hand, is considered hobbled when moving along the ground; most slow creatures have some alternate movement mode (such as flight or swimming) that is their natural movement.

Many animals also have various senses that are either poor or superior compared to a mortal. Alertness checks made with poor senses are harder, while Alertness checks made with superior senses are easier. A few animals have senses that are completely unique, such as a bat’s sonar. Finally, some animals have special feats that they can perform during a combat encounter. An animal can perform such a feat a number of times per encounter equal to its level.

At the referee’s discretion, a particular animal or beast might have better or worse abilities than a typical specimen — the referee might roll a d6 to determine which ability is affected, then roll a second d6 and adjust that ability by +1 on a ‘6’, or by -1 on a ‘1’.

## Animal Intelligence

Most animals are of bestial intelligence, with sufficient cunning to problem-solve but not to understand complex concepts. Mechanically, this is represented by an Intelligence of -4; creatures with higher Intelligence might be capable of some amount of complex reasoning and training, sufficient to teach them skills outside their normal repertoire. Dolphins, crows, and elephants in particular display signs of intelligence that rivals our own (-2 to +0 Intelligence); the kinds of skills that such creatures can be taught are left up to the referee to decide. Some creatures do not have an Intelligence ability at all; such a monster is essentially mindless, only responding to basic stimulus and following a few rote instincts.

## Animal Companions

Druids and hunters with expertise in Survival can gather a menagerie of animal companions about them; these creatures follow the druid like a retinue but do not count against their Bearing limit. A druid or hunter may have a total number of creatures in their menagerie based on Survival rather than Bearing, and those creatures’ total levels cannot exceed twice the character’s level. Keep in mind that feeding a Large creature will represent a substantial investment in resources, and such creatures cannot typically follow a party of adventurers underground into tight caverns and darkly-lit spaces.

Other characters with Intuition of 5+ or better may have a single trained animal companion - either a 2 hd large dog or a 3 hd warhorse.

Commanding a trained animal companion in battle requires an Intuition check each time a command is given, to ensure that the creature understands and obeys the order. This check will be easier or harder depending on the intelligence, stubbornness, and training of the animal, as well as its current attitude and relationship to its handler.

## Natural Animals

Natural animals are creatures from the Material Plane, with no particular connection to Faerie or Shadow. The vast majority of creatures that scurry about the Material are native to this realm, and are simply trying to survive as best they can within their territory. Most such creatures only fight if cornered, or (if carnivorous) when more desperate for a meal than they are wary.
