## Divine Miracles

Each priest has a domain of faith, which is a particular aspect of the divine that they dedicate themselves to. A priest can perform divine miracles from that domain simply by channeling that innate faith, while other miracles must be prepared. Preparing a divine miracle requires time spent in deep prayer and meditation while writing the request for that miracle out on a prayer strip, which will retain potency until the next sunrise or sunset.

### Prayer Strips

A prayer strip is a small piece of pure white paper or parchment, approximately the size of an open palm, upon which a short prayer has been written in expensive ink with fine calligraphy. This prayer strip is the physical token used to perform a prepared miracle, and must be kept clean and unspoiled in order to be used for divine purposes.

If something causes you to lose a charged prayer strip before you can use it, you don’t necessarily lose the magic that you invested - the Saints are not quite so fickle. As an exploration action after losing your prayer strips, you can use any available paper and writing utensils to attempt to recreate suitable tokens. So long as you show proper reverence, the original prayer strips will be consumed - wherever they are - and whatever makeshift scraps you have produced will magically transform into exact copies of the originals. Once you have prepared a miracle into a prayer strip, you must use the strip as you perform the miracle; the sacrifices performed when you prepared it fuel the miracle. If a prayer strip miracle has a range of touch or melee, you must physically touch the prayer strip to the target, at which point it magically adheres and begins to burn away in a nimbus of holy light. If the miracle is ranged, you hold the strip up towards the target, say the first verse of the prayer aloud, and fling it at the target, at which point it magically flies from your hand to attach to the target, and then begins to glow brightly and burn away as it activates.

#### Prayer Scrolls

A prayer strip lasts until the next sunrise or sunset, after which it loses its divine power and must be discarded or recharged with additional prayer and sacrifice. You may choose to make sacrifices and burnt offerings worth at least 100 gp per circle while preparing a prayer strip; if you do, that prayer strip becomes supernaturally resilient, and will retain its potency indefinitely until used. Such a prayer strip can be used by any priest to perform the prepared miracle.

A non-priest may attempt to perform a miracle from a prayer scroll, but doing so takes additional time and effort. First, the nonpriest must save vs. Curse, which is easy for a first-circle prayer and hard for a third-circle prayer. If they succeed, they perform the prayer properly and the miracle occurs; if they fail, the prayer scroll is consumed but a divine mishap occurs - possibly targeting the prayer at random, reversing the effect of the miracle, or some other effect chosen by the referee.

### Divine Rituals

A priest’s miraculous power is significantly enhanced by proper adherence to ritual.

Divine rituals led by a priest of the appropriate domain allow multiple priests working in tandem, or even sufficiently devout non-priests, to each contribute to the ritual’s success.

Each earnest participant in a divine ritual saves vs. Curse, even if they are not a priest. Each participant that succeeds contributes to the ritual’s intended effect.

A divine ritual lasts for as long as all of its contributing participants continue to maintain focus on it. If any participant who positively contributed to the ritual breaks focus, the ritual and its associated miraculous effect immediately ends.

Any priest may perform a ritual blessing or curse from their faith’s domain during the hour before dawn or sunset to cause that ritual’s effects to last until the next sunrise or sunset, without anyone needing to maintain focus on it. Others may participate in the ritual as well, increasing its power and hardening it against counter-magics.

#### Sanctified Oaths

Many divine blessings have multiple different ritual forms, adjusting the nature of the blessing to match a special occasion. For example, various wedding ceremonies weave a Bless or Sanctuary effect into the officiating priest’s sanctification of the couple’s vows. Any such blessing is known as a sanctified oath.

All recipients of a sanctified oath must directly participate in the ritual sanctifying their oath, and must succeed in their save vs. Curse to contribute to the ritual. An oath must be sworn in earnest by all participants, for the oath’s own sake, or it will not be sanctified by the Divine.

A properly sanctified blessing sustains itself indefinitely without focus so long as all ritual participants continue to uphold their end of their sworn oaths. If any member breaks their oath, however, all members who participated in the ritual lose their blessing, and the participant who directly violated their oath suffers a curse proportional in strength to their original blessing. The details of this curse vary from oathbreaker to oathbreaker, but will always be ironically appropriate to the nature of their vow and violation.

Sanctified oaths are visible via identify magic and similar effects. An oath blessing appears as strands of light woven into the oathkeeper’s aura, while an oathbreaker’s curse appears as dark chains binding them and cutting painfully into their aura.

A priest can release another creature from a sanctified vow through a ritual of atonement, which must be performed at the same circle as the ritual that originally sanctified the vow. The creature being released must participate successfully in the ritual to be released from their vow, and must make appropriate sacrifices to demonstrate their sincerity. If successful, the oath (and its attendant blessing or curse) is nullified.

A creature may not be bound by more circles worth of sanctified oaths than their level or hit dice. If they attempt to swear an oath beyond this limit, they simply fail until they can be released from one of their previous oaths.

### Good and Evil, Right and Wrong

The chief duty of a priest is offering moral guidance to others. As such, a priest must be an exemplar of the four cardinal virtues of justice, mercy, honor, and prudence.

### Justice

A priest should encourage their companions to be honest and forthright with all their dealings, not to cheat or steal, and to obey the local authorities insofar as those authorities are themselves known to be virtuous.

Conflicts between different authorities should be handled through the proper authorities whenever possible, unless doing so would create a larger miscarriage of justice.

#### Mercy

The Prophet was sent into the world to redeem it, and turn all minds to obedience to the Divine Principle. This offer of redemption is the most sacred tenet of the Faith, and inflicting cruelty in the name of "justice" is one of its gravest sins. Inasmuch as surrender and redemption can be offered to one's enemies, the priest and their allies must seek to do so.

#### Honor

A priest and their companions should face danger with courage, and should never place another mortal at risk when they could reasonably take that risk on themselves.

Above all, a priest and their companions should never abuse their power and authority to gain unfair advantage over others, or use trickery and guile when a fair fight is possible.

#### Prudence

While justice, mercy, and honor are all important, prudence is the virtue that tempers them and seeks balance between them.

Justice that turns to cruelty or madness is no justice at all, and the genuine needs of the people and the realm must temper the principles that uphold them.

Creatures that betray a good-faith offer of surrender and redemption might forfeit their right to mercy, if the benefit of the doubt would reasonably place innocents at risk.

Those that show themselves without honor similarly forfeit their right to be fought with honor, and may be dispatched expediently.

### Church Dogma

Church dogma holds that the Divine Principle lays out an ideal course for man to follow, but that greed and vengeance and fear tempt men to disobey; that the Prophet, by fully accepting their role as the Lamb of Sacrifice, was made one with the Will of the Divine, thus planting the seed for the Kingdom of Heaven which cleanses the sins of the world, and that obedience to the Divine Principle, as demonstrated by the Lamb of Sacrifice, allows men to participate in the communion of the Divine Spirit, by which the Kingdom of Heaven is built and eternally improved.

#### Sects and Heresies

Various groups show themselves perfectly capable of channeling Divine magic, whether or not they agree with the Church's dogma.

Some of these sects are considered to be in faithful communion with the Church, while others are considered heretical. Nevertheless, a priest of the Divine must never sanction the following acts:

- Blood sacrifice of an intelligent mortal

- Cruelty or torture towards any being

capable of feeling

- Slavery or imprisonment lasting more

- Murder, including deliberately

depriving a mortal of their necessities

for personal gain

#### Sins and Atonement

A priest that fails to live up to the ideals of the four cardinal virtues, or who commits even graver sins against the Divine Principle, may find themselves unable to perform miracles until they atone for their sins. The process of atonement depends upon the sins committed, but invariably involves confessing those sins to a higher-ranking priest, then making an honest effort to right whatever wrongs can be undone, and finally a heartfelt commitment to avoid similar transgressions in the future. A priest who attempts to atone under false premises will find themselves completely cut off from their granted powers until they align their beliefs and behavior with the Divine.

### Ghosts and The Underworld

Another duty of a priest is maintaining the boundary between the living material world and the underworld of the dead. Angry ghosts require appeasement and guidance to give up their mortal passions and move on to the afterlife; convincing them to do so falls upon the clergy. Failure to fulfill that duty in a timely fashion can lead to an unappeased ghost becoming one of the undead; over an extended time, accumulations of necromantic energy can even lead to the Shadow of the underworld bleeding over into the material.

Like Faerie, the Shadow is a liminal realm between the Underworld proper and the Material Plane, whose distance waxes and wanes with the phases of the moon. During the three days around the full moon, travel into and out of the Shadow is harder, while during the three days around the new moon, travel between Shadow and the Material is easier. Both of these times create a lot of work for priests, as the new moon permits ghosts and the undead to manifest, while the full moon tends to trap them on whichever side of the veil they find themselves on.

Whenever a living being dies, its death will emit a burst of Aetheric energy which ‘imprints’ upon the Shadow, creating a pale copy of its consciousness called a ‘ghost’. This imprint might be anything from a vague impression of the being it once was, to a fully formed visage and personality, capable of carrying out the wishes of the living being it was copied from. Stronger ghosts contain most of the memories and personalities of the creature that they echo; intelligent beings, such as sapient mortals, produce a ghost that is nearly identical to its mortal form. Even so, most ghosts arrive in the Shadow disoriented and confused. If the body that spawned a ghost is restored to life, that ghost will be pulled irresistibly to it, restoring its mind and memories and adding the ghost’s memories to its own.

Most ghosts immediately leave the Shadow realm for the Everafter, the collection of afterlives that serve as the “eternal reward” for the once-living. The church maintains that those in faithful communion will reach heaven, an Everafter where they are united with the Divine Principle and become saints.

#### Saints

Ghosts that have been properly appeased and sanctified move on to the afterlife, where they are judged for their deeds in life and united with the divine. Most religion in the world treats sacred ancestors, called saints, as intermediaries between the Divine and living mortals. It is part of a priest’s duties to serve as an ambassador and messenger between the saints and the living, reminding both of their duties to the other.

Other Saints, called Numina, were never living mortals at all; these Saints are Fair Folk or even Baalim that were offered redemption and communion into the Faith, and accepted.

These beings are considered just as saintly as the martyred dead; most such Saints concern themselves with the protection of a particular area within the Material world, and its corresponding echoes within Faerie.

One of the more common responses to a priest’s prayers for divine intervention is the manifestation of a Saint. A summoned saint will manifest adjacent to the priest as they pray, and will act to aid them directly, with the referee controlling the saint's actions. A Saint manifests as a 5 hd medium creature with 5 faith; it returns to the Everafter once either its hit points or its faith are depleted, or at the next sunrise or sunset.

#### The Unholy

Undead and infernal creatures together make up the unholy - creatures so inimical to the divine order that they must be defied and destroyed on sight. Particularly savage fair folk might also qualify as unholy, depending on their natures.

Mortal beings are never unholy, unless they are currently being possessed or directly controlled by an unholy supernatural being.

Unholiness is not mere wickedness, but is an intrinsic nature that is deeply inimical to both the divine and primal orders. Countering the efforts of the unholy is the primary reason that the Divine imbues priests and sends them forth into the world.
