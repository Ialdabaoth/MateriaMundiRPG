## Magic

Some classes are "magic-users", allowing them to cast spells, miracles, or incantations depending on their class and origin. Each type of magic-user is a unique class, with its own list of magics that it can perform, and each of these magics describes how it is cast. In an encounter, most magics require your action to perform, but a few can be performed in reaction to other events or actions. Some effects require that you touch a target, while others have a range listed in fivefoot paces.

Some monsters and fey beasts also have access to magic, either as part of their innate biology or as trained class abilities. The rules for monsters performing magic are usually the same as the rules for player characters.

## Preparation

Most magic requires preparation to perform at a speed faster than a ritual; each type of magic-user prepares their magics in different ways, but they share certain core features.

The most effective time to prepare spells or attunements is within an hour of sunrise or sunset. The liminality of dawn and dusk thins the Veil which separates Faerie and Shadow from the Material, allowing Aetheric energies an easier conduit into a magic-user’s tools. Some minor amount of re-preparation is possible outside of this time, depending on one’s character class, but a magic-user’s basic daily preparation allotment must be attended to during this time. Daily preparation requires focus, concentration, and a clear head, so a magic-user typically begins their preparation immediately upon waking up – and schedules their sleep and waking accordingly.

### Preparation During Exploration

Some classes can attempt to prepare or regain some of their magics during a 10 minute turn. You will need to either roll a d6 task check or a d20 saving throw to do so, depending on your character class. If this roll fails, the magic is not yet prepared; you can choose to either immediately make another attempt to prepare the same magic during the next exploration turn, or abandon the attempt and waste the resources required to prepare the magic. If you are interrupted, you automatically fail and waste resources.

## Casting

### Casting in Combat

Casting in a combat encounter is slower and riskier than other combat actions. You must declare to the referee what effect you intend to cast. If another creature hits you with an attack after you have declared your effect but before you can perform your action, you must save vs. Doom or the magic is disrupted.

#### Fast Magics

Some magics are fast to perform. A fast magic can be performed in reaction to another action in combat, even if the caster hadn't originally declared that they were casting magic this round. They simply switch their action to casting the magic, then do so.

#### Touching a Target

Some spellcasting requires that you touch a target to affect it. Touching an unwilling creature requires a melee attack throw.

### Focus

Some magics require your active attention to maintain; while you are focusing on a magical effect, you cannot perform any other magic, but can perform weapon attacks and any other task that does not also require focus to maintain.  If you suffer any hit point damage while you are casting or focusing on a magical effect other than a battle-frenzy, you must save vs. Doom or your focus is broken and the effect ends.

Certain magical items and allies, such as dragon pearls or wisp companions, can maintain focus on behalf of a magic-user; if you are injured while an item or wisp is maintaining focus for you, you still need to make the saving throw to avoid the effect ending, although you are not technically focusing on it yourself.

## Rituals

Most magics can also be performed as rituals; ritual magic takes too long to perform during an encounter, but may be performed without the need for preparation if you have the time and access to the correct prayer or spellbook for the duration of the ritual. (Primal rituals do not require a book.)

### Performing a Ritual

Most magics may be performed either in an encounter or as a ritual, while certain other magics may only be performed as rituals. A ritual might take several exploration turns to perform, depending on the ritual’s circle and your character’s class and level – during which time your character can perform no other actions.

Ritual magic takes effect at the beginning of the next exploration turn after its casting is complete; once the ritual is finished, the resulting effect is identical to a prepared casting of that magic unless that magic specifies a difference between prepared and ritual casting effects.

A ritual that is interrupted or fails mid-casting might have no effect, or it might cause some kind of wildly unpredictable magical mishap, at the discretion of the referee. Generally, more powerful magics are more likely to go awry; the referee should allow the caster to make a saving throw to channel the built-up energies of an interrupted or aborted ritual, with a difficulty based on the ritual's circle.

### Ritual Materials

All ritual magic requires the same arcana, materia, and soma as a prepared casting of the same magic; any resources that would be consumed when casting a prepared magic are also consumed when casting that ritual.

### Sworn Oaths

Any divine ritual blessing that requires focus to maintain can instead be maintained by the recipient of that blessing, if they swear an appropriate oath during the ritual. Violating such an oath will reverse the blessing, turning it into a curse until they atone (if atonement is even possible).

### Group Rituals

Multiple magic-users of the same type may come together to participate in a group ritual. Wizards may combine their efforts to cast an arcane spell as a group ritual, druids can perform group glamour rituals, and priests can even lead a group divine ritual that includes non-priests, so long as each member of the ritual is currently in faithful communion with the church and has not committed any unatoned cardinal sins.

To perform a group ritual, one magic-user must be designated as the leader of the ritual. Then, each member of the ritual must make a task check or saving throw to align their energies with those of the group. Wizards and dweomersmiths roll Science, druids roll Survival, and priests roll Bearing. If the ritual’s leader fails, then the entire ritual fails regardless of how many other participants succeeded. Each participant in the ritual who contributes to its power must maintain focus throughout the ritual; if any of them lose focus, the ritual fails.

If the ritual has a voluntary target, they are considered a participant in the ritual even if they are not the correct kind of magic-user; they must also succeed in the appropriate task check or saving throw, or the entire ritual fails. A voluntary target’s roll is easier than the other participants’, since they are merely receiving the ritual’s energies rather than channeling and shaping them.

Rituals that affect an area can have their area of effect amplified by the number of participants, increasing the radius of effect by 50% at 2 participants, by 75% at 3, or doubling it at 4. Each additional participant increases it by 10% up to 8 (two and a half times the base effect radius); each participant past 8 increases the area of effect by another 5% of its base radius.

Other rituals can have their effects amplified in other ways – a ritual intended to do damage might add an additional die of damage per extra participant, a ritual effect with a duration might have its duration extended, or an effect that requires a saving throw to resist might become harder to resist if a large enough group contributes to it.
