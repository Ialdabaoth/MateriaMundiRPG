## Primal Magics

Druid glamours and talismans are much simpler than Wizard spells, and so do not require a spellbook to prepare. A druid never needs to prepare glamours; these magics are performed through raw force of will, while elemental talismans require focusing through elemental materia, somewhat like light focuses through a lens.

Druids recognize five kinds of elemental materia - Stone, Water, Wood, Air, and Flame. Psychic and aetheric materia can be used by druids to craft primal magic items, or to create non-elemental talismans.

All glamours and talismans are fey magic. As such, you cannot cast them if you are wearing iron or steel armor, wielding an iron or steel weapon or shield, or carrying any other iron or steel objects.

Primal glamour is attuned to the ebb and flow of energies within Faerie, making it easier to replenish from within faerie, and harder to replenish within the Shadow. While on the Material Plane, preparing talismans and regaining glamour on the night of the full moon is easy; recharging on the night of the new moon is hard.

Primal glamour is also dependent on the attitudes and dispositions of the local fey spirits. Each 3-mile hex will typically have its own disposition, although those near a sacred grove will likely be strongly influenced by the disposition of that grove. When entering a new area, the referee can roll a d6 – on a ‘1’, the local fey are unfavorably disposed to the druid; on a ‘6’, the local fey are favorably disposed. Their disposition makes preparing talismans and regaining glamour easier or harder for any druids in the party. Since the whims of the fey shift from day to day, the referee should make this roll fresh each morning and evening.

Adjusting the disposition of the local fey typically involves convoluted arguments and quests that obey the twisted logic of Faerie. A particularly important favor done for the local fey might earn their favor for up to a year and a day, but typically any favor earned will only last until the next sunrise or sunset.

### Learning Glamours

Druids learn new glamours by practicing them under the tutelage of a druid, wisp, fey beast, or fair folk that knows the glamour.

Each day spent attempting to learn the glamour allows the student to make an Intuition check; once they have accumulated a number of successes equal to the glamour's circle, they may add it to their list of known glamours. If a druid's total known glamours exceeds the maximum they can know at their level, they must "forget" one glamour as they learn the new one. Forgotten glamours may be re-learned at a later time.

A good teacher can make this process easier, while a bad teacher can make it harder. If the teacher expressly guides the student during their study, they also make an Intuition check; success makes the student's check easier, but failure makes the student's check harder.

### Primal Talismans

Druids must shape and focus their glamours in a manner similar to (but simpler than) an arcane spell. Glamours are prepared onto small objects called talismans, which contain enough materia of the appropriate element to power the magic contained within them.

To prepare a primal talisman, a druid begins with a charge of materia or a dragon pearl, and paints or carves runes and other shapes and patterns into it. This process takes a number of Survival checks equal to its circle, with each roll taking one exploration turn.

The talisman remains prepared until the next sunrise or sunset. Once the talisman is used to cast the glamour it is consumed by faerie fire, and burns harmlessly to ash in the caster's hand. More permanent talismans can be fashioned by using a dragon pearl of the appropriate element and rarity or a willing wisp of the appropriate element, thus crafting the talisman into a proper magic item.

Druids who are not of a sufficient level to craft a talisman of a given circle may attempt to cast a glamour from such a talisman by first spending their action to save vs. curse, with a difficulty based on the talisman’s circle. If they succeed, they cast the glamour as their next round's action.

### Ritual Glamours

Druids can perform any primal effect as a ritual glamour with an appropriate materia charge. A druid that performs any ritual glamour requiring focus is assumed to continue focusing on it to maintain its effect; while focusing, that character cannot be on watch or perform other exploration actions.

Druids of any level can participate in any primal rituals led by a druid that knows the glamour, following all the normal rules for group rituals. This is usually used to "anchor" the effects of various rituals permanently, such as sacred grove, mirage, or geas. Making such effects permanent usually also requires the sacrifice of a dragon pearl of the appropriate elemental aspect and rarity, both to anchor the glamour into the material plane and to provide it with a steady supply of elemental energies.

Some ritual glamours can also be anchored by repeatedly performing the ritual each sunrise and sunset for a lunar month, effectively infusing the local environment with enough ambient elemental energies to act as a sort of diffused dragon pearl. Continuing to perform a ritual after it has been permanently woven into the material plane provides a kind of "aetheric armor" which makes dispelling the magic harder; certain sacred groves, which have been in constant use for thousands of years, cannot be dispelled short of direct divine intervention.

In civilization, primal magic is often used by druids and priests of the nature domain to weave the effects of the bountiful harvest glamour into local farmland, dramatically increasing crop yields. This requires a side-byside application of magic and scientific knowledge, to prevent the overabundance from permanently depleting the soil fertility.

### Wild Magic

In a pinch, a druid can shatter a talisman to release the energy within as a wild elemental attack, either as a ranged attack with a range of five paces or a three pace long cone of energy. The attack or blast inflicts one die of elemental damage per circle of the glamour enchanted into the talisman.

**Stone** manifests shards of flint or obsidian that deal piercing, blunt, or sundering damage as if from a weapon. Fourth-circle stone talisma may instead deal petrification damage by transforming flesh and wood into sandstone, metal into rust, and liquids into mud.

**Water** deals cold damage, encasing the target in ice and slowing their movement, or acid damage, slowly dissolving the target. A druid may also choose to cast any water elemental glamour non-lethally by spraying the target with seawater rather than acid, dealing blunt damage instead.

**Air** deals lightning damage, stunning and cooking creatures from within. Any saves vs. blast against a lightning strike are harder for a creature wearing metal armor or wielding a medium or heavy metal weapon. Air can also inflict thunder damage, blasting the target with hurricane winds that might deafen them, push them back or knock them prone.

**Flame** deals fire damage, roasting the target with intense heat that might even set them alight. It can also deal radiant damage, flashburning the target with intense light that might also blind or stun.

**Wood** deals poison damage to living creatures, exposing them to deadly toxins that continue to work their way through their bloodstreams, or deal necrotic damage to them by invigorating all the tiny microbes and parasites within a creature to begin rotting them from within.

**Dream** directly attacks the target's mind with psychic damage. Dream materia is used to prepare Dream and Song glamours.

Finally, **Aether** deals aetheric damage, channeling eldritch energies directly from Faerie into the core of the target's being.

Aetheric materia is used to prepare Wyrd and Mist glamours.
