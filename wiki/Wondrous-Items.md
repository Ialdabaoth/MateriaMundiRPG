## Wondrous Items

Wondrous Items are magic items which are neither worn, nor wielded in the hand. Some wondrous items are autonomous constructs, operating as creatures on the battlefield; others are devices that must be interacted with to perform their functions.

Many wondrous items must be held in the hand to use or activate; while using such a wondrous item, a creature may not hold any weapon or other item in the same hand.

### Dragon Pearl

*Any Rarity (Arcane or Primal)*  

*Ingredients: Elemental materia*

A dragon pearl is a sphere of flawless elemental or aetheric materia, called such because the first dragon pearls were taken from the skulls of recently slain dragons. Common dragon pearls are approximately pea-sized gemstones, while uncommon gemstones are the size of a grape or cherry, rare gemstones are usually the size of a plum, and legendary gemstones can be the size of an apple or larger. An unusually flawless dragon pearl might be smaller than normal given its power and rarity, while one with inclusions or other flaws will likely be larger. Dragon pearls can be harvested from magical creatures that produce them naturally, or they can be grown artificially from harvested raw materia by any sufficiently skilled alchemist.

A dragon pearl acts as an additional spell, dweomer, or talisman preparation for a magic-user that is not destroyed when used.

Any elemental spell or talisman of the same type as the pearl can be prepared into it, with a circle up to the pearl's rarity; legendary dragon pearls can power effects of any circle. Once the spell or talisman is cast, the pearl cannot be used again until it has recharged. This takes ten minutes if the effect is two circles less than the pearl’s rarity level, one hour if the effect’s circle is one less than the pearl’s rarity level, or until the next sunrise or sunset if the effect’s circle is equal to the pearl’s rarity level. A first-circle effect can be cast once per combat round from a legendary dragon pearl.

The pearl sustains any spell or talisman cast through it directly without requiring the caster’s focus, although if it leaves their hand the spell’s focus is considered automatically broken. The spell must be maintained by the caster’s focus for a number of rounds equal to the spell’s circle, before the pearl can sustain it. Once the pearl begins sustaining the spell, it can maintain the spell without further contact or interaction from the caster. A pearl can sustain a spell at its own rarity level until the next sunrise or sunset, or below its own rarity indefinitely.

### Aether Pearl

*Any Rarity (Arcane or Primal)*  

*Ingredients: Aetheric materia*

An aether pearl is identical to a dragon pearl, except that it empowers aetheric arcane spells instead of elemental magics.

### Dream Pearl

*Any Rarity (Arcane or Primal)*  

*Ingredients: Illusion materia*

A dream pearl is identical to a dragon or aether pearl, except that it empowers psychic arcane spells and fey glamours, instead of elemental magics.

#### Crafting With Dragon Pearls

Dragon pearls are also used as permanent power sources for many magic items. A common item requires a common dragon pearl, while an uncommon item needs an uncommon dragon pearl, and so on.

#### Crafting Without Dragon Pearls

Magic items can be crafted without a dragon pearl, if they incorporate sufficient materia to power their magic. This results in a magic item with a set number of "charges", which are depleted as the item is used. Primal items of any rarity may also be created using a wisp instead of a dragon pearl, so long as the wisp consents to the process. This always results in an intelligent item with a will of its own, which is treated as a retainer (tracking loyalty and counting against the user's retinue limit).

### Clockwork Servitor

*Legendary (Arcane)*  

*Ingredients: Aether Pearl, Air Pearl, orichalc,*

*feysilver, brass, steel*

A clockwork servitor is a golem automaton from the height of the Old Empire. Servitors were often built to resemble small, medium, or large mortals, beasts, or monsters and could be directed to perform various simple tasks autonomously. A servitor has the the following base abilities, with its physical abilities adjusted based on the abilities of the creature that it was built to resemble:

Strength +2 (adjusted)

| | |
|---|---|
| Constitution | +3 (adjusted) |

Dexterity +0 (adjusted)

Intelligence -2

| | |
|---|---|
| Wisdom | -1 |

Charisma  -3

Most servitors in the Old Empire were built with modular “cognition engines”, which could be removed and replaced to give the servitor various arrays of expertise. Each cognition engine resembles a translucent crystal cylinder etched with intricate geometric patterns of dark gold and silver, with a gold ‘plug’ on one end covered in long silver tabs. Cognition engines for bipedal servitors typically hold a Guardian program (3hd Warrior), a Scout program (3hd Scout), or an Engineer program (3hd Wizard), while cognition engines for quadrupedal servitors generally only hold enough programming for locomotion, navigation, self-defense, and basic verbal command recognition (treat as a 2hd Champion).

### Enchanted Crockery

*Common (Primal)*  

*Ingredients: Wood Pearl, Water Pearl, orichalc,*

*copper, gold, jade, bamboo*

Enchanted crockery appears to be a set of gold-inlaid copper cookware, kitchen knives, a small cutting board, various utensils and bowls, and various herb and spice bottles. The entire set is designed to nest within each other, so that they fit together into the 15” x 15” x 12” slow-cooker pot. Upon closer inspection, each implement is engraved with small, intricate runes and sigils inlaid in a dark golden metal.

When used to prepare a meal as part of an exploration action, the crockery automatically casts the purify food and drink glamour on any ingredients added, and any creature that partakes in the meal while resting makes easier hit die rolls. With sufficient ingredients, one meal prepared by the crockery can serve 2d6 medium-sized creatures.

The crockery also allows an alchemist to prepare any alchemical elixir they know into the meal, extending its potency and softening its aftereffects. Any elixir that normally measures its effects in combat rounds instead measures its effects in exploration turns, and any aftereffects that inflict a critical hit are easier to save against.

### Flying Carpet

*Rare (Arcane)*  

*Ingredients: Air Pearl, feysilver, dragonmoth silk*

Often imported from the Sultanate, a flying carpet is an ornate 3’ x 5’ rug that can be commanded to levitate with a word. Once airborne, the rug can carry up to 500 lbs of passengers or cargo at a speed of 100 paces per combat round, or 60 miles per hour, following the mental commands of the creature who activated it. It can maintain this pace for up to 1d6 hours before its enchantment suddenly fails, dropping itself and anything carried to the ground. The rug can also be deactivated manually by speaking the same command word while flying. Once deactivated, the carpet cannot be used again until the next sunrise or sunset.

### Folding Carriage

*Rare (Arcane)*  

*Ingredients: Aether Pearl, orichalc, feysilver,*

*bamboo, giant spider silk, sapient pearwood*

A folding carriage is a variant of the bag of holding enchantment built into an ornate covered wagon that folds down into an intricate wooden tile the size of a large human’s palm. Unfolding a folding carriage is an intricate process that takes an entire exploration turn, and requires a Finesse check to unfold it correctly. Once expanded, the inside of the cart holds a trapdoor with a stairway down into a 20’ by 30’ by 10’ tall internal space. It also holds four carriage seats, a seat for a coachman, and a modular hitch for two horses or one ox-sized beast.

### Healer’s Staff

*Uncommon (Primal or Divine)*  

*Ingredients: Wood Pearl, orichalc, feysilver, exotic*

*wood quarterstaff*

A healer’s staff appears to be a 6-foot long wooden quarterstaff with a crook at the end. Inlaid into the crook is a large round jade gemstone. Upon closer inspection, thin golden and silvered runes and sigils have been inlaid along the staff’s length.

The healer’s staff may be used as a normal quarterstaff in combat, or as a divine or primal focus. While you are holding it as a focus, whenever you heal any creature, any test or save made to perform a healing action is easier, any saving throw that you assist with is easier, and any hit dice rolled to recover hit points as a result of your actions are easier.

### Holy Relic

*Legendary (Divine)*  

*cannot be forged*

Each holy relic weapon is unique, being the holy symbol used in life by a martyred saint. A holy relic grants a priest one additional preparation of each circle; these preparations may only be used to prepare spells from that saint’s domain.

### Lantern of Revealing

*Uncommon (Arcane)*  

*Ingredients: Flame Pearl, Aether Pearl, orichalc*

A lantern of revealing appears to be a dark brass lantern with a carved white gemstone holding the oil wick, and another golden gemstone inset into the front screen. The gaps in the lantern-cage that let out the lamp’s light seem to form strange sigil-like shapes, which cast odd shadows when lit.

When a lantern of revealing is filled with normal lamp oil and lit, it gives off bright light out to 5 paces and dim light out to 10 paces. Additionally, any invisible object, or any object disguised by illusion magic, casts shadows based on its true shape, rather than any illusion cast on it. Noticing that a shadow does not correspond to any object is an Alertness roll, which is made easier if a creature devotes its entire action to scrutinizing the environment and the lamp’s shadows.

### Magic Wand

*Any Rarity (Arcane)*  

*Ingredients: Dragon Pearl, orichalc or feysilver,*

*exotic wood dowel*

A magic wand is a stick of carved and polished wood with metal-inlaid runes carved along its body and a dragon pearl affixed to one end. Each magic wand can carry a different conjuration or transmutation spell, with the maximum circle that can be cast dependent on the rarity level of the dragon pearl used (which determines the rarity level of the artifact as a whole). The wand uses its wielder’s attack throws, saving throws, and task checks to determine any spell effects.

A magic wand can be used once per sunrise or sunset at its full strength, or once per hour if its imbued spell is one circle lower than its dragon pearl. A rare magic wand may cast an imbued first circle spell once per turn.

A magic wand’s cantrip effect can be used indefinitely until all its other charges have been expended, at which point it is merely a stick of wood with a shiny knob on the end until at least one casting has recharged.

### Magic Staff

*Any Rarity (Arcane)*  

*Ingredients: Two Dragon Pearls, orichalc or*

*feysilver, exotic wood quarterstaff*

A magic staff is identical to a magic wand, except that it is embedded in a longer stick of wood. This gives more space for the staff’s runes and sigils, both focusing its magic, and allowing it to gather aetheric energies at a faster rate and thus recharge more quickly. It may also be used in combat as a standard (non-magical) quarterstaff weapon.

A magic staff operates identically to a magic wand, but any saving throw against its effects is one step harder. Additionally, it recharges as if it was one rarity level higher than an equivalent wand.

### Magnificent Mansion

*Legendary (Arcane)*  

*Ingredients: Aether Pearl, Air Pearl, Flame Pearl,*

*orichalc, feysilver, brass, silver, sapient pearwood*

A magnificent mansion is another variant of the bag of holding enchantment designed for storage volume rather than portability. A magnificent mansion typically appears as a pair of items: a brass doorknob with a keyhole, and a matching ornate silver key with a small inset crystal.

To activate the mansion, a creature needs only affix the doorknob to any flat surface more than 5’ by 8’ in size, then insert and turn the key. A doorway will then form on the surface, allowing entry to a vast internal space – often more than 500 feet on a side and more than 30 feet high. Within the space is a hearth with inset Air and Flame pearls to supply warmth and breathable air, plus whatever other amenities the designer sees fit to include.

If the outer doorway is closed, the doorknob can be detached and the key removed, causing the doorway to disappear altogether. The inner doorway does not require a key, but it can only be opened so long as the outer doorknob is attached to a suitable surface – legends abound of wizards trapped within their own mansions when a rival or rebellious servant made off with their doorknob while they were inside.

### Mapmaker’s Kit

*Common (Arcane)*  

*Ingredients: Earth Pearl, orichalc, jade, bamboo,*

*cloth*

A mapmaker’s kit appears to be a wooden tablet nine inches wide, twelve inches long, and a quarter inch thick, attached to a fine cloth pouch by its drawstring. Inside the pouch are a quill pen, an ink bottle, and a number of small hexagonal jade tiles, each with an engraved symbol Upon closer inspection, the tablet and tiles are engraved with intricate designs inlaid with a dark gold metal, and the pouch’s drawstring is closed by a golden-brown striped gemstone that emits a soft inner glow. Each of the jade tiles is an inkstamp of a common cartographic symbol from the Old Empire.

A mapmaker’s kit may be activated by filling the ink bottle with magic ink, placing a blank parchment on the tablet, dipping the quill into the ink and beginning to draw. It makes any Lore check easier to produce a map of what the viewer has seen over the past exploration turn or hour of travel.

### Mirror of Distant Visions

*Rare (Arcane)*  

*Ingredients: Aether Pearl, feysilver, silver*

A mirror of distant visions appears to be a bluish-silver oval mirror with a clear gem inset below it. Various sigils and runes are engraved into the border in a different metal. When a creature touches the gem, they can cause the view within the mirror to turn or move as if seen from the eye of a flying creature. The view can move at up to 1,000 paces per combat round, or 600 miles per hour. This can be quite disorienting; the controller must make a Lore or Survival check every three miles in order to know precisely where they are viewing. The mirror resets its viewpoint to a “normal” mirror at each sunrise or sunset.

If the controller has met another specific creature, they may speak their name aloud while touching the gem to see from that creature’s eyes (albeit reversed left-to-right), and may then move the mirror normally from that position.

### Ornithoptoculus

*Rare (Arcane)*  

*Ingredients: Air Pearl, Aether Pearl, feysilver,*

*brass, exotic leather, balsa wood, oiled parchment*

An ornithoptoculus is a small silver-and-gold sphere the size of a ripe plum, covered in ornately carved grooves and whorls. Upon closer inspection, a small button can be found on one side; pressing this button activates the device.

When activated, the ornithoptoculus unfurls one or more pairs of mechanical wings, often fashioned to resemble dragonfly or hummingbird wings. A large, spherical eyelens opens on the side opposite the activation button, and then the contraption rises to the air and hovers, awaiting the proper commands. If none is given within a span of six seconds, it slowly descends, then deactivates and closes back up into a sphere.

Construct -The ornithoptoculus is a tiny automaton with a

Dexterity of +3 and a saving throw vs. blasts of 5+.

It is always considered to be evading, and automatically

attempts to dodge any area effect that would affect it; it is

destroyed if it suffers any damage, and can operate for one

hour before it must power down and recharge until the next

sunrise or sunset.

Synchronizing - An ornithoptoculus is designed to be used in

conjunction with a Spectacles of Arcane Sight, Spectacles of

Distant Vision, Mirror of Distant Visions, or Mapmaker’s Kit.

Without one of these devices, it is nothing more than a curious

novelty.

When properly synchronized with a Mirror or pair of Spectacles,

it may be piloted remotely by the wearer while they focus,

relaying everything it sees into the viewing device. It has an

intrinsic Arcane Sight enchantment woven into it, which any

wearer may use at-will. It may also broadcast the wearer’s

voice, with a slight ‘tinny’ distortion, at normal speaking volume.

When synchronized with a mapmaker’s kit, the kit also self-

animates, its quill and tiles clacking across the tablet as they

draw a map of whatever the construct sees. A mapping

ornithoptoculus always hovers directly above the kit it is

synchronized to, at a height based on the scale of the map being

drawn (five feet above the map kit for a 25-pace dungeon map,

100’ above the kit for a half-mile exploration map, or 1000’

above the kit for a 24-mile travel map.) The kit cannot draw any

features that the ornithoptoculus cannot see; areas of

obstructed terrain are simply left blank.

### Portable Hole

*Rare (Arcane)*  

*Ingredients: Aether pearl, giant spider silk,*

*feysilver, mimic ink*

A portable hole appears to be a four-footdiameter circle of black cloth with a drawstring. A glittering gem is affixed to the end of the drawstring, and thin metallic runes and sigils are woven into its border.

When the hole is unfolded and affixed to any solid surface, it immediately transforms into a 10 foot deep hole in that surface. If placed on a surface less than ten feet thick, the hole opens to the other side.

The hole may be removed from the surface by grabbing the gem on its drawstring and pulling; once used, a portable hole cannot be used again until the next sunrise or sunset.

### Replenishing Decanter

*Uncommon+ (Arcane)*  

*Ingredients: Water pearl, feysilver, silver, quartz*

A replenishing decanter appears to be an expensive quartz flask with a silver handle and lid and an inset aquamarine gem, filled with clear fluid. On closer inspection, the handle is inlaid with runes and sigils of a darker silvery color. When the decanter is tipped while the gem is pressed, the lid opens and an unending stream of water pours out. A decanter can produce one cup of fresh, drinkable water per six-second combat round, or up to 5 gallons of water per ten-minute exploration turn.

An uncommon decanter can produce up to 5 gallons of water between one sunrise or sunset and the next. A rare decanter can produce up to 30 gallons of water per day, and a legendary decanter (sometimes called a never-empty decanter) can produce 30 gallons of water per hour indefinitely.

### Revealing Mirror

*Rare (Arcane)*  

*Ingredients: Dream pearl, feysilver, silver*

A revealing mirror is a large silver wallmirror with an inset gem on its frame. Any object or creature seen through the mirror is briefly shown for what it truly is, stripped of any illusion magic that might conceal it.

Creatures that can shapeshift are seen as their natural forms, and undead creatures show a dark aura of decay.

### Teleportation Circle

*Legendary (Arcane)*  

*Ingredients: Aether pearl, feysilver, orichalc*

A teleportation circle is a 10-foot-diameter circle on the ground inscribed with modular sigils that can link its location to any other teleportation circle, anywhere in the universe. A teleportation circle is activated by touching its activation sigils in a particular sequence to specify a destination portal.

When activated, a shimmering portal opens within the circle and remains open until the end of your next turn. Any creature that enters the portal instantly appears within the destination circle. If the destination circle is occupied, if an improper sequence is keyed in, or if the destination portal is damaged, the travelers can choose for the activation to fail, or can attempt to push through. If the issue was due to an occupied or blocked portal, the travelers suffer 3d6 aetheric damage, but arrive within the circle and push any interposing creatures or material out of the circle in a random direction.

Determining a portal's sigil sequence after examining the portal for one hour is a hard Science check. You can commit a new sigil sequence to memory or write it in your spellbook after studying it.

### Wishing Stones

*Legendary (Unknown)*  

*Ingredients: Dream Pearl, Aether Pearl, Earth*

*Pearl, Water Pearl, Wood Pearl, Air Pearl, Flame*

*Pearl, unknown additional ingredients*

The wishing stones are a collection of seven enormous dragon pearls, one of each element. When brought together in one place, they allow their possessor to perform a third-level Arcane, Divine, or Primal ritual which culminates in the summoning of the Mother of Dragons, an Aetheric being of immense power.

She will patiently listen as the ritualist describes their wish, and then will cause that wish to be fulfilled - but often with unexpected side-effects or consequences. She sometimes interprets wishes in inscrutably metaphoric ways, and other times interprets them shockingly directly. The ritualist must succeed in a Lore test, an Intuition test, and a Wit test, all with a difficulty based on the complexity of the wish; failure at any of these tests means that the wish was fulfilled with subtle twists or ironies that will be revealed later.

Once the wish is fulfilled, the seven wishing stones are scattered, each teleporting to a different random – but invariably difficult to reach – location.
