## Economics & Technology

Magic and dwarven science allow for much faster technological progress than our own world has experienced. The most obvious examples of this are the relics and wonders of the Old Empire, which reached a level of technology comparable to our own mid-20th century before social and economic collapse left it all in disrepair.

Even so, the world's medical and metallurgic understanding are roughly equivalent to our own, at least in areas with strong dwarven influence. What the Successor Kingdoms lack most is large-scale infrastructure.

### Human Technology

Europa's government and economies are laid out as a hierarchical feudal empire, which suits the needs of its people well enough, but does not inspire technological innovation the way a modern economy would. New inventions tend to be hoarded as guild secrets, or simply never make it into full-scale production due to a lack of industrial factories to produce them. Even so, anyone who can recognize the signs can see that humans are on the verge of a new technological revolution. The Church is wary of this dawning Renaissance, unsure whether it and the Successor Kings have learned their lessons from the Crusades and the Plague.

### Dwarven Technology

The dwarves maintain an impressive level of industrial capacity. In their heyday, with the vast resources of the Old Empire available to them, dwarven caers built aqueducts, power plants, airships, and the most famous symbol of the Old Empire's rule: the golem armor.

Without the ability to transport and trade vast quantities of ore, precious gems, and materia, dwarven smithing is a shadow of its former glory, even though their technology and skill have not diminished. Given access to the right materials in sufficient quantities and the correct design schematics, a crack team of dwarven engineers could probably restore the Successor Kingdoms to the Old Empire's level of prosperity and luxury. The problem is that those schematics are lost in vaults that no one has opened for centuries, and the materials required would take decades to gather, refine, transport, and finish.

### Barbarian Technology

The fey and barbarian cultures of the wilds would seem to have no need of technology in the traditional sense, but they still possess knowledge and skills that serve their culture. In particular, barbarian knowledge of healing and biology rival our own, including a good understanding of germ theory, hygiene, and pharmacology. Druids guard this knowledge jealously, but enough has been independently discovered by dwarf and human alchemists that medical care in the Successor Kingdoms is surprisingly advanced and effective.

Druids also have an unrivaled understanding of genetics, inheritance, and evolution, which they use to tend to the health of their local ecosystems. This knowledge is guarded even more jealously than their medical science, as they understand quite well what the less restrained imagination of "civilized" humans might do with it. Gnomish druids tend to be a bit more adventuresome than elven or human barbarian druids, hybridizing livestock and crops or even outright engineering new strains with magic to increase yields, guard against pests and disease, or produce new mounts or beasts of burden. Even with their more adventurous spirit, the gnomes still guard this knowledge jealously; ancient texts warn that meddling with the fundamental building blocks of life can produce horrors beyond even the great Plague.

## Technology and Magic

The world of the Successor Kingdoms makes no distinction between "mundane" science as we would recognize it, and magic powered by materia. To a wizard or druid, "magic" is as much a part of their laws of nature as gravity or electromagnetism. Designing an arcane spell, brewing an alchemical formula, or crafting an arcane magic item is no more "supernatural" to them than designing a new rocket would be for us; it simply requires specialized skills and decades of training and study in order to master the techniques. An experienced wizard, dweomersmith, or druid in the Successor Kingdoms is thus as rare or "special" as a brain surgeon or rocket scientist is in our own world.
