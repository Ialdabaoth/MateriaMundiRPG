#### Magic Item Curses

Cursed magic items can have all manner of strange effects, depending on the type of item. By far the most common is a straight reversal of the item’s normal intended effect – bonuses become penalties, and healing becomes damage. Rarer effects can be generated by rolling 2d6 and consulting the following table:

#### Curse Type (2d6)

| | |
|---|---|
| 2-7 | Effect reversal |
| 8 | Slow mental corruption |
| 9 | Hostile Illusion (friends appear as enemies) |
| 10 | Ability loss (one point per day at random) |
| 11 | Rapid Aging (one year per week) |
| 12 | Weapon: attacks always strike self or allies |

Healing item: reverse aging (1 year per week)

Other item: random hostile activation

Feel free to use this table as a starting point for your own curse effects.

After rolling for type, rarity, and any curse effects, you can roll on one of the following tables based on the kind of item to determine the actual item found, rerolling if the item shown is not available at the rolled rarity:

### Prices

Prices should vary according to the local economy and the availability of specific goods. A good rule of thumb is to look at the modern cost of an equivalent good or service in US dollars or Euros, and then multiply by 100 to roughly gauge its cost in drakhs.

The following costs are listed in silver pieces; remember that 1 silver piece is 10 copper, and 1 gold piece is 10 silver. These costs are roughly standard, but might adjust up or down by a factor of 2 depending on the local economic conditions. Custom-made gear will be, on average, two to five times more expensive than something bought “off the shelf”, but will be higher quality. If in doubt, use a multiplier of (2d6+3) x 10% for an offthe-shelf item, or (3d6 x 25%) for a piece of custom work. If the player wants to haggle, you can apply a price adjustment in the winner’s favor after a Wit or Charm contest of somewhere between 10% and 25%, depending on how the haggling plays out.
