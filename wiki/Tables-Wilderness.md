## Wilderness Terrain

The party will occasionally find itself beyond the borders of your campaign map. If you need to generate wilderness terrain on the fly, these tables will help. Of course, as with all the rules presented in this book, dice should always take a backseat to plausibility and fun.

When you come across a road or river, immediately roll a d6 to determine its direction, then roll a d20 for each subsequent river or road hex along the indicated direction:

D20 River or Road Direction

| | |
|---|---|
| 1-2 | 2 hexsides counter-clockwise |
| 3-6 | 1 hexside counter-clockwise |
| 7-14 | straight |
| 15-18 | 1 hexside clockwise |
| 19-20 | 2 hexsides clockwise |

Believably filling in a map with new terrain requires that adjacent terrain types have some plausible connection to each other. This can be achieved through the following method.

First, note the type of terrain that the party is currently on. Then, for each adjacent hex that the party has not yet explored, roll a d20 and consult the following table.

For each new hex, you can also roll a d6 to determine whether it should have a particular point of interest. On a '6', the hex has some kind of point of interest appropriate to the terrain - hills and mountains will likely have lookouts, but might also have a cave, barrow, or even a mine; plains, rivers, roads and oases are more likely to have settlements or ruins, and wetlands and forests are likely to have monster lairs or faerie inhabitants. You should create your own d6 table for each terrain type, that reflects the kinds of encounters and ruins that you want your campaign to focus on.

### Resource Value

If the player characters decide to build a domain on a hex, you will need to determine the fertility value of each hex, as well as whether there are any useful mineral deposits.

#### Fertility

The base fertility of each half-mile sub-hex within a 24-mile domain hex should be rolled when it is first surveyed. Roll a d6:

Terrain Type / Fertility (D6)

| River | 1-2 abundant | 3-5 fertile | 6 marginal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wetland | 1 abundant | 2-5 fertile | 6 marginal |
| Forest | 1 abundant | 2-4 fertile | 5-6 marginal |
| Plains | 1 abundant | 2-4 fertile | 5-6 marginal |
| Rough | 1-4 fertile | 5 marginal | 6 barren |
| Hills | 1-4 fertile | 5 marginal | 6 barren |
| Mountain | 1 fertile | 2-5 marginal 6 barren |  |
| Cliffs | 1 fertile | 2-5 marginal 6 barren |  |
| Desert | 1-2 marginal | 3-4 barren | 5-6 blighted |
| Oasis | 1 abundant | 2-5 barren | 6 blighted |

Feel free to adjust these rolls based on the lay of the land and actual geographical sense; for example, ‘abundant’ hexes in an oasis will tend to clump together near a single body of water, while ‘abundant’ hexes along a river will typically be closer to the riverbanks.

Surveying a sub-hex is a one-day project, requiring a Science check, a Lore check, and a Survival check. If at least two checks succeed, the surveyor accurately assays the land.

Once a farm is established within a half-acre hex, its yield is determined by a fertility roll (assuming all goes well through the year):

| Land Quality Fertility Value |  |
|---|---|
| Blighted | 0 |
| Barren | 1d6-1 |
| Marginal | 2d6 |
| Fertile | 2d6+3 |
| Abundant | 2d6+8 |

The amount rolled is months of food supply produced at harvest per acre of farmed land; a single half-mile sub-hex has 100 acres, of which 60 are typically farmed at any one time. Thus, for a properly managed subhex, the fertility roll measures the number of years of food produced for a family of five.

#### Ore Deposits

To determine whether a 24-mile domain hex has any useful mineral deposits, roll a d6 as it is surveyed:

| Terrain Type | Ore Deposits (D6) |
|---|---|
| Mountain | 1-3 none, 4 poor, 5 some, 6 rich |
| Hills | 1-4 none, 5 poor, 6 some |
| Other Terrain | 1-5 none, 6 poor |

Finding actual minerals within a domain hex requires prospecting the various sub-hexes within that hex to find mineral veins, then constructing mines once they're found.

Prospecting a sub-hex is a one-day project requiring a Science check.

On a success, roll 2d6 to determine whether a mineral vein is discovered, and if so what type. Roll 3d6 and add the lower two in a poor hex, or roll 3d6 and add the higher two in a rich hex.

| 2D6 | Type of Ore Deposit | Output per crew |
|---|---|---|
| 2-9 | None | N/A |
| 10 | Iron | 3d6 x 200 lb |
| 11 | Stone | 3d6 x 100 tons |
| 12 | Metal (roll 1d6): |  |
|  | 1-5 Copper | 3d6 x 500 lb |
| 6 | Precious Metal (roll 1d6): |  |

| 1-4 Silver | 3d6 x 50 lb |
|---|---|
| 5 Gold | 3d6 x 5 lb |
| 6 Gemstones | 3d6 x 10 carat |

If the roll indicates no deposits, the answer is definitive. Otherwise, a mine or quarry can be established to begin extracting the mineral from the ground. Digging a mine or quarry is an extended construction project.

Once a mine is dug, roll 4d6 and add the lowest two dice together. This is the mine’s total sustainable labor capacity, measured in 100-man (or 20-dwarf) work crews. Each crew that works the mines for a month outputs a certain amount of resource, which is measured in tons, pounds, or carats of finished material. A roll of ‘18’ on a gold, silver, or gemstone mine indicates a rare deposit of materia or magical metals:

| Type of Ore Deposit | Materia Found per Crew |  |
|---|---|---|
| Silver | feysilver | (1d6 lb) |
| Gemstones | materia | (3d6 x 10 charges) |
| Gold | orichalc | (1d6 lb) |
