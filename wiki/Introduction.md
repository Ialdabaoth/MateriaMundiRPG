## Introduction

This book contains the rules for a roleplaying game in the "old school" style of the first tabletop RPGs created in the 1970s.

## What’s a Role-Playing Game?

This is a *role-playing game*. In it, you play the role of an adventurer in a fantasy world, where magic is real and heroes venture forth on dangerous quests for fortune and fame.

Characters gain experience by overcoming danger and recovering treasure, growing in power and ability as they do so.

You need at least two people to play this game, although it's far more enjoyable with a troupe of four or more people. You don't need a board or playing pieces to play (although many players find them useful) - all that you really need are these rules, some dice, a pencil and paper, and your imagination.

### Definitions

When you set up to play this game, one person will be designated as the *referee*. Each of the other people plays the role of a fantasy character, designated a *player character*. The person assigned to play that player character is their *player*. You can think of a player as an actor in a partially improvisational theatre performance, and the referee as the director. The group of player characters adventuring together are called the *party*.

### How to “Win”

“Winning” and “Losing” are not the right way to think about a fantasy adventure game. While the referee controls monsters and traps that might threaten the player characters, the referee should not see themselves as playing “against” the players - they are a guide and a judge, whose primary job is to keep the action flowing and present the adventure to the party in a neutral manner. They are not “on the players’ side”, but they are equally not “on the monsters’ side”; their job is to create and present an exciting adventure.

Keep in mind that the referee’s primary job is to maintain the sense that the party exists within a world. If the rules in this book result in an obviously absurd situation, it is the referee’s job to ignore them, and figure out what happens instead.

### Setting Up

It’s the referee’s job to prepare the setting for each adventure before it begins. They will create maps, monsters, and mortal non-player characters (NPCs) for the party to interact with. Traditionally, the destination for an adventure is often called a "dungeon", because most early games were set exploring vast underground caverns or stone rooms beneath ancient castles. Adventures might be set in the wilderness, at sea, or in a bustling city, but the term ‘dungeon’ remains as a legacy of those first days. Dungeons might be purchased or designed by the referee from scratch, but in either case the referee can expect to spend a lot more time preparing than the players. The referee’s job takes the most time, but is also the most creative and rewarding.

The next few pages will explain character creation, which is the process each player uses to create the details of their character. Your character will have abilities that define their core talents and weaknesses, an origin (such as human, dwarf, elf, or gnome) that makes them part of the setting, and a character class (essentially a profession) that gives them the skills and competencies that they will rely on to survive the adventure.

When the referee has created their dungeon and all the players have created characters, the game is ready to begin! Each session of the game is called an adventure, and might last only an hour or might stretch to fill an entire weekend. An adventure begins when the party sets out for the dungeon, and ends when they return home and divide up their treasure. The amount of time that this takes depends on the players and the referee.

Several related adventures (one leading to another, often with the same players and characters) are called a campaign. Campaigns can last a very long time indeed, with some going on for several years.

### Dice

For ease of play, this game only uses two kinds of dice: the d20 and the d6. Each player generally needs two d20’s, and between three and six d6’s.

## Medieval Setting

This game's default setting is a fantasy version of our own world’s medieval Europe, where magic is real and heroes venture forth on dangerous quests for fortune and fame. In this world, the Old Empire was a magicallypowered, high-tech, and brutally dystopian society, until the coming of a new Faith led to its slow collapse – a period of history called the Successor Wars. For nearly a millennium, medieval Successor Kingdoms fought to claim the Emperor’s empty throne, either by winning the approval of the Faith’s stewards or through direct military conquest.

Meanwhile, the barbarian tribes to the North of the Successor Kingdoms have slowly begun to join the Faith, while to the South and East the Sultanate maintains an uneasy truce with the Successor Kings over control of the vital silk-and-spice trade routes to the far Orient. The brutality of the Successor Wars has recently come to an end, due to the even more brutal horror of the Great Plague – a sorcerous pestilence unleashed during the wars between the Successor Kingdoms and the Sultanate that slowly killed its victims, then raised them again as hordes of mindless undead.

Now that the Plague has mostly run its course, the Successor Kings are enjoying a Renaissance of discovery and opportunity, gradually re-discovering the technological wonders of the Old Empire, re-claiming and re-settling territories lost during the plague, and occasionally fanning or dousing the embers of the last Succession War.

Beyond the safe walls of civilization’s keeps and cities, the wilds churn with dangerous magical energies. Two adjacent magical realms – called Faerie and Shadow by mortals – leak energy into the familiar material world, creating fey and undead menaces that must be defended against.

The style of this setting could be called a postapocalyptic fantasy, but the focus should be on the hope and possibility of a brighter future. The goal is to strike out into the world, find lost wonders, restore them to their former glory, and use them to rebuild civilization.

## For Those Familiar With Other Systems

This edition uses a familiar “old-school”

edition of the first published roleplaying game as its base rules, but makes some changes to those rules in order to both simplify play, and better reflect the nature of the setting. If you’re familiar with “B/X” or “OSR” style play, you should probably read the following section to get used to the changes.

### Simplified Dice

For ease of play, this game only uses d20s and d6s; you’ll generally need two d20s and four d6s. If you’re familiar with similar games that use a wider range of dice, don’t worry: hit dice, weapon and magic damage, and other effects that would normally use different dice, have been adjusted to fit neatly into these two probability ranges. Determining success or failure has also been simplified, with fewer situations needing mathematical "modifiers"

to a player character's dice rolls.

### Simplified Classes

The character class system for this game is vastly simplified: each class only has 9 levels, with all attack and saving throw thresholds always increasing at +1 per level. Characters gain a base of one experience per adventure, and gain a new class level whenever their experience equals their new class level.

Defeating monsters and gaining treasure are worth additional experience, so it is possible to level up faster if you are smart and brave.

### Simplified Abilities

Abilities do not have a separate “ability score” and “ability modifier”; what B/X calls the “ability modifier” is simply your ability.

These rules include six saving throws – vs.

Doom, vs. Hold, vs. Blast, vs. Trick, vs. Curse, and vs. Fear  – where each saving throw is associated with a different ability. Thus, each of your abilities will be important in different circumstances.

### Simplified Skills

In addition to saving throws, these rules also include a unified "skill system", with twelve “n-in-6” talents split evenly between four abilities (Dexterity, Intelligence, Wisdom, and Charisma). Each of those four abilities will affect a player character's starting proficiency in up to three of those talents.
