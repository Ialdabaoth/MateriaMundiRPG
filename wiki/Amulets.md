## Amulets

An amulet is any magic item worn on the neck or shoulders, such as a cloak or pendant.

### Amulet of Dedication

*Uncommon (Divine)*  

*Ingredients: Dream Pearl, feysilver, duskworm silk*

Amulets of dedication are small worn pendants that may be affixed to a necklace, earring or similar position. While worn, the amulet protects the user from psychic harm and influence. If the wearer would be beguiled, panicked, or injured by a source of psychic damage, any saving throw made to deny or snap out of those effects is easier. The wearer also halves all psychic damage that they would receive. They are usually carved in the shape of holy symbols.

An amulet of dedication can also be used to protect the wearer against violating a sworn oath. If the wearer whispers an oath to the amulet at dawn or sunrise, any effects that might compel them to act against that oath are harder, and any save to deny such effects is easier. However, if they willingly choose to violate an oath that has been whispered to the amulet, it immediately and violently shatters, inflicting 3d6 psychic damage to the wearer.

### Amulets of Friendship

*Uncommon+ (Arcane or Primal)*  

*Ingredients: Dream Pearl, feysilver, orichalc,*

*silver, gold, jade, amethyst*

Amulets of friendship are paired lockets, one silver and one gold. When worn by two different creatures who are not hostile to each other, the wearers can communicate telepathically across any distance.

Rarer amulets exist in identical sets of three to eight. These amulets allow an entire group to communicate telepathically with each other, and additionally allow any magic-user in the group to touch their amulet and cast a spell on another member of the group as if they were touching, as long as they are within 20 paces of each other. Amulet sets require both a dream pearl and an aether pearl in the construction of each amulet.

### Amulet of Protection

*Uncommon (Arcane)*  

*Ingredients: Aether Pearl, orichalc, gold or silver*

Amulets of protection are small worn pendants that may be affixed to a necklace, earring or similar position. While worn, the amulet protects the user from physical or magical harm. If the wearer would be injured by any source of damage, it immediately casts shield to protect the wearer.

This shield does not require the wearer’s focus to maintain, and lasts 2d6 combat rounds.

Unfortunately, it also grants the user far less control than if they had cast it themselves; the protection cannot be dismissed until it has worn off, and if the shield’s temporary hp are reduced to zero, the amulet shatters.

Once an amulet has been activated, it requires one sunrise or sunset to recharge.

### Amulet of Water Breathing

*Uncommon+ (Arcane or Primal)*  

*Ingredients: Water Pearl, Air Pearl, feysilver*

Amulets of water breathing are small worn chokers that clasp onto the neck. While worn, the amulet allows any creature that breathes air to breathe underwater without difficulty, or allows any creature that breathes water to breathe air without difficulty.

Dolphin centaurs called ‘merfolk’ also create rarer amulets, which have the additional effect of transforming a merfolk wearer’s lower half into a pair of human-like legs when dry, or a non-merfolk wearer’s lower half into a merfolk’s dolphin tail when wet.

### Cloak of Invisibility

*Rare (Arcane)*  

*Ingredients: Dream Pearl, feysilver, duskworm silk*

A cloak of invisibility appears to be a finely made silk cloak with a cloudy quartz clasp, and thin silver-threaded runes woven along its inner lining. A keen observer might notice that its colors subtly shift to match the wearer’s surroundings. While worn, a cloak of invisibility makes all Stealth tests to hide or pass unseen easy. As an action, the wearer can pull the cloak around themselves, casting the Blur or Invisibility spell upon themselves until dismissed. This spell does not require focus to maintain, but taking damage has a chance (1-4 on 1d6 for an uncommon amulet, 1-2 on 1d6 for a rare amulet) of disrupting the spell.

Once used, the cloak’s invisibility requires one hour to recharge.

### Cloak of Shadows

*Common (Arcane or Primal)*  

*Ingredients: Dream Pearl, feysilver, duskworm silk*

A cloak of shadows appears to be a finely made dark grey silk cloak with an obsidian clasp, and thin silver-threaded runes woven along its inner lining. While worn, a cloak of shadows makes all Stealth tests to hide or pass unseen easy, so long as the wearer is not in bright light. As an action, the wearer can pull the cloak around themselves while standing in shadows, casting the Invisibility spell upon themselves while they remain stationary. This spell does not require focus to maintain, but taking damage has a 50% chance of disrupting the spell, and any bright light shone upon the wearer will automatically break the effect.

Once used, the cloak’s concealment requires an hour to recharge.

### Foreigner’s Amulet

*Uncommon (Arcane)*  

*Ingredients: Dream Pearl, feysilver, silver*

A foreigner’s amulet appears to be a silver choker with an inlaid pearl at the throat.

Upon closer inspection, the inside of the choker is engraved with small, intricate runes and sigils inlaid in a darker bluish-silver metal.

When worn, the amulet allows the wearer to listen to a foreign language that they do not understand for at least ten minutes (one exploration turn), after which they may understand and speak that language until they stop focusing or remove the amulet. The amulet only grants understanding of one language at a time; once used, it cannot be used again until the next sunrise or sunset.

### Gull-Wing Cloak

*Rare (Arcane or Primal)*  

*Ingredients: Air Pearl, feysilver, giant spider silk,*

*thunderbird feathers*

A gull-wing cloak appears to be a light bluishgrey satin cloak with a ruffle of white and grey feathers woven into the shoulders and collar. While worn, a gull-wing cloak will buffet your fall using the feather fall cantrip, always allowing you to land on your feet without injury.

You may activate the cloak to cause it to unfurl into a pair of 15-foot wingspan wings, granting you a Flying speed and flying dash speed of double your current speed and dash speed. Any acrobatic maneuvers use Agility to maintain control while airborne.

Roll 1d6+3 level to determine the maximum number of combat rounds that the cloak can sustain its flight speed. Once you use this effect, the cloak requires one sunrise or sunset to recharge. Its feather fall effect continues to work while the cloak is recharging.

### Iron-Will Amulet

*Uncommon (Arcane)*  

*Ingredients: Dream pearl, orichalc, iron*

An iron-will amulet appears to be a cloudy translucent stone set in an iron cage. Upon closer inspection, tiny golden runes are inscribed upon the insides of the iron wires, inlaid in a dark golden metal.

While worn, all saving throws that the wearer would roll vs. any effect that would alter their thoughts, emotions, or behavior are easier.

### Magic Pendant

*Common+ (Primal or Arcane)*  

*Ingredients: Dragon pearl, feysilver, orichalc*

A magic pendant appears to be a precious gemstone set into a gold and silver backing, on a gold and silver chain. On closer inspection, the back of the setting contains tiny sigils and runes, filled with metal of a slightly different color. The pendant functions identically to a dragon pearl of the same rarity, but the wearer can hold other items in their hand while using it.

### Pendant of Recall

*Rare (Primal or Arcane)*  

*Ingredients: Two aether pearls, feysilver*

A Pendant of Recall appears to be a clear gemstone pendant in a silver wire cage, set on a silver chain. Upon closer inspection, peering through the pendant appears to reveal a different location altogether.

When a pendant of recall is constructed, it uses two aether pearls, which must be carved from the same materia or created together in the same alchemical process. These two pearls are 'bound' together, with one set into the pendant and the other left free. When used, the pendant and its wearer disappear in a flash of light, reappearing within five feet of the free gem. If there is no available space to appear, the user takes 5d6 aetheric damage and fails to teleport.

Once used, a pendant of recall cannot be used again until the next sunrise or sunset.

### Soulcage Amulet

*Rare (Arcane)*  

*Ingredients: aether pearl, feysilver*

A Soulcage Amulet appears to be a dark, nearly black gemstone pendant in a silver wire cage, set on a silver chain. The gem itself appears cool to the touch, regardless of the ambient temperature.

When the wearer of a soulcage amulet fails a saving throw vs. Doom, the amulet rips their soul from their body. This immediately reduces the wearer to zero hit points and kills them, without any saving throw. However, so long as the amulet is not removed from their body, their body will be preserved against decay and corruption, and cannot be used to create a corporeal undead. Their soul remains bound to the amulet, allowing them to manifest as a wraith while within 5 paces of it and not in direct sunlight.

So long as the amulet has not been removed, the revive miracle, the reincarnate glamour, and potions of regeneration treat the wearer as if they had just died moments ago. If the amulet is removed from the body, normal decay begins to set in immediately, but it stops immediately if the amulet is returned. If the body is destroyed or too much decay sets in, the wearer's soul becomes trapped within it, unable to move on to the everafter until a priest performs the lay to rest miracle on the amulet. Priests of the death domain tend to find the soulcage amulet troubling, and consider its use to be questionable at best.

### Wild Shape Talisman

*Common+ (Primal)*  

*Ingredients: Dragon pearl, (optional) animal pelt*

Druids can perform a primal ritual to craft wild shape talismans, which can be used to transform into natural animals or fey beasts. Each wild shape talisman holds the form of a specific creature. At level 7, a champion with an animal patron may also perform this ritual, but only to create or use a pelt talisman that corresponds to your patron animal.

First, you must carve a dragon pearl into a talisman that represents the animal that you wish to transform into. A common dragon pearl can hold the shape of any 1 to 2 hd animal or beast, while an uncommon dragon pearl can hold the shape of any 3 to 4 hd animal or beast, and a rare pearl can hold the shape of a 5 hd animal or beast. Beasts with elemental powers, such as a thunderbird or a phoenix, require dragon pearls of an element appropriate to their powers; natural animals instead use wood pearls, and beasts with stranger powers use dream or aether pearls.

Once the talisman is carved, you must wear it in the hour before sunrise or sunset, and find a living example of the creature you wish to become. You run with the herd, pack, or individual of that species until next sunset or sunrise – at which time, you have transformed into that animal, and the talisman is complete. Each talisman is attuned to the mortal that crafted it, and cannot be used by anyone else.

#### Pelt Talismans

A druid can craft a wild shape talisman out of the pelt of the animal to be transformed into, with an appropriate dragon pearl incorporated somewhere into the pelt; this is safer than an amulet talisman, since the pelt transforms along with the druid, but the druid cannot wear any armor or other gear as they don the pelt. A pelt is destroyed if the druid is reduced to zero hit points while in wild shape.

#### Amulet Talismans

Amulet talismans are easier to craft, but more dangerous to use, since your natural form is "stored" within the talisman while you take the shape of the beast; losing the talisman traps you within your new form. Any creature holding or wearing the talisman while you are transformed can understand you as if under the effects of the Speak with Animals glamour.

### Transforming

Once you have crafted a wild shape talisman, you may put it on to transform into a typical member of that species. Anything you are carrying or wearing doesn’t transform with you, unless you perform the dedicate object ritual on it prior to changing. Your new limbs and body shape will likely make wearing armor, wielding weapons, or using tools difficult; any items in your possession will be dropped, or possibly damaged during the transformation. An amulet talisman will fall off as you transform, and must be stored in a safe place until you wish to transform back; a pelt talisman will transform with you, so that you can revert to your original form at will.

#### While Transformed

When you transform, your abilities and saving throws are replaced with those of the animal you transform into, and you gain its size, speed, armor, and any special movement types that it has. You immediately roll the creature’s hit dice to determine your new maximum hit points; you begin at full hit points if you had at least half your hit points before you transformed; otherwise, you begin at half your new maximum hit points (round up). You also gain the animal’s attack throws when attacking with any of its natural weapons, such as fangs or claws, as well as each of its natural talent thresholds if they are superior to your own.

While you retain your own intelligence, personality, and memories, they are filtered through the animal’s brain and instincts, making complex thought more difficult unless you devote your full attention to it.

#### Transforming Back

Reverting to your natural form requires you to touch the talisman that you originally used to transform and will yourself to change back As you revert to your original form, you roll each of your hit dice to determine your new current hit points (up to your original form’s maximum), halving the roll result if you were below half your maximum hit points before you transformed. If you revert to your natural form due to being reduced to zero hit points, you still have zero hit points when you return to your original shape.
