When combat begins, each creature that isn't surprised gets to act in the first round, which is also called the **surprise round** (see [Starting an Encounter](E9-Exploration#starting-an-encounter)). After the surprise round is over, all creatures in the encounter who are capable of action may do so. Once all actions have resolved, a new round begins. Each round of an encounter is about **6 seconds**.

## Combat Actions

Actions normally happen simultaneously. If the order of two actions matters, each creature whose actions are in conflict makes a Dexterity [ability check](E9-Abilities#ability-checks), and the winner chooses whether to act first or last.

### Movement

Unless a creature's action or circumstances prevent movement, it can move as it performs its action. Each character has a natural movement rate of **five paces (25 feet)** over even ground; difficult terrain or other slowing effects halve this movement speed, to three paces per round.

### Attacks

The most basic thing you can do during an encounter is attempt to attack an enemy within reach or range of your weapon. Make an [attack throw](E9-Abilities#attack-throws), with the difficulty based on the circumstances; if you hit, you make a damage roll based on your [weapon](E9-Weapons). See [[Damage|E9-Damage]].

### Other Actions

You can attempt to perform any reasonable action during a combat round; the referee determines an appropriate difficulty based on your description, and tells you which talent or saving throw to roll.

### Magic

Casting in a combat encounter is slower and riskier than other actions. You must declare to the referee the effect you intend to cast at the beginning of the round, and you cannot move while you are casting. If an attack hits you after you declare your effect but before you can cast it, you must save vs. [Doom](E9-Abilities#doom) or your magic is disrupted. Using magic items that aren't weapons also follows these rules.

#### Fast Magic

Certain magics are fast to perform. A fast magic can be performed in reaction to another action in combat, even if the caster hadn't originally declared that they were casting magic this round. They simply switch their action to casting the magic, then do so.

## Stunts and Maneuvers

In a combat encounter, you are encouraged to think tactically and creatively. Unless otherwise specified, these combat options are available for any creature to use if the circumstances are appropriate. The following is a common, but by no means exhaustive, list of combat actions that may be performed if the circumstances favor them. The referee and players are also welcome to come up with new maneuvers to add to their own game.

### Dash

Dashing increases your movement to **20 paces** per round if you are unencumbered, or **10 paces** per round if you are encumbered, at the cost of not being able to do anything else during that round. If you are within reach of a guarding creature's melee weapon, you provoke an interrupting attack from it as you move.

### Charge

A charge combines a dash and attack into a single action. For every 5 full paces that you move directly towards your target, your attack gains +1d6 damage. If you hit, your target is shoved backwards, and you end your movement in the target's space.

### Evade

Evading makes you harder to hit with all weapon attacks, at the cost of not being able to do anything else during the round. You can also make your movement before other creatures. Your Dexterity must be +1 or better to evade, and you cannot be encumbered.

### Dodge

If you successfully save vs. [Blast](E9-Abilities#blast) while you are evading, you may also move up to 1d6+Dex paces before the blast resolves. If you manage to leave the blast area, it does not affect you.

### Grab or Shove

You can attempt to shove or grab a target by making a melee attack roll. A grab attack must be made with a free hand or an entangling weapon, while a shove can be made with any melee attack. If you hit, the target makes a save vs. [Hold](E9-Abilities#hold) to halve the damage and avoid being grabbed, moved, or knocked prone, with a difficulty based on the relative sizes of the attacker and target.

### Disarm or Pilfer

An [expert](E9-Expert) can attempt to disarm or pick a pocket by making a [Finesse](E9-Talents#finesse) check instead of a weapon damage roll after hitting with a grab attack. If you succeed, you pilfer or plant one reachable item of your choice instead of dealing damage.

### Guard

Guarding is a special action that lets you make interrupting attacks. Only [fighter](E9-Fighter) classes can guard. While you are guarding, you can interrupt each other creature's movement, attack, or other action while it is within reach of your weapon to make an attack of your own. If your attack hits, their movement or action is stopped.

### Brace

If you are wielding a polearm with the piercing property, you may brace it into the ground as you choose the guard action. You choose a direction as you brace your weapon; if a creature moves towards you from that direction, your interrupting attack against them gains +1d6 damage for each 5 full paces the target moved towards you, and ignores all armor (even heavy armor). While braced, you cannot interrupt attacks from other directions.
