## Weapons

While mortals continue to devise an endless variety of ways to kill each other, most weapons fall into a few simple categories.

Any unusual or improvised weapon should be treated as if it was the closest logical equivalent to a weapon on this list.

### Simple vs Martial Weapons

Simple weapons may be wielded by most creatures; any weapon without the "simple"

tag is harder for a character class who is not trained in its use to effectively attack with it, and untrained creatures cannot use a nonsimple weapon to make opportunity attacks or special attack stunts.

Fighter and expert classes are usually trained in non-simple weapons; most magic-user classes are effectively limited to using simple weapons without significant difficulty.

Arrows and Bolts

An arrow or bolt fired from a bow or crossbow normally has

the Piercing property; broadhead arrows can be used instead,

which do not have the Piercing property but instead give any

bow or crossbow +1d6 to damage against non-steel armors.

### Masterwork Weapons

Masterwork weapons may be commissioned, which grant a +1 bonus to their damage rolls. Unlike armor, a masterwork weapon does not need to be made for a particular individual. Masterwork ranged weapons instead grant a +1 bonus to their attack throws.

### Weapon Sizes

All weapons have a Size, which represents the weapon’s weight, length, and heft. If you are not wielding any medium or heavy melee weapons, you can choose whether to use your melee or ranged attack throw threshold to determine if your melee attack hits or misses.

#### Light

*weapons always deal 1d6 damage. A*  

light weapon may be wielded in your off hand, if your main hand is wielding a light or medium weapon. If you do, you add both weapons’ damage rolls together to determine the attack’s total damage.

#### Medium

*weapons may be wielded in one or*  

both hands by medium creatures, giving you the option to leave your other hand free to wield an off-hand light weapon, a shield, or to perform other tasks. Small creatures must wield medium weapons two-handed, and so cannot pair them with a shield or an off-hand weapon.

Medium melee weapons deal 1d6 damage + your Strength if wielded one-handed, or 2d6 damage (with no Strength modifier) if wielded two-handed.

Medium ranged weapons always deal 1d6 damage; wielding a medium ranged weapon two-handed steadies it and makes its attack throws easier.

#### Heavy

*ranged weapons always deal 2d6*  

damage, while heavy melee weapons always deal damage equal to 2d6 + your Strength. All heavy weapons require both your hands to wield, so they cannot be used with an offhand weapon or a shield. Small creatures cannot wield Heavy weapons easily, finding all attacks and parries with them harder.

A medium creature can hold a two-handed weapon with one hand to perform actions that require a free hand, but attacking requires that you have both hands on the weapon.

### Weapon Properties

#### Parry

*- Some melee weapons allow you to*  

parry incoming melee attacks with them.

Parrying with a weapon gives up your attack for the round to force an incoming melee attack to be re-rolled. If the attack still hits and would sunder your armor, it breaks your weapon instead.

#### Blunt

*weapons stun instead of bleeding*  

whenever any damage die rolls a '6'.

#### Entangling

*weapons can ensnare an opponent*  

whenever they roll a '6' on a damage die. The attacker can choose to trip or disarm the target unless they save vs. Hold.

#### Piercing

*weapons penetrate light or medium*  

armor, ignoring its damage reduction; their damage is still reduced by heavy armor.

#### Polearm

*weapons may be braced against a*  

charge from a particular direction as a combat action. A braced polearm immobilizes its wielder until they un-brace, but allows its wielder to make an easy opportunity attack against any creature that charges them in the braced direction, and adds the charger’s own charge damage bonus to the damage of the wielder’s opportunity attack against it.

#### Ranged

*weapons have a range or thrown*  

value, which is measured in paces. When attacking out to this distance, all attacks with the weapon are easy. Out to twice this range, attacks are at normal difficulty; out to 3x this range, weapon attacks are hard. Making a ranged or thrown attack provokes an attack of opportunity from anyone whose melee weapon can reach you.

#### Reach

*- Some melee weapons allow you to*  

attack creatures further away than normal. A weapon with Reach can attack creatures up to an additional pace away, while a weapon with Reach 2 can attack creatures up to 2 paces away. You may also use Reach weapons to make opportunity attacks at the same distance.

#### Sundering

*weapons reduce the target’s armor*  

by 1 for each damage die that rolls a ‘6’. If the target has a shield or successfully parries with a weapon, the target's shield or weapon is damaged instead. A damaged shield or weapon can't be used until it is repaired.
