## G. Mass Combat

### Scale

The mass combat rules presented here are designed to operate at a base scale of 100’ x 100’ fields. When heroes engage each other within a single 100’ x 100’ field (or 100' diameter hex field), the referee might decide to provide the party a single 20-pace x 20-pace map for that specific melee, but otherwise movement and combat within each field is abstracted. A single half-mile sub-hex will typically serve as an entire battlefield, which is 25 fields across along each axis, ideally divided into approximately 600 field hexes.

Damage in mass combat is always delivered as structure damage. Large and huge creatures always move as individual units, while medium creatures that are not player characters or heroic NPCs are grouped into ranks of up to 10 creatures, all typically outfitted with the same armor and weapons.

### Ranks

A rank effectively operates as a single unit that has the equivalent of one structure point per hit die among its constituent creatures. They attack as a single unit, using their leader’s attack throw value to determine hit or miss. Any members of the rank with weapons that normally inflict hit point damage roll their damage dice without bonus or penalty, inflicting one sp of damage per ‘6’ rolled.

Each time a rank suffers damage that reduces its total hits below 10, it must save vs. Fear to resist breaking. A broken rank cannot make attacks, and can only flee towards safety at the end of each round. If two allied broken ranks occupy the same field, they may combine into a single new rank (adding their total hd together), and then make a new save to rally. A rank that rallies can rejoin the battle.

A broken rank that is attacked must make a new save each time it sustains further damage; it does not rally on a success, but is completely destroyed on a failure. The referee can determine the survival of each NPC in a destroyed rank after the battle; any player characters in a destroyed rank are reduced to zero hit points and must cling to life each round until they stabilize.

### Movement

As its action, a creature or rank may dash into any adjacent field. If either its current square or the new square contains difficult terrain, or if the creature or rank is encumbered, the unit must roll Agility to not become bogged down and stuck in their current field. Flying units never bog down.

Units with a dash speed of 30 paces or greater (designated as fast or very fast in the bestiary) may cross one field per 20 paces of dash movement that they possess. Moving into or out of a field with any difficult terrain takes 30 paces of dash movement. If such a creature attempts to move into a space when it has fewer paces of movement remaining than required, but still at least ten paces of movement, it may roll Agility to attempt to move through one additional field, becoming bogged down on a failure. A unit may only attempt this once per round, at the end of its movement on its turn.

If you are using a map delineated with squares rather than hexes, each field of diagonal movement costs 30 paces of dash movement, while diagonal movement through difficult terrain takes 40 paces.

### Attacks

Any creatures or ranks that occupy the same field are capable of engaging in melee combat with each other. Any unit may freely use its movement to engage with another unit in its field, forcing those two units into melee combat; an already-engaged unit may attempt to disengage, prompting an opportunity attack from any enemy unit that it was previously engaged with. If a unit without reach (or with shorter reach) engages a unit with longer reach than it, the unit with longer reach may make an interrupting opportunity attack.

Units in the same field that are not engaged with each other may instead perform ranged attacks against each other without range penalties. Un-engaged units may make ranged attacks against units in other fields; count each field as 100’ of range (counting every other diagonal field twice, as normal for combat on a square grid).

### Special Units

#### Mounted Cavalry

A knight mounted on a warhorse counts as a single unit with a size equal to that of the larger creature, with sp equal to the combined hit dice of the knight and mount. The warhorse uses its action each turn to dash, freeing the knight’s action to attack – this gives the unit a total effective speed of 30 paces, allowing it to move freely and attack at will. A cavalry unit finds it easier to avoid being bogged down, and when it engages another unit, it resolves it as a charge attack. Up to five mounted knights may form a single cavalry rank, adding their hit dice together. A polearm-equipped rank that does not move during its turn may choose to brace against cavalry charges; bracing allows the polearm rank to make an easy interrupting opportunity attack against any unit that attempts to charge it, and doubles the damage roll for the opportunity attack.

A cavalry unit that is damaged by an attack must make a saving throw vs. Hold to stay in the saddle. On a failure, the rider is knocked off their mount and falls prone.

#### Field Cannon

A field cannon and its crew count as a single rank, with minimum hd equal to the crew requirement of the cannon. Aetheric cannon require additional crew trained in Science with total hd equal to their power draw. If the cannon rank’s structure points are reduced below its minimum crew size, the rank automatically breaks, and the cannon itself is destroyed. Any critical hit against the rank also destroys the cannon and breaks the rank. A field cannon must track its position in 45 degree increments, using its action each turn to either load or charge the cannon, aim the cannon at a new 90 degree arc of fire, or fire the cannon at a target within its current arc of fire. All field cannon use the crew’s Science to load and fire.

#### Hero Units

Player characters, magic-user NPCs, and important named NPCs are hero units. Each hero unit moves independently on the battlefield, unless it chooses to join ranks with another allied unit. A hero unit that joins ranks adds its current sp and hd to the rank, and automatically survives and falls out of rank whenever its unit breaks ranks, suffering 1 sp of damage and continuing on as a solo hero unit unless it decides to form up with another rank.

A hero unit that is reduced to 0 sp is reduced to 0 hp and must immediately cling to life, following all the normal rules for tracking injury. If a hero unit stabilizes, it regains 1 sp and may rejoin the battle if conscious. After the battle, each 1 sp of damage that a hero unit suffered is converted into 1d6+2 hp damage.

Any hero unit that attacks a huge creature with an easy attack and hits with both d20s may choose the hit location; if the hero unit achieves a critical hit, it may also negate that creature’s Duck reaction.

#### Large and Huge Creatures

Each large or huge creature counts as a single unit on the battlefield. Each of its location’s hit points are divided by 10 to determine its effective structure points, to a minimum of 1 sp per section. A creature that has both sp and hp divides its hp by 5 and adds it to its sp. A medium creature or rank that attacks a huge creature must re-roll any head hits that it achieves, unless it flies or has sufficient reach with its weapon to reach the head. Each die of damage inflicted by a rank must roll a separate hit location.

#### Formations

Multiple units within the same field can use their movement to align themselves into a formation. Each unit in a formation declares and performs the same action each round.
