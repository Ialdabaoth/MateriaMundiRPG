## Huge Creatures

## Combat Rules

A huge creature has a span of 3 or more, taking up a circle on the battlefield with a width equal to its span and filling most of its space. Its attacks naturally reach another span paces beyond this, and if it wields a weapon with the reach property, it multiplies that property by its span.

Attack throws from a large or smaller creature that target a huge creature are easier, and its attack throws against those creatures are harder. It also has disadvantage to its saving throws vs. any area attack roll that completely covers all of its spaces. Even so, it typically has five times the hit points of a medium creature, and so represents a threat equivalent to an entire party of heroes of equivalent level.

### Structure Points

Some Huge monsters such as dragons have an outer layer of tough hide or armor. Such creatures will have structure points in addition to hit points; in order to inflict hit point damage, the creature’s structure points must first be reduced to zero.

Any attack against a structure with a weapon that inflicts hit point damage inflicts one point of structure damage for each damage die that rolls a '6'.

### Huge Weapons

Weapons wielded by Huge creatures always deal structure damage, rather  than hit point damage. If they make an unarmed attack, their unarmed strike also inflicts structure damage. Against creatures that do not track structure, such a weapon deals 5d6 damage for every 1D6 of structure damage they inflict, plus (or minus) 1d6 for each static +1 bonus.

### Hit Locations

A Huge creature’s hit points are split between multiple locations; any attack that hits must roll a die to randomly determine the location hit, unless the attack was easy and both the attacker’s d20s hit.

The referee should be sensible about which hit locations are exposed to a particular attack, and adjust the hit location roll accordingly (or forgo it altogether if it’s clear which section would be hit).

#### Ducking

Most Huge creatures have a head or head-like appendage that is extremely sensitive to damage. They can react whenever that section is hit to force the attacker to re-roll the hit location.

#### Crippling Wounds

Each location on a huge creature becomes crippled if its hit points are reduced to zero. Critical hits only affect the hit points of the section hit.

#### Frenzy

A Huge creature that suffers a crippling wound will often immediately fly into a frenzy, immediately making an attack against everything nearby even if it has already acted in this round of combat.

#### Area Attacks

A Huge creature that suffers an attack from an area effect rolls the attack’s damage for each of its locations, reducing that damage by half if it saved vs. the attack; if successful, no damage is dealt to its head or head-like location. A huge creature does not distinguish between burst and blast attacks, since the creature itself fills most of its space.

#### Falling

Giant creatures that fall prone suffer 1d6 bashing damage to each of their possible hit location die roll numbers, due to their immense bulk. If they fall from a height of 10 feet or more, increase this damage by another 1d6 for every full 10 feet fallen. This damage ignores structure, and is applied directly to each location’s hit points. A giant creature or construct that has only structure points instead takes 1d6-1 structure damage to each possible location roll per 20 feet fallen.

### Treant

*(Level 4)*  

A huge tree may be awakened by a primal ritual, granting it motion and sentience. If left to its own devices, such a creature typically becomes a warden and guardian of the forest where it first sprouted, learning primal magic and tending to the living things within it.

A treant’s thoughts and reactions are naturally slow, but that does not make it stupid. When given time to think, a treant can show a deep and clever wisdom - although it will seldom apply that wisdom beyond its duties. Above all else,  treants love peace, quiet, and sunlight; if nothing threatens their forest home, they are content to sit and meditate for years on end, soaking up nourishment from sun and rain and soil as they contemplate the beauty of the world around them.
```
Doom 6+ Hold 6+  Blast 13+ Trick 13+ Curse 9+ Fear 11+
Movement:      Normal          Size: Huge 3
Armor:         2               HP:  [2d6+10]
Attacks (5+):  battering blow (1D6 blunt structure)
Con +5 Str +5  Dex -2  Int -2  Wis +1 Cha +0
Talents (2+):  Stealth, Survival
```
**Huge Size -** A treant’s hit points are split between multiple body sections. When the the treant is hit by an attack that wasn’t easy, the attacker must roll 2d6 and consult the following chart:

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| 3-4 | Left Leg | 1x [hp] |
| 5 | Left Arm | 1x [hp] |
| 6-8 | Body | 4x [hp] |
| 9 | Right Arm | 1x [hp] |
| 10-11 | Right Leg | 1x [hp] |
| 2, 12 | Face | ½ [hp] |

**Barkskin -** The treant halves all slashing, piercing, and bashing damage inflicted on it that isn’t inflicted by a sundering weapon, but all fire damage against it automatically inflicts the ongoing fire damage condition.

**Cripple -** A critical hit to any location halves that location’s remaining hp, or reduces that section’s hp to zero if it fails its saving throw. Reducing the body to zero hp kills the treant, while reducing either leg to zero hp hobbles it, reducing either arm to zero hp makes all attacks harder, and reducing the face to zero hp blinds it.

**Regeneration** - If not destroyed, the treant’s Body heals 1 hit point per combat round.

**Frenzy -** Whenever any location is reduced to 0 damage, the treant immediately replenishes all glamour, then may immediately make a sweep attack.

**Sweep Attack -** As its action, the treant makes an area burst attack with its battering blow vs. all creatures within 3 paces of it. Any damaged creature is knocked prone.

**Primal Magic -** The treant may cast any Wood-aspected spell by spending glamour equal to its circle.

**Regenerate -** As its action, the treant spends 1 glamour, then chooses a damaged location and rolls 1d6+5, healing that many hit points in that section.

### Ogre

*(Level 6)*  

A well-fed goblin redcap will eventually grow into a hobgoblin, and a well-fed hobgoblin will eventually grow into a bugbear. A bugbear that can keep its bottomless stomach full might eventually become an ogre.

Other processes exist that can create ogres; certain fey curses can transform a mortal into an ogre, for example, and ogres might themselves beget more goblin-whelps already at ogre size.

An ogre’s appetite is truly voracious, and above all else they crave the flesh of other mortals. It is possible for an ogre to ignore this hunger, for a time, but the smell of blood will invariably send them into a feeding-frenzy that they have little hope of denying.

And ogres can smell blood from miles away.
```
Doom 5+ Hold 5+  Blast 11+  Trick 12+ Curse 9+ Fear 9+
Movement:      Normal          Size:  Huge 3
Armor:         2 (0)           HP:  [3d6+12]
Attacks (5+):  unarmed strike (D6 blunt structure),
               earthbreaker maul (3D6+4 blunt structure)
Con +4 Str +4  Dex -2  Int -3  Wis +0 Cha +0
Talents (3+):  Alertness, Survival
Senses:        Smell (superior)
```
**Sweep Attack -** The ogre has three rage. It can spend one rage as its action to make a 3d6 damage burst vs. each creature within 3 paces of it; each creature may either save vs. Blast or save vs. Hold for half damage. Any creature that fails to save is knocked prone and flung 1d6 paces away.

**Huge Size -** An ogre’s hit points are split between multiple body sections. When the the ogre is hit by an attack that wasn’t easy, the attacker must roll 2d6 and consult the following chart:

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| 3-4 | Left Leg | 1x [hp] |
| 5 | Left Arm | 1x [hp] |
| 6-8 | Body | 4x [hp] |
| 9 | Right Arm | 1x [hp] |
| 10-11 | Right Leg | 1x [hp] |
| 2, 12* Head |  | ½ [hp] |

***Duck -** The ogre can react to force an attack that hits its head to re-roll another location; damage to its head stuns it.

**Cripple -** A critical hit to any location halves that location’s remaining hp, or reduces that section’s hp to zero if it fails its saving throw. Reducing the head or body to 0 hp kills the ogre, while reducing a leg to 0 hp hobbles it, and reducing an arm to 0 hp makes all attacks harder when not using its reckless attack.

**Frenzy -** Whenever any location is reduced to 0 damage, the ogre immediately replenishes all rage and makes a sweep attack.

**Smell Blood -** Ogres automatically succeed on any attempt to smell blood, and can smell freshly-spilt mortal blood from a distance of nearly a mile.

### Jötunn

*(Level 8)*  

The titanic kings of the Elemental Chaos, Jötnar are massive Fair Folk the size of hills, wielding terrifying elemental powers.
```
Doom 5+ Hold 5+  Blast 7+  Trick 7+  Curse 7+ Fear 7+
Con +5 (5+)    Str +5  (5+)   Dex +0  (5+)
Int  +0 (5+)   Wis +0  (5+)   Cha +0  (5+)
Movement:      Fast            Size:  Huge 4
Armor:         5 (elemental armor) HP: [4d6+20]
Attacks (5+):  unarmed strike (D6 structural bashing),
               ax or sword (2D6+5 structural slashing)
Con +5 Str +5  Dex +0 Int +0   Wis +0 Cha +0
Talents (2+):  Science, Medicine, Survival,
               Lore, Charm, Wit
```
**Elemental Glamour -** The jötunn has eight points of glamour, which it can spend as follows:

**Elemental Attack -** The jötunn makes a melee weapon attack and adds +3D6 elemental structure damage if it hits.

**Primal Spellcasting -** The jötunn may cast any primal glamour of its element, by spending glamour equal to its circle.

**Elemental Vulnerability -** The jötunn halves all elemental damage that matches its element, but suffers double damage from any damage of an opposing element.

**Huge Size -** A jötunn’s hit points are split between multiple body sections. When the the jötunn is hit by an attack that wasn’t easy, the attacker must roll 2d6 and consult the following chart:

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| 3-4 | Left Leg | 1x [hp] |
| 5 | Left Arm | 1x [hp] |
| 6-8 | Body | 4x [hp] + 2 SP |
| 9 | Right Arm | 1x [hp] |
| 10-11 | Right Leg | 1x [hp] |
| 2, 12* Head |  | ½ [hp] |

***Duck -** The jötunn can react to force an attack that hits its head to re-roll another location; damage to its head stuns it.

**Elemental Armor** - In addition to hit points, a jötunn is protected by elemental armor, providing it with a small amount of structure. Before any hit point damage can be dealt to its body, this armor must be penetrated.

**Cripple** - A critical hit to any location halves that location’s remaining hp, or reduces that section’s hp to zero if it fails its saving throw. Reducing the head or body to 0 hp kills the jötunn, while reducing a leg to 0 hp hobbles it, and reducing an arm to 0 hp makes all attacks harder.

**Frenzy** - Whenever any location is reduced to 0 damage, the jötunn immediately replenishes all glamour, then makes an elemental attack without spending glamour.

### Primordial Dragon

*(Level 10)*  

Primordial wyrmlings that survive for more than a few centuries eventually grow into adult primordial dragons. Each primordial dragon has imbued itself with one of the five elemental essences – Stone, Water, Wood, Air, or Flame – in a primal ritual that also grows its body to an enormous size.
```
Doom 5+ Hold 5+  Blast 5+  Trick 5+  Curse 5+ Fear 5+
Movement:      Fast / Fly Very Fast Size: Huge 3
Armor:         5 (dragonscale) HP:    [3d6+15]
Attacks (5+ x2): claws (2D6 sundering structural),
               bite (1D6+5 piercing structural),
               wing buffet (1d6 thunder structural),
               tail bash (1D6+5 blunt structural)
Con +5 Str +5  Dex +1  Int +5  Wis +5 Cha +5
Talents (2+):  All skills
Senses:        Dragons have a form of heat vision,
               allowing them to see warm-blooded
               creatures in total darkness.
```
**Elemental Ichor -** The dragon has ten points of elemental ichor. It may cast any primal glamour of its element by spending charge equal to its circle, or may cast a glamour of any other element by spending charge equal to twice its circle. It can also spend charge on the following feats:

**Dive Bomb -** The dragon can fly 160 paces on its turn, or dash 100 paces per phase.

**Breath Attack -** The dragon makes a breath attack in a 10-pace cone, which inflicts 2D6 structural elemental damage to everything along its path.

**Focused Breath -** The dragon can spend 3 ichor to make a breath attack in a 60-pace line, which inflicts 3D6+15 structural elemental damage to everything along its path. If the line is stopped short by a barrier, roll each damage die one at a time against the barrier until it gives way; if it does, the line continues past the barrier reduced by the number of damage dice rolled to penetrate it.

**Huge Size -** A dragon’s hit points are split between multiple body sections. When the the dragon is hit by an attack, the attacker must roll a d20 and consult the following chart unless the attack was easy:

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| 1-5 | Body | 3x [hp] + 25 SP |
| 6-9 | Tail | 2x [hp] + 15 SP |
| 10 | Left Hindleg | 1x [hp] + 7 SP |
| 11 | Right Hindleg | 1x [hp] + 7 SP |
| 12 | Left Foreleg | 1x [hp] + 5 SP |
| 13 | Right Foreleg | 1x [hp] + 5 SP |
| 14-16 | Left Wing | ½ [hp] + 3 SP |
| 17-19 | Right Wing | ½ [hp] + 3 SP |
| 20* | Head | ½ [hp] + 5 SP |

***Duck -** The dragon can react to force an attack that hits its head to re-roll another location; any hit point damage to its head stuns it.

**Dragonscale -** In addition to hit points, a dragon’s hide is protected by a thick layer of dragonscale. Before any hit point damage can be dealt to the dragon, this armor must be penetrated. While any structure points remain in a location, a primordial dragon halves all elemental damage to that location that matches its element.

**Cripple -** A critical hit to any location halves that location’s remaining hp, or reduces that section’s hp to zero if it fails its saving throw. Reducing the head or body to 0 hp kills the dragon, while reducing a leg to 0 hp slows it and makes all claw attacks harder; and reducing a wing to 0 hp prevents the dragon from flying.

**Frenzy -** Whenever the head suffers hit point damage or any location is reduced to 0 damage, the dragon immediately replenishes all charge, then makes a focused breath attack without spending charge.

### Tarrasque

*(Legendary Threat)*  

There are huge creatures, and then there is the tarrasque. The tarrasque is a vast draconic creature the size of a small island. It spends most of its time lurking beneath the waves, but can be roused from its slumber by warfare or powerful works of magic. Once awakened, the beast will rampage across the earth, devastating settlements and toppling tall buildings, until it can be laid low.

Towering hundreds of feet high, the tarrasque’s roar and aetheric breath are devastating weapons that can destroy cities before the creature even reaches shore. Once landed, the tarrasque’s trampling feet and thrashing tail destroy buildings and bridges, while crushing thousands underfoot.

It is believed that there is only one tarrasque in all the world. No known power has yet managed to kill it — when gravely injured, the tarrasque will retreat into the waves and submerge, wandering along the seabed until it finds its nest. There, it slumbers until some new disturbance awakens it.
```
Doom 3+ Hold 3+  Blast 3+  Trick 3+  Curse 3+ Fear 3+
Movement:      Normal          Size:  Huge 20
Armor:         5 (dragonscale) HP:    special
Attacks (5+):  stomp (5D6+10 blunt structure),
               bite (5D6+10 piercing structure)
```
**Primordial Rage -** The tarrasque has ten points of rage, which it can spend as follows: **Roar -** The tarrasque’s roar inflicts 1D6+10 thunder structure damage and 3d6 psychic hp damage to everything within 50 paces. Each creature must save or be frightened for 1d6 minutes; this saving throw is hard.

**Thrash -** As its full action, the tarrasque makes an area attack vs. everything within 10 paces of it. Any creature that fails to save suffers 5D6+10 structure damage and is knocked prone and stunned.

**Aetheric Breath** - The tarrasque can spend 5 rage to deal 5D6+10 structure damage in a 100-pace cone or 500-pace line.

**Huge Size -** Ranged and melee attacks against the Tarrasque are always easy.

The tarrasque’s hit points are split between multiple body sections. When the tarrasque is hit by an attack at long range, the attacker rolls a d20 and consults the following chart:

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| 1-6 | Body | 500 hp, 150 SP |
| 7-9 | Left Hindleg | 275 hp, 80 SP |
| 10-12 | Right Hindleg | 275 hp, 80 SP |
| 13-16 | Left Foreleg | 250 hp, 70 SP |
| 17-19 | Right Foreleg | 250 hp, 70 SP |
| 20* | Head | 50 hp, 20 SP |

***Duck -** The tarrasque can react to force any ranged attack that hits its head from any range to re-roll another location; any hit point damage to its head stuns it.

**Dragonscale -** In addition to hit points, a tarrasque’s hide is protected by a thick layer of dragonscale. Before any hit point damage can be dealt to the tarrasque, this armor must be penetrated. While any structure points remain in a location, the tarrasque ignores all critical hits to that location that aren’t from attacks that deal structure damage.

**Cripple -** Once the dragonscale is penetrated, a critical hit to any location halves that location’s remaining hp, or reduces that section’s hp to zero if it fails its saving throw. Reducing a leg to 0 hp hobbles the tarrasque, while reducing the head or body to 0 hp incapacitates it.

**Frenzy -** Whenever the head suffers hit point damage or any location is reduced to 0 damage, the tarrasque immediately replenishes all rage, then makes a thrash attack without spending rage.

**Regenerate -** The tarrasque regenerates 1d6 hp and 1 SP per damaged section at the end of each round, even if that section has been reduced to 0 hp.
