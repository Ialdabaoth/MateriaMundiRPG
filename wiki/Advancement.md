## Returning Home

## Experience

Whenever you complete an experience milestone and then arrive safely home, your character gains one experience. Once you’ve gained as many experiences as your next class level, reset your experience total to zero and advance your class level by 1. Thus, if you’re starting as a first level character with no experience, you will gain your second class level as soon as you achieve two experiences.

### Prime Abilities

Each class has a prime ability, which adjusts the rate at which  experience levels in that class are gained. If your prime ability is -1 or lower, you will require additional experience each level to advance.

### Hit Dice, Attacks, and Saves

Once you gain a class level, you immediately gain a hit die and any other features from your new class level, up to a maximum of nine hit dice. Roll all your hit dice and add them together; if the result is higher than your character's maximum hit points, set your new maximum hit points to the rolled result.

Gaining a class level reduces each of your saving throw and attack throw thresholds by one, to a minimum threshold of 5+.

### Expertise Training

Expert classes gain training in a talent of their choice each time they gain a class level, while magic-user classes gain training in a specific talent at each even class level. Fighters instead choose and gain a new fighter feat that they do not already possess at each even level.

For experts, which talents were used in the last set of adventures should have some bearing on which gets trained; if there is no clear talent that a character focused on during the last few adventures, then training whichever talent has the worst threshold is usually a safe bet. Referees and players might maintain a tradition of declaring which talents they intend to train at their next level, before they even earn the experience to do so.

## Class-Based Experience

In addition to surviving adventures, each class gains additional experience based on its actions during the adventure. Members of the party split their accomplishments between all members of the same class group once they have successfully returned home; if an individual's share of the tally reaches the appropriate milestone for their level, they gain one bonus experience for that adventure. The referee might also award specific player characters extra experience for exceptional cleverness or bravery during an adventure.

### Fighters

Fighters gain additional experience through combat. Each level of foe defeated, whether through death, surrender, or fleeing, is added to their tally. Large and huge monsters count as 2x and 5x their level, respectively.

### Experts

Experts gain additional experience through recovering treasure. The gold cost of all mundane (non-magical) treasure brought back from the adventure is tallied.

### Magic-Users

Each class of magic-user has their own goals, which determine what accomplishments gain them additional experience.

**Priests** gain additional experience through accomplishing the goals of their faith. They track enemies defeated in the same manner as fighters, but if the party willingly kills a mortal that could have reasonably been redeemed instead, no priest that participated in the murder gains experience for the entire adventure. Priests also count the levels of any allies that they save from mortal peril towards their accomplishments, as well as the levels of any creatures willingly converted to the Faith. **Wizards** and **dweomersmiths** gain additional experience through studying magic. The circle or rarity of each new magic item or arcane spell discovered counts towards their tally. **Druids** gain experience by studying fey creatures in their natural habitat; each new kind of fey creature observed for a full hour counts its level towards the druid's tally.

Restoring the balance of a damaged ecosystem might automatically grant all druids in the party an extra experience.

## Dual-Classing

A player may choose for their character to gain a second character class during play, becoming a "dual-class" adventurer. Each character's origin limits their new class just like it limited their first class, and a character may never have two base classes of the same type (i.e., magic-users cannot choose a second magic-user base class, experts cannot choose a second expert base class, and warriors cannot choose a second warrior base class).

In order to dual-class, a character must have a +1 or better in both their current base class's prime ability and their new class's prime ability. Gaining their new class costs one experience; they gain all their class's first-level feats, but do not improve their attack throws or saving throws. If their new class has a better hit die bonus than their old class, their hit die becomes their new class's; re-roll the character's maximum hit points accordingly.

Whenever they reach enough experience to gain a class level in their new class, they do so. They do not gain a hit die or adjust their attack or saving throws, but any of their class feats that depend on their new class's level will increase in effectiveness as normal.

Once their new class's level matches that of their old class, they may choose which class to train next; when they gain sufficient experience to gain that class's next level, they also gain a new hit die and improve their attack and saving throw thresholds as normal. They then continue alternating leveling up each class until both classes reach level nine.

## Beyond Ninth Level

The rules presented here deliberately limit character progression to nine class levels. This helps keep player characters within the bounds of human capabilities, while still allowing growth and progress. Rewards beyond level 9 involve creating and maintaining domains, managing reputation and renown, or researching powerful magics.

Fighters typically take on a leadership role for a small band of warriors, and begin using their martial prowess to gain control of a geographical area - either through outright violence and intimidation, or by offering their protection from the violence and intimidation of other marauding brigands.

Experts typically take on a role appropriate to a master of their craft. They might gather together a band of fellows, coordinating activities across a wide area.

Magic-users become powers unto themselves, integrating into the world around them in a manner befitting their power and goals.

### Noble Titles

The default world presented in the History handbook is dominated by feudal societies. In feudalism, the land of a kingdom is held by a king or queen, who grants noble titles to those who pledge to defend it. A noble title always corresponds to a portion of land, called a fief, which that noble is expected to defend with their life if called upon — so a county is given to a count, a barony to a baron, a duchy to a duke, a bordermarch to a marquis, and so on.

### Keeps and Domains

Eventually, an adventurer will have made enough of a name for themselves that they might plausibly be granted a title of nobility, royally recognized coat-of-arms for heraldry, and a small fiefdom to rule. Alternatively, barbarians or outlaws might have amassed enough power that they can simply take control of an area by force, acting as its de facto rulers even if not recognized de jure by the king. Maintaining control of a fief, and administering to its prosperity, often becomes the primary focus of a high-level campaign.
