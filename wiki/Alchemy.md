## Alchemy

Alchemy is the science of transmuting physical substances by combining or distilling them into their components.

Wizards, druids, and dwarf dweomersmiths can all become highly skilled in alchemy, although their practices and ingredients might be somewhat different. At core, though, the principles of elixir and potion design are based on the alchemical interactions of the ingredients; thus a druid who examines a wizard’s methods or products is likely to be able to duplicate them with little effort.

Elixirs and poisons are alchemical brews that are meant to be imbibed, and tinctures are meant to be rubbed onto or into their target, while potions are meant to be thrown, poured, or otherwise externally applied.

## Brewing

Brewing an alchemical formula is an arcane or primal ritual. Wizards and dwarves require a separate formula from a spellbook for each formula that they wish to brew, while druids can improvise from whatever is on-hand.

A single common alchemical formula requires one materia charge, an uncommon formula requires two materia charges, and a rare formula requires three. Brewing is an arcane or primal ritual of the same circle as the resulting formula’s rarity, which must be repeated once per hour until the formula is finished; common formulae take two hours to brew, while uncommon formulae take four hours, and rare formulae take six. Legendary potions must be nursed for an entire week, and require a common dragon pearl of the appropriate element dissolved into the formula.

An attending alchemist must make a Science or Medicine test once an hour to keep the formula from spoiling. When brewing legendary potions, alchemists often work in shifts so that the process can be monitored around the clock.

### Ingredients

Most elixirs and potions are recipes handed down over generations. Other than the Materia, each ingredient has a hundred different possible substitutions or variations. If you can’t find enough “eye of newt” for your brew, the referee can have you make a Science or Medicine test to figure out what substitutes might be more readily available that will still produce the same effect.

Regardless of the non-magical ingredients, each Common potion requires one “charge”

worth of materia; an uncommon potion requires two charges, a rare potion requires three charges, and a legendary potion requires an entire common dragon pearl. A “charge” of materia may be as small as a pinch or drop or as large as a fist-sized stone; more potent materia will require less physical matter to amount to one “charge”. A Science check can determine how much elemental or aetheric energy a particular piece of materia contains. Gathering materia is a Survival or Science test that takes approximately an hour, against a difficulty based on the local environment; success nets 1d6 charges of types determined by the referee. Druids tend to be picky about their materia, only gathering it carefully and sustainably in a ritual context, but they also tend to be much better at gleaning useful scraps out of nearly any natural environment.

### Ingredient Substitution

As long as the brewer has the correct element and amount of materia, the rest of a potion’s ingredients can be substituted by a clever alchemist. Substituting ingredients will make the Science or Medicine check harder, unless the alchemist performs all the work to create a new formula that properly uses the new ingredients. Deciding which ingredients can substitute for each other is up to your referee, but should follow common sense, along with a passing nod to science and folklore.

### Harvesting Ingredients

All alchemical formulae, magic items, spells, and talismans require materia to power them. Characters can gather their materia as an exploration action by making a Survival check and a Science check, each with a difficulty based on how much natural material is in the local environment; if both checks succeed, they gather one charge worth of materia. The referee can randomly determine the types of materia gathered based on the area searched:

| Terrain Type | D6 | Materia Type |
|---|---|---|
| Cavern, Mountains 1 |  | Stone |
| River, Swamp | 2 | Water |
| Forest, Jungle | 3 | Wood |
| Plains, Hilltops | 4 | Air |
| Desert, Volcano | 5 | Fire |
| Eldritch | 6 | Psychic / Aetheric |

The referee rolls 2d6; if both dice roll the same number, the materia found is the type indicated by that number. If the dice roll different numbers, the materia found is the type indicated by the terrain type searched. On double sixes, a rarer psychic or aetheric materia charge is found; the referee will roll an additional die to determine which, with a 50% chance of either type.

#### Harvesting from Monsters

The bodies of various supernatural creatures are also rich sources of materia and other ingredients; cleaning a carcass and harvesting it for resources is also a Survival test that can be performed as an exploration action in the wilds or the dungeon.

#### Harvesting in Faerie

The desperate or adventurous might even choose to venture directly across the veil into Faerie to harvest materia; while incredibly dangerous, gathering materia from Faerie is as simple as plucking the nearest brightlycolored berries, leaves or rocks. Survival rolls in Faerie to harvest materia are less about determining whether materia is found, and more about avoiding random encounters against fey beasts or the Fair Folk.

## Consuming Elixirs and Poisons

When an elixir or poison is consumed, it immediately begins producing its effects.

These effects will often have a duration, before it begins to wear off. Some elixirs will wear off gradually, with their beneficial effect lessening until it dissipates, or even reverses into a harmful aftereffect.

### Elixir Aftereffects

After an elixir’s beneficial effects wear off, its aftereffects begin. An elixir’s aftereffects are treated exactly like a poison. The imbiber always gets a save vs. Doom to resist the aftereffects; this saving throw is easy for common elixirs, and hard for legendary elixirs. On a success, any damage inflicted by the aftereffects is halved, and any duration that would normally last until the imbiber sleeps 6 hours instead only lasts one hour.

### Tailored Elixirs

A brewer can attempt to make an elixir easier for a particular creature to imbibe; this requires an exploration action spent making a Medicine check, while the brewer studies the intended imbiber’s metabolism and health to optimize the brew for them. On a success, it will be easier for that imbiber (and only that imbiber) to resist the aftereffects of that brewed elixir, if consumed within the same day that it was brewed. The brewer must perform this action each time they wish to brew an elixir for a specific imbiber, since each creature’s health, metabolism, and diet changes from day to day.

### Consuming Multiple Elixirs

If a creature imbibes a second Elixir while still under the ongoing effects of a previous elixir or poison (including aftereffects), it must immediately save vs. Doom, and cannot gain any benefit from temporary Constitution bonuses (but still suffers from any penalties). If the imbiber succeeds, it gains the bonus of each elixir it has imbibed, but also suffers all aftereffect penalties from all such formulae. On failure, the imbiber suffers 1d6 damage per rarity of potion imbibed since it last slept, all such potions drop to their maximum penalty aftereffect, and the imbiber cannot gain any positive effects from further potions until all damage has healed.
