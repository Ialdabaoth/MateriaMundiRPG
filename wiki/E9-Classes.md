This game tracks your character's talents and experience through a concept called a **character class**. Your class is an abstraction that represents the general focus of your character's talents as an adventurer. Each class focuses on a different aspect of the game: **fighter** classes focus on combat prowess, **expert** classes focus on skills and tricks, and **magic-user** classes focus on magic and the supernatural. As you advance, your character gradually gets better at the talents their class focuses on, while also improving their general ability to survive hardship and danger.

> **Classes and Themes**
> The class names presented here are generic "catch-all" categories for various kinds of proficiency. For example, an "expert" might represent a thief, treasure-hunter, diplomat, spy, assassin, wandering noble dilettante, or any number of other professions. If you have a particular background in mind for your character, work with your referee to determine which character class best fits your idea.

## Choosing a Character Class

Once you have an origin and your six abilities, you can choose a character class. Each origin should have its own set of three classes to choose from: one fighter class, one expert class, and one magic-user class. Some origins might have additional class options; humans, for example, often have two different magic-user classes to choose from, one for "arcane" or scholarly magic and one for "divine" or religious magic.

The four basic classes are one fighter class, one expert class, and two magic-user classes. Together, they illustrate how the rules handle each of the three basic class roles of the system.

## Hit Points

Each class has a "hit die", which is a d6 + your Constitution. Fighters gain an additional +2 bonus to each hit die. Once you've picked your class, your character's maximum hit points are the maximum possible roll of your hit die (as if the d6 had rolled a 6): **8 + Con** for a fighter, **6 + Con** for anyone else.

## Class Feats

Each class also gives you one or more class feats, such as sneak attack or some kind of magic ability. The rules for each class feat are explained with that class.

### Fighter Training

Fighters improve their attack throw thresholds by 1 each time they gain a level. Once their attack throw threshold becomes 9+ or better, they inflict a critical hit whenever their attack throw beats their threshold by 10 or more. See [[Fighter|E9-Fighter]].

### Expertise

Experts train a talent of their choice each time they gain a class level, including level one. They also gain expertise in various actions; expertise makes all actions under its purview easier, allowing almost-impossible feats of skill. The referee is the final arbiter of what actions are or aren't physically possible for an expert to attempt with their expertise. See [[Expert|E9-Expert]].

### Magic Preparation

Magic-user classes can prepare and cast magic. As a magic-user gains class levels, they can prepare more and more powerful magic. A magic-user may prepare their full repertoire of magic each dawn and dusk, and can also make a talent check to attempt to re-prepare their expended magic during exploration. Each class uses a different talent to prepare their magic, and improves that talent at class levels 4 and 8. They also have another related talent, which they improve at class levels 2 and 6. See [[Wizard|E9-Wizard]] and [[Priest|E9-Priest]].
