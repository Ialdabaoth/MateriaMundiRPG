Every roll in E9 is one of four kinds:

- **Attack throw**: d20, match or beat your attack threshold. See [Attack Throws](E9-Abilities#attack-throws).
- **Saving throw**: d20, match or beat your saving throw threshold. See [Saving Throws](E9-Abilities#saving-throws).
- **Task check**: d6, match or beat your talent threshold. See [[Talents|E9-Talents]].
- **Ability check**: d6 + ability, against a threshold set by the referee. See [Ability Checks](E9-Abilities#ability-checks).

## Easier and Harder Rolls

Any attack throw, saving throw, task check, or ability check can be made easier or harder by various circumstances. When circumstances combine to make a roll **easy**, you roll two dice and use the better roll. When circumstances combine to make a roll **hard**, you roll two dice and must use the worse roll. If a roll is neither hard nor easy, you roll a single die.

If some circumstances make a situation easier while others make the same situation harder, each circumstance making it easier cancels out one circumstance making it harder, unless the referee decides that a particular circumstance overwhelms all the others.

If multiple circumstances all make a situation easier or harder, the referee might declare that it is now impossible to succeed or fail at that task, and not call for a roll at all. The referee is the ultimate arbitrator of whether a particular action is possible for a particular character; both your level of talent and the difficulty of the circumstances are taken into account. As a rule of thumb, the referee shouldn't make saving throws impossible or automatic in most circumstances, while talent checks should often become automatic or impossible as a matter of course, simply based on the players' descriptions of their actions and how they interact with the reality of the situation.

## Difficulty

| Difficulty | Roll |
|---|---|
| Automatic | No roll necessary |
| Easy | Roll 2 dice (choose better) |
| Normal | Roll 1 die |
| Hard | Roll 2 dice (choose worse) |
| Impossible | No roll possible |

## Multiple Attempts

Unless the referee decides otherwise, each subsequent attempt of the same action is one difficulty step harder than the last, as you run out of things to try.

## Rewarding Ingenuity

A large part of a fantasy adventure game is coming up with clever solutions to problems. If the party's approach to a particular task is particularly clever or effective, the referee will lower the difficulty of that task check accordingly, or possibly just declare that the task is successful and move on.
