## Resting and Healing

Player characters who are injured or fatigued can choose to rest in the dungeon, although doing so is hazardous.

Resting requires relative safety, peace, and quiet; an encounter always interrupts a rest.

## Crafting, Repair, and Preparation

Some magic-users can attempt to re-prepare magics during an exploration turn, while other characters can roll Finesse checks to attempt to craft ammunition, make simple repairs to damaged equipment, or perform other tasks of similar complexity.

## Tending to the Injured

### Fatigue

Characters and other creatures who have lost less than half of their hit points are merely fatigued, rather than actually injured. Each turn of rest allows a fatigued creature to roll one hit die, then regain that many hit points. A resting creature can perform no other actions that turn.

### Injury

A creature is injured if it has lost half or more of its hit points. Without magic, healing actual injury is a slow and arduous process, which cannot be done in the dungeon. A character can use Medicine to attempt to treat an injured character immediately after an injury, if both of them spend an exploration action tending to the wounds. On a success, the injured character rolls one hit die and recovers that many hit points. If this heals them above half their hit points, they are no longer injured; otherwise, no further non-magical healing will be possible until they return home from the dungeon, or find another suitable hospice. This first-aid attempt can only be made once, in the hour immediately after the injury is sustained; once this opportunity has passed, non-magical healing is no longer possible in the dungeon for that character.

### Healing Magic and Potions

Magic can also heal injury or fatigue, and can even be used during exploration or in combat. Priests have access to healing magic directly, while wizards, dwarves, and druids may brew up healing elixirs to aid the injured.

## Interrupted Rests

Resting in the dungeon is risky and leaves the party somewhat vulnerable to attack, prompting the referee to roll for a random encounter. If the party is interrupted by a random encounter while resting, they do not regain any resources or hit points, and are automatically surprised by the encounter.

Most parties will post a watch while the rest of the party rests, guaranteeing that at least one character will be ready to respond to any threats should they arise.

## Camping Overnight

If the party must camp in the dungeon overnight, their sleep must be uninterrupted for at least six hours, and they must not be excessively hungry or thirsty. Each uninjured character rolls all their hit dice and adds them together, then regains up to that many hp if the result is higher than their current hp total. Magic-users may then begin preparing any daily magics at the next dawn or dusk.

### Preparing Magic

Wizards and dweomersmiths can prepare their full set of magics at dawn or dusk after a six hour rest in the dungeon. This process takes one hour (six exploration turns), but does not require any task checks or saving throws. If a magic-user is interrupted while preparing their daily magic, they are automatically surprised.

## Deprivation

Each day that a character cannot get a clean drink, a full meal, and at least 6 hours of sleep without being interrupted, they do not regain hit points. Until they have gained enough food, water, and sleep, all their rolls will be harder, and they cannot prepare spells, prayers, or other magics. If they go more than three days without rest or water, they begin suffering 1d6 hit points of damage per day until they die.
