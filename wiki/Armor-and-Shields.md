## Armor and Weapons

Adventurers can be expected to get into combat with other creatures with alarming frequency. Wearing armor and having a trusty weapon by your side in such situations can mean the difference between life and death.

## Armor

Armor provides creatures with a Defense value, which reduces the damage of weapon attacks. If armor would completely negate the damage from a weapon attack, the attack instead deals 1 blunt damage. Armor provides no protection against any elemental, aetheric, or psychic damage. Light and medium armors cannot protect against piercing attacks, but heavy armor can.

Wearing any kind of medium or heavy armor makes all Agility and Stealth checks harder, and wearing heavy armor if your class isn't familiar with it makes all weapon attacks and saving throws vs Blast harder as well. Fey classes who wear steel armor, or wizards who wear any armor, lose access to their magic until they take that armor off.

These numbers are for normal, quickly manufactured armors; a skilled craftsman with access to high-quality raw material can choose to dedicate their time and skill towards making a masterwork piece of armor, which grants a +1 to its Defense. Such armor will often be listed with a +1 after the name, such as “Heavy +1”, indicating that their defense is higher. Masterwork armor must be custom-built and fitted to the person it was designed for; it does not provide its +1 bonus for anyone but its intended wearer.

### Materials and Manufacture

Light armors are made from leather or other soft materials, while medium and heavy armors are typically steel. Medium armor weaves small rings or scales of steel together into a flexible mail; heavy armor uses solid bands or plates of metal contoured to the body of the wearer. This means that heavy armor made for one creature cannot usually be worn comfortably by another without some degree of modification by a smith.

Magical metal armor is forged from feysilver or orichalc. Feysilver is a silvery-blue metal with extraordinary lightness and hardness, while orichalc is a deep golden-red metal that is much denser than steel. Armors made from feysilver reduce their encumbrance by one step (medium armor becomes light and heavy armor becomes medium), while armors made from orichalc increase their encumbrance for their weight but have +1 higher defense.

Druids and champions lose access to their magics if they wear steel armors, but champions can still access their battle-frenzy while wielding steel weapons or using a steel shield. Feysilver and orichalc don't interfere with primal magics the way iron and steel do.

### Donning Armor

Putting on or taking off armor is too involved to happen in combat. In a 10-minute turn, a character can don light armor and still perform an action, or don medium or heavy armor as their full action. If someone assists them in donning their armor, both characters can make a Finesse test to attempt to don medium armor more quickly; on a success, both characters can perform an exploration action in addition to putting on the armor.

Heavy armor is complex and heavy enough that it requires assistance to properly don, and can only be donned as a full action. A character who attempts to don heavy armor alone must succeed at a hard Finesse test.

Removing any armor is much simpler, but still too involved to do in combat. A character can remove their armor during an exploration turn and still perform an action, but removing one set of armor and donning another takes their full action - even if both are light armor.

## Shields

Using a shield makes you harder to hit with attacks, essentially by providing you with partial cover that you can carry around with you on your arm. Shields can only be used by warriors who are trained with them; without this training, all attack throws and physical saving throws are harder.

### Heraldry

Shields and armor are often emblazoned with identifying ornamentation called heraldry. A paladin’s heraldry will usually incorporate a holy symbol of some kind, while a warrior’s heraldry simply serves to identify them on the battlefield. Heraldry typically interferes with stealth, which is why it is usually only incorporated into medium and heavy armor or emblazoned on a shield.

Properly designed heraldry can benefit a warrior on the field of battle, if they have sufficient renown that their allies and enemies recognize the symbols and colors. This might make the saves vs. Fear of allies easier and enemies harder, make Bearing and Charm checks easier, or have other effects determined by the referee.

Warriors and priests that come from a noble family will typically inherit their heraldry from their ancestors, while newly-knighted warriors will typically earn sufficient renown to warrant developing unique heraldry by level 5. Dwarves do not typically display individual heraldry, instead opting to display the heraldry of their ancestral clan. The sight of a regimented formation of identicallyoutfitted dwarves is intimidating in its own right, providing the same basic effects as an individual warrior’s coat of arms.

Champion heraldry typically takes the form of tribal paint patterns, tattoos, and runes describing their past feats in battle. These typically have the same effect as warrior heraldry once the champion has accumulated enough of them to imply a real threat (around level 5, as above).

Non-fighter individuals that adopt heraldry will usually be seen as somewhat pretentious, since these classes are not known for their courage and prowess in melee combat. Scouts and hunters also tend to forgo heraldry for more practical reasons, since it interferes with stealth.
