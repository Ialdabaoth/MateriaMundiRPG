## Managing Followers

## Gaining Followers

Each player character can collect a retinue of followers, with a maximum troupe size based on their Bearing.

| Bearing | Maximum Followers |
|---|---|
| 6+ | 1 |
| 5+ | 2 |
| 4+ | 3 |
| 3+ | 4 |
| 2+ | 5 if gaining a new follower causes a player character’s retinue to exceed their Bearing, that character finds it harder to manage all their followers until they let enough of them go to fall under the limit. |

To recruit a new follower, the player character first presents their case; if the referee deems it sufficiently convincing for that particular creature, the player can make a Charm check with a difficulty based on the appeal of the offer and the prospective recruit’s current attitude. If they succeed, the creature decides to join that character’s retinue. Demonstrated loyalty and offered incentives make this check easier, while extenuating circumstances will make it harder.

It’s a good idea to keep a few personality notes about each follower NPC in your party’s retinues; giving each of the party’s followers a distinct voice and personality goes a long way towards fostering immersion and engagement.

### Directing Followers

Bearing is also used to determine whether followers obey orders when under duress, in dangerous or stressful situations, or when the orders are unclear or complex. A Bearing check can be made in most situations to quickly determine if plans go awry due to lack of coordination; this check will be normal difficulty for naturally Friendly followers, easy for Helpful followers, or hard for Indifferent followers.

## Follower Loyalty

Each member of the party’s retinue is an NPC under the control of the referee. Helpful followers will obey orders to the best of their ability, while merely Friendly followers might weigh the potential risks and rewards before jumping into a dangerous situation. A Follower whose attitude falls to Indifferent is unhappy with their lot, and is likely to abandon the group at the first opportunity; a follower whose temperament falls to Unfriendly or Hostile will actively mutiny if they see a good opportunity to do so.

A disgruntled follower will typically bring the mood of the entire party down, sowing dissension and distrust among the ranks. The player character leading the retinue finds all tests managing any followers with an attitude other than Helpful harder, as long as at least one follower is actively Unfriendly or Hostile.

## Beast Companions

In addition to followers, player characters can gain a second retinue of beast companions - using their Survival instead of Bearing to determine the total hit dice worth of trained animals that they can manage (usually riding mounts or trained hunting dogs). Druids and hunters with expertise in Survival use their Survival to determine the total number of beast companions they can manage, and can even befriend wild animals or fey beasts.

Beast companions are usually under your control. They act on their own initiative, using their own action and movement as normal, to obey the commands of their handler to the best of their understanding and training. It is your job to set limits of what actions are or aren’t possible for a given beast companion to perform, based on its intelligence, anatomy, disposition, and training.

One exception is for a well-trained warhorse being actively ridden; the bond between rider and mount is sufficiently strong that they operate effectively as a single unit, using the mount’s movement during the rider’s turn as if it was his own, leaving the rider to perform their own full action as normal.
