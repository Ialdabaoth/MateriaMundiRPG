## Golem Armor

The golem armor is both an ancient symbol of feudal authority, and a marvel of dwarven magic. Golem armors are Huge magical constructs, usually humanoid in appearance, which can be “worn” and controlled by a golem knight - a trained warrior or stalwart who sits in the upper chest, and whose movements are mirrored by the golem armor through a synergy of engineering and arcane sympathetic magic, using a sequence of pulleys and gears to cause the golem armor to mimic the wearer’s movements. Supporting the golem knight is their engineer - a squire trained in the Arcane sciences who sits just below the knight’s harness and manages the golem armor’s various systems while the knight focuses on the fray.

Possessing golem armor is proof of patronage and legitimacy within the feudal hierarchy; these giant constructs are passed down from generation to generation, along with ancestral land to support them and the pledge to use them in defense of that land and its people. The first golem armors were constructed by the dwarven kingdom of Samar, to defend it against the encroaching might of the Old Empire. They failed, and were conquered, but the Empire was so impressed with the design that they immediately granted full citizenship to the dweomersmiths who designed and built them, and commissioned them to produce enough to outfit six Imperial Legions.

## Design and Construction

When the Old Empire fell, the knowledge to design and construct new golem armors was mostly lost; although a few of the Southern Sultanates retained or re-discovered the art. Recently, the industrious Hansa have at least partially re-discovered the art within the past two decades, and have risen to challenge the Sultanate for supremacy with a steady supply of newly-Knighted warriors in untested armors. The majority of golem armors, however, are over a thousand years old - passed down from father to son as a symbol of a noble family’s right to rule.

### Equipment, Speed, and Encumbrance

The internal systems of a golem armor weigh 2000 lbs, and can support another 8,000 lbs of additional equipment before becoming burdened. A golem armor's base walking speed is 20 paces per round (one field), and its dash speed when unencumbered is 120 paces per round (six fields). Its dash speed is reduced by 20 paces per round for every 2,000 lb of armor and equipment carried.

### Combat Capabilities

In combat, a golem knight uses their own talents, attack throw thresholds, and saving throw thresholds whenever necessary. The difficulty of a given task should be adjusted appropriately for a huge construct. The golem armor itself has an effective Strength and Constitution of +5 when performing ability checks, and uses the golem knight's other abilities.

Golem armors are 15 to 20 feet tall, and have ten sections: the Helm, Body, two Shoulders, two Arms, two Hands, and two Legs. When a golem armor is hit in combat by a large or huge attacker, the attacker must roll 2d6 to determine which section of the golem armor is damaged unless the attack is easy. Medium and smaller creatures choose which section to hit with a melee attack if their attack isn’t hard, but can only hit sections they can reach. Golem armors are essentially an array of rare arcane artifacts, all constructed around a central legendary dragon pearl called the materia core, and then surrounded by armored plates of steel and orichalc alloy.

Each section of a golem armor is an entire array of interlinked arcane magical items powered by this core; if the structure points of any one section are reduced to zero, each additional point of structure damage to that section automatically inflicts one critical hit. Each critical hit against a golem armor destroys one of its internal artifact slots at random in the section that was damaged — roll a d6 and eliminate that slot.

#### The Materia Core

There are three known designs for Materia cores. Each type of core must be integrated into a chassis that was designed to accommodate it, since the basic principles governing locomotion and power distribution are completely different between each type of core and chassis.

The simplest — if such a device can even be considered ‘simple’ — Materia core design is the alchemical cauldron. An alchemical cauldron uses a legendary flame pearl, suspended within an orichalc ‘boiler’ filled with water materia. The interaction of flame and water produces enormous pressure, which is vented through a series of valves at the top of the cauldron; each valve is connected through the golem armor’s gearworks, allowing steam pressure to be vented to the golem’s pistons when the knight moves their corresponding joints.

A much rarer design is the aetheric dynamo.

The dynamo uses a legendary earth pearl, suspended within a set of orichalc coils and rings charged with Air materia. The interaction of earth and air produces an incredibly powerful magnetic field, which the orichalc rings tap into and channel into a series of relays behind the dynamo - each relay is connected through the golem armor’s gearworks, allowing electrical power to be routed to the golem’s limb solenoids when the knight moves their corresponding joints.

Finally, the rarest materia core is the greenheart. A greenheart is a legendary wood pearl, suspended within a beating ‘heart’ sculpted from living wood materia. Within the heart’s chambers is even more wood materia, kept in a liquid state to form the golem armor’s ichor. This ichor flows through veins and arteries into living musculature, which is controlled by a nervous system grown organically into the gearworks.

Greenheart armors are made entirely without iron or steel components, allowing them to be piloted by fey champions. Additionally, greenheart golem armors are capable of selfrepair, slowly healing any armor or critical damage inflicted on them. A greenheart golem heals one critical slot, or one point of armor per damaged section, each day that it is allowed to rest.

#### Construction Process

Once the Materia Core is designed and constructed, each additional ‘slot’ is a single rare magic item that can be built by an enchanter with sufficient experience.

First, an array of Pressure Pumps, Power Lines, or Arteries is built above the Core, to distribute power to the limbs. Then the Cauldron, Dynamo, or Greenheart is built around the Core and distribution network.

Then the Gearworks are built atop the Hips, completing the Body section.

Second, the Engineer’s Seat, Knight’s Harness, and Scrying Lens are installed into the Helm. Third, each Hip Linkage is built, followed by Pressure Pumps, Power Lines, or Arteries, then followed by an array of Leg Pistons, Servos, or Musculature necessary to hold the golem armor upright, followed by a Foot Assembly, which completes the Leg.

Finally, each Shoulder Linkage is built, followed by the Pressure Pumps, Power Lines, or Arteries, then followed by an array of Arm Pistons, Servos, or Musculature, and then the Hand Linkage.

The resulting assembly is a single Huge 3 construct, controlled by the knight strapped into the harness in its chest. Each Shoulder and Hand section of the armor is essentially an empty scaffolding, ready to accept any weapons or other equipment desired.

Once the entire set of internals is completed, the Golem Armor’s armor plating is forged and fitted to it. Greenheart Golem Armors must organically ‘grow’ their own armor around their musculature at this point, creating interlocking plates indistinguishable from dragonscale.

Hand-held and shoulder-mounted equipment can be attached or removed from the golem armor at any time. Shoulders are attached to the chest through a set of rivets and pins – a process which takes roughly a day’s work for a team of engineers with the proper tools and scaffolding. Hand-held equipment is simply picked up by the golem armor and wielded; many noble and royal golem armors are attended to by teams of squires carrying additional hand-held options.

#### Alternate Designs

Several Golem Armor variants were experimented with during the height of the Old Empire. Almost none of these designs survive to the modern day, and those that do are typically in severe disrepair.

A golem armor may convert one or both shoulders into turrets. A turret shoulder mounts its own Scrying Lens and a Gunner’s Seat slot, giving a character in that seat control of all weapons in that Shoulder. Alternatively, a single oversized weapon may be mounted across both shoulders, taking up half its size in each shoulder. The weapon itself will sit above the Chest, with braces and moorings spanning either side of the back and shoulders to keep it secured. An oversized weapon cannot be mounted into a turret.

A golem armor may choose to trade flexibility for power, removing its Hand Linkage slot and permanently mounting a particular Hand assembly onto its Arm. This allows a twohanded melee weapon, or an oversized ranged weapon, to be mounted permanently into the combined space of the Arm + Hand.

If even more space is desired, the Lower Arm Piston can also be removed, and the Shoulder, Upper Arm Piston, and Pumps can be moved inwards from the arm to the shoulder. This frees up 12 full spaces into that Arm + Hand location, allowing it to mount even the largest bombard.

For those golem armors with two functioning hands, a single two-handed weapon may be constructed which splits its total spaces across both Hands locations. Each ‘half’ of a twohanded weapon will have its own structure points, which protect the spaces mounted on its ‘side’ of the weapon. Ammunition on either side of a two-handed ranged weapon may feed both sides of the weapon.

A few rare Golem Armors were built with two extra legs, giving them an animalistic or centauroid appearance. The extra legs grant the armor an additional 20 paces of movement, and make it easier to maintain balance or traverse difficult terrain.

Centauroid Golem Armors replace both shoulders with an extra pair of legs, while animal-form Golem Armors replace the arms.

#### Mounting Armor

Once all of a golem armor's equipment is mounted, armor is bolted onto the chassis.

The weight of each structure point of armor depends on the material used, which also determines the maximum armor per section.

Golem armors that mount a gull-wing cloak can only use feysilver or dragonscale armor on each shoulder, and cannot use orichalc armor on any other section.

### Construction Examples

The legendary golem armor worn by St. George the Dragon-Slayer was called *Ascalon*. It was an alchemical cauldron design, with 20 SP of orichalc armor covering the body and helm (2,000 lb), and another 15 SP of gilded steel on each arm, shoulder, and leg (2,250 lb).

Each shoulder held a Heavy Sunlance (500 lb x 2), and an Amulet of Protection (250 lb).

Altogether, it weighed 7,500 lb before wielding any hand-held weaponry, and thus could dash 10 paces per phase.

When St. George famously slew his dragon, the Ascalon wielded a shield (1500 lb) and a spear (1000 lb) in its hands, increasing its total weight to 10,000 lb - the maximum possible.

As such, it had a dash speed of only 3 fields per round.

By contrast, Percival's golem armor, the *Siege*

*Perilous*, was much more nimble. Clad entirely

in 800 lbs of feysilver, its only internal augmentations were a pair of Light Sunlances (100 lb x 2), bringing it to a total weight of only 3,000 lb. This allowed it to either carry a longsword (1000 lb) while maintaining a dash speed of 6 fields, or a longsword and shield (1000 lb + 1500 lb) at a dash speed of 5 fields.

### Siege Weapons

Golem Armors and other artifact vehicles can mount siege weapons, such as cannon or other heavy alchemical weaponry. Each siege weapon has a size rating, which is how many artifact slots that weapon takes up within an artifact vehicle, or the number of Sciencetrained engineers needed to man the weapon when mounted in a crew-served carriage.

Siege weapons typically have two damage ratings - the first represents the structure damage they inflict, and the second represents the damage dealt to creatures within 10’ that track hit points. Against creatures that track hit points, a direct hit from a siege weapon inflicts 5d6 piercing or elemental damage per structural damage die, plus (or minus) 1d6 per +1 static damage bonus.

#### Range

Golem armor ranged weapons can fire out to three times their listed range. Attacks out to the listed range are easy against other Huge targets; attacks out to twice the listed range are at normal difficulty, and attacks out to 3x the listed range are hard.

#### Area Weapons

Area weapons inflict damage to each section of a huge target caught within their blast; this damage is halved if the target saves vs. blast and the attack’s area does not fully cover the target’s occupied space. Area weapons must still make a ranged attack throw to hit their intended impact point; on a miss, the round scatters d6 paces in a random direction.

Bombards are massive siege cannon that fire explosive shells. Bombards are hard to aim while moving, or at targets within 500’. A bombard that rolls a ‘1’ on its d20 attack roll detonates, obliterating each section it is mounted to and dealing its attack damage to each of the mounting vehicle’s other sections. Seeker Weapons are large hollow orichalc tubes, each of which contains a single selfpropelled automaton that resembles something in between a crossbow bolt and an elongated bird. Each seeker ties into the vehicle’s scrying lens, and is aimed and launched by the armor’s engineer; on launch, a seeker will fly to the location designated, then ram its target or explode into a blast.

#### Ammunition

Ballistic siege weapons require ammunition.

The amount listed is the amount that can be carried directly within the weapon's internal magazine, or within one external magazine; each external magazine takes up 1 space and weighs 100 lbs. Any ammunition magazine in a fixed section (such as the shoulder or chest of a golem armor) may feed into weapons in adjacent sections without difficulty; ammunition in a ‘limb’ of a golem armor is more limited, and may only feed a weapon that is mounted in the same section as that ammunition bay. If an ammunition magazine suffers a critical hit, it utterly destroys the section it is mounted in.

A golem knight may use their action to load a weapon or ammunition bay in one section with a spare magazine from another section, or a golem armor may use their action to load an ammunition magazine cartridge into an adjacent golem armor’s weapon if neither golem armor moves during their action this round. The Old Empire's legions had "squire" class golem armor designed expressly for this purpose, who often follow heavier units as shield-bearers and munitions-loaders.

#### Power Draw

Each round on the engineer’s turn, the golem armor's materia core produces 5 points of aetheric power. This power is stored within the alchemical cauldron until used; the cauldron can store a maximum of 10 points of power when undamaged. Extra dragon pearls can be mounted in various locations to increase the amount of power produced per turn, but not the maximum amount stored.

In order to fire an aetheric weapon or attack with an enchanted melee weapon, the golem armor must draw power from the cauldron into the weapon. Each aetheric weapon has a power draw, which determines how much power the weapon needs to fire. Various other golem armor-sized enchantments also draw power from the cauldron when in use; if the cauldron's power ever drops below zero, the golem armor shuts down until reactivated.

## Golem Armor Combat

A golem armor operates as a single Huge 3 sized unit on the battlefield.

### Knight Actions

The knight directs the armor’s movement and attacks with all melee weapons and handheld ranged weapons. Attacks made by the knight use their melee and ranged attack thresholds. Each ranged weapon may be fired once per round. The Knight may fire all identical weapons in the same hand as a single attack. If the target has hit locations, each weapon in an attack rolls a separate hit location.

All available melee weapons may be used once per melee attack, and each weapon applies its damage to a single hit location roll. Any melee weapons with reach multiply their effective reach by 3.

The Knight may also use their action to perform a feat of strength, such as lifting a boulder or uprooting a tree. All feats of strength rolls made by the golem armor are scaled up to the armor’s size (multiplying any relevant weight limits by 100).

#### Knight Reactions

The golem knight can also make opportunity attacks with their armor’s melee weapons.

A golem armor’s balance is somewhat precarious; whenever a golem armor takes structure damage and one or more damage dice roll a '6', the golem knight must save vs. Hold to avoid falling prone. This saving throw is easy if the golem armor hasn’t suffered critical damage to either of its legs or to its gearworks, and hard if the golem armor has suffered critical damage to both systems.

### Engineer Actions

The engineer may use their action to perform an attack with any mounted ranged weapons, scry for hidden units with the golem armor’s scrying lens, activate or deactivate various enchantment modules, or overcharge the core. Overcharging the golem armor’s materia core requires the engineer to succeed at a Science check; on a failure, the entire golem armor shuts down before any of the knight’s actions can be resolved, the knight must save vs.

Hold to keep the golem armor upright, and the core must be restarted by the engineer as another action with another Science check.

While a golem armor’s core is shut down it is immobile and helpless, and the knight and engineer are both effectively blinded to everything happening outside of the armor.

The risk of shutdown comes with substantial rewards. Successfully overcharging the golem armor's materia core allows the engineer to choose one of the following benefits until the end of the current turn:

- The warrior may perform two actions

during this round.

- The engineer may increase the core’s

power output this round from 5 to to 2d6+3. The Science roll for this is easier for Dynamo-based golem armors, and harder for Greenhearts.

- The engineer may add +1D6 to each

unarmed or melee attack the warrior makes this round. This Science roll is harder for Dynamo-based golem armors, and easier for Greenhearts.

#### Engineer Reactions

The engineer may fire any Thunder Rattlers or Light Sunlances mounted on the armor’s shoulders or chest as anti-seeker point defense weapons whenever targeted by one or more seeker weapons as a modified "opportunity attack", rolling 1d6-1 per Rattler or Sunlance fired and reducing the incoming seeker salvo by that many missiles. The engineer may also engage any salvo of seekers that targets another unit, if the salvo’s line of fire passes within 100’.

### Damaging a Golem Armor

Roll 2d6 to determine which section of a golem armor is hit by incoming damage.

#### Component Damage

Each time a golem armor receives a critical hit from a weapon that deals structure damage, one of its internal components is destroyed; roll 1d6 to determine which. If a golem armor receives more structure damage than its current structure points to any location, one component in that section is also destroyed for each excess sp of damage.

Damaging a golem armor’s internal components degrades its performance, until those components can be replaced or repaired. A destroyed component must be removed from the golem armor, stripped down to its base parts, and rebuilt in order to be repaired.

**Helm (2, 12) -** The helm section contains the ‘Knight’s Harness’, which is a large suit of plate armor with chains and pulleys riveted to it, allowing the knight to control the golem. Below this seat is the Engineer’s Seat, where an engineer sits to maintain the Cauldron.

critical hits to either seat inflicts 3d6 hit point damage to that crew member.

In front of the knight’s harness is a ‘Scrying Glass’, which feeds sensory data to the knight; damage to this component blinds the armor.

Because the chest is where the knight and engineer sit; each point of structure damage dealt to the helm also inflicts 1 hp of thunder damage to both occupants.

**Body (7) -** The body contains the ‘Materia Core’ critical slot itself; a critical hit to the core obliterates the entire body section in an enormous blast, and deals two damage dice of structural elemental damage to everything within 5 paces. Above the core is the Gearworks, which translates the Knight’s motions into the Armor’s; damage to this delicate system makes all rolls harder.

Integrated with the Gearworks are the Pressure Pumps, which distribute power throughout the armor. The remaining slots are the Alchemical Cauldron itself; damage to any Pump or Cauldron slot reduces the armor's total power generation and storage by 1 each, as energy leaks and vents from the core.

**Shoulders (6: Left / 8: Right) -** Each shoulder may house as many ranged weapons and ammunition as will fit. A critical hit to any ranged weapon disables that weapon, while a critical hit to an ammunition bin or aetheric weapon will obliterate that shoulder.

If a golem armor’s shoulders have been outfitted with thunder pinions, a critical hit to either wing tears that wing off the armor, causing the armor to immediately plummet to the ground.

**Legs (5: Left / 9: Right) -** Each leg contains one ‘Hip Linkage’ critical slot; if this slot is damaged, the entire leg is torn free of the armor, hobbling it. Each leg also contains one 'Suspension' slot and one ‘Foot Assembly’ slot; if any of these slots are hit, the armor immediately falls prone, and all subsequent saving throws to move or brace are harder.

The remaining slots are Pistons, Solenoids, or Musculature, depending on the Materia Core used; damage to any of them slows the armor. **Arms (4: Left / 10: Right) -** Each arm contains one ‘Shoulder Junction’ critical slot; if this slot is damaged, the entire arm (and attached hand) is torn free of the Armor. Similarly, each arm contains one ‘Hand Linkage’ slot; if this slot is damaged, the corresponding hand section is torn free. The remaining slots are ‘Arm Pistons’; damage to any of them makes any attack, parry, or other action performed with that arm harder.

**Hands (3: Left / 11: Right) -** Each hand can mount one or more ranged weapons along with their ammunition, wielding it like a giant crossbow. A critical hit to any ranged weapon disables that weapon, while a critical hit to an ammunition bin or a charged aetheric weapon will obliterate that entire handheld weapon. Alternatively, a hand can mount a giant melee weapon or a shield. Golem Armor shields and melee weapons are essentially giant solid slabs of metal, and thus halve all structure damage dealt to them from non-sundering attacks. A critical hit to a melee weapon or shield is resolved as a sundering attack, and vice-versa.
