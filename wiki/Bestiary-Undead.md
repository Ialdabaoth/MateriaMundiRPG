## The Undead

An undead creature is any being from the Shadow which has pushed through to manifest into the Material world. Breaching the veil requires an enormous amount of willpower and drive, so only those beings with intense unresolved passions manage it.

The undead are chiefly divided into two categories - ghosts and wights. A ghost is any undead being which does not possess a corporeal form, but instead uses its sheer will to exert influence over the material world via miracle-like effects. A wight, on the other hand, is a corpse animated by undead energy from the Shadow - either a mere animated construct such as a skeleton or a plagueshambler, or a being animated by its own possessing ghost, such as a revenant or a vampire.

The energies that animate the undead are inimical to the life-force that flows through all living beings. When a priest performs any miracle that would heal an undead creature’s hit points, that miracle instead inflicts an amount of damage equal to the amount it would normally heal. If the healing miracle has a range of touch, the priest must make a melee attack throw in order to touch it. If a priest miracle heals multiple creatures, it heals those that are living while simultaneously damaging those that are undead.

All undead are also vulnerable to feysilver. Damage to an undead creature from any feysilver weapon makes all saving throws harder until it heals.

## Ghosts

Ghosts, the most common form of undead, are those restless spirits who do not possess a material body. As spirits, they are immune to physical attacks, and reducing them to 0 hit points merely forces them back across the Veil into the Shadow, where they cannot return to the mortal world until after sundown, within the 3 days around the next full moon.

Truly ridding a haunting of a ghost requires fulfilling its unfinished business, or binding it with divine oaths to abandon the Material world; both are tasks for a Priest, especially one dedicated to the Death domain.

**Spirit Form -** As incorporeal beings, ghosts are immune to all damage except force, psychic, and radiant damage. When its hit points are reduced to zero it is banished, and cannot manifest again until the next sunrise or sunset.

### Shade

*(Level 1)*  

Shades are the sad echoes of mortals who die with will and passion sufficient to keep fighting for something, but insufficient to hold onto their identity and consciousness. A shade repeats a few scripted sets of actions, over and over again, until the passion that drove it to manifest is fulfilled. Any attempt to interfere with a shade’s routine will be met with terrifying hostility.

### Wraith

*(Level 2 to 5)*  

Wraiths are the spirits of mortals with sufficient willpower to maintain their identity and consciousness as they cross the Veil into Shadow. While most ghosts choose to move onto the Everafter, or content themselves in Shadow with maintaining their descendants’ ancestor-cults, a rare few vex the living with darker passions.

## Wights

Wights are those undead which possess corporeal form. The most common wights are animated skeletons and revenants - mindless automata restored to a mockery of life by necromancy, possessing all the malice and fear and rage of the Shadow but no independent thought or will.

**Animated Corpse -** Any mortal, natural animal, or fey beast can be made into a skeleton or shambler. Wights are immune to poison, psychic, and necrotic damage, and automatically succeed at any saves vs. Fear.

Shamblers suffer half damage from all piercing weapons. They are instantly slain by any divine attack that inflicts hit point damage. A shambler's actions are always slow, and it can’t dash or react.

**Infectious -** A creature bitten by a shambler must save vs. Doom or contract the plague.

Plague prevents a victim from healing hit points, and inflicts 1d6 damage per day until the victim dies. Saving throws vs. plague are hard, and natural recovery requires three days of consecutive saves.

Curing the plague magically requires a remove curse or restoration miracle; a cure disease miracle will merely allow the target to regain hit points normally for one hour. Any creature killed by plague rises as a shambler on the next sunset.

### Revenant

*(Level 1 to 5)*  

More dangerous are the intelligent undead, who possess all the cunning and drive that they did in life. An intelligent undead is a conduit for necromantic power, able to cast dark curses or raise armies of lesser undead to do their bidding.

A revenant is the result of a curse, or a botched attempt to revive a mortal. A revenant possesses all of the intelligence and memories of the mortal it once was, but its living spirit does not control its body; instead, it is guided only by malevolent, feral desires for cruelty and vengeance on the living.

#### Necromancy

The revenant has dark faith equal to its level, which it may use to cast any miracle from the Death or War domain.

**Raise Undead -** By touching a corpse and spending faith equal to the corpse’s desired level, the revenant may bid it arise and join its ranks as a shambler or skeleton, based on its current level of decay. The Revenant then rolls 2d6 + its own hit dice; if the result is greater than the total number of shamblers and skeletons the revenant currently controls, the corpse arises as a new shambler or skeleton under the Revenant’s control.

### Vampire

*(Level 6 to 9)*  

Vampires are the apex predator of the undead, stalking the night to drink the blood of the living. Vampires may use their blood to create thralls. A thrall is a mortal or vampire minion that has been fed the blood of a vampire - even unwillingly or unknowingly - and whose will is thus completely enslaved to their vampiric master.

#### Vampiric Blood

The vampire has blood equal to its hit dice, which it can spend as follows:

**Blood Frenzy -** The vampire may spend three blood to gain an additional action on its turn. **Undead Strength** -The vampire may spend any amount of blood when it hits with a Strength-based melee attack to add 1d6 to its damage roll per blood spent.

**Blood Potency -** The vampire may spend one blood to make any one roll easier.

**Dark Miracles -** The vampire may cast a necromantic version of any Divine miracle by spending blood equal to twice its circle.

Miracles which deal damage inflict necrotic damage.

**Enthrall -** The vampire gives a mortal or vampire with fewer hit dice than its own a drop of its blood to drink, enthralling them and granting them the vampire’s bite attack and a +1 bonus to each of their abilities for one lunar month.

Enthralled creatures cannot save vs. their master’s powers, and must make a save vs.

Curse whenever adjacent to a living mortal to deny the desire to drink blood. If an enthralled mortal tastes another mortal’s mortal living blood and is subsequently killed, it will arise upon death as a new vampire, still enthralled to its master.

**Undead Vigor -** a vampire is immune to poison and disease, and halves all damage received from any non-Divine attack that isn’t fire or radiant damage, or inflicted by a feysilver weapon. Any fire or radiant damage that a vampire receives also inflicts ongoing fire damage.

Even if a vampire is “killed”, it rises again the next night unless it is staked through the heart with a rowanwood shaft, after which the  lay to rest miracle must be performed on its incapacitated body by a priest, followed by decapitation with a feysilver blade. Correctly performing this process is a hard Lore check.

### Lich

*(Level 10)*  

A lich is an undead creature whose soul has been preserved through ancient Atlantean arts, known only in modern times through the funeral-rites of the Khemetian pharaohs.

Unlike a vampire, a lich requires no sustenance to maintain their undead form; so long as their animating relic remains undisturbed, they can slowly regenerate from literally any injury, including the complete annihilation of their body. A lich temporarily bereft of physical form functions as a 10 hd wraith until they can possess a new host, or their original form regenerates sufficiently to move about.

#### Master Necromancer

Liches have 10 points of dark faith, which they can use as follows:

**Priest-King of Undeath -** A Lich may perform any divine miracle from the Death domain by spending faith equal to its circle; they can  also prepare miracles from any other domains into prayer strips. Their considerable knowledge and occult power also allows them to prepare arcane spells as a fifth level wizard, spending their faith as they prepare the spell as if it was a prayer strip.

**Raise Undead -** By touching a corpse and spending faith equal to the corpse’s base hit dice, the lich may bid it arise and join its ranks as a shambler or skeleton, based on its current level of decay. Unlike a revenant, a lich has no limit to the number of mindless undead that they can control.

**Animating Relic -** Each lich has an animating relic that binds their soul to the material plane. If this relic is found, a sufficiently powerful group of Divine priests might be able to destroy it with a collective ritual. Determining the necessary rituals and sacrifices is likely to be an extended, and arduous, research task.

**Blush of Life -** When at full hit points, a lich appears to be a living, breathing mortal in exceptionally good health. Only as they take damage is their undead nature revealed.

When the lich first takes damage, anyone paying close attention can make a hard Alertness check to notice that something is amiss. Once the lich is injured (below half its hit points), this check becomes easy; at zero hit points, noticing is automatic.

A lich in the process of regenerating from destruction is a ghastly sight indeed, often little more than a bleached skeleton with dried flesh hanging off it in rags.
