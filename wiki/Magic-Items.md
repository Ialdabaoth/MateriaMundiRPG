## Magic Items

## Magic Item Traits

During the course of the game, a character might find or craft various magically enhanced items. Magic Items, or the materials necessary for crafting them, are some of the more rewarding forms of treasure that an adventurer might find in a dungeon.

### Rarity

Rarity represents the general potency of the artifact’s components and ingredients. There are four levels of rarity: common, uncommon, rare, and legendary. A common artifact can typically produce a single first-circle effect, an uncommon artifact can generate a single second-circle effect or two first-circle effects, and a rare artifact can generate a third-circle effect, or more rarely might hold multiple spells at first- or second-circle. Each legendary item is unique, often with a number of effects at different levels. Many magic items power their magic by incorporating dragon pearls, which allows them to recharge each sunrise and sunset (or faster, if a more powerful pearl is used to power weaker magics). Other items incorporate depletable materia charges.

### Origin

Magic items can have an Arcane, Primal, or Divine origin. Wizards and dweomersmiths craft Arcane magic items, while druids craft Primal magic items; Divine magic items are not crafted, but instead occur spontaneously but rarely in the presence of truly heroic actions. Certain magic items have both an Arcane and a Primal version; the design and construction methods of each are recognizably different, but studying an item from one origin might still give a magic-user of a different class some clues or inspiration for their own designs. Magic-users may craft common items at third level, uncommon items at fifth level, rare items at seventh level, and legendary items at ninth level.

A magic item can be automatically used by any magic-user whose magic corresponds to its origin, and magic weapons and armor may be used without a check. Non-magic-users can attempt to use other magic items with a task check (Science for arcane items or Survival for primal items); this check is easy for common items and hard for rare items.

### Item Type

A character has several places on their body where a magic item might be worn. Each item is designed to be worn in a specific area, and does not work unless it is worn there.

#### Weapons

*are simply held in the hand like*  

normal weapons. A character may wield two different one-handed magical weapons at once, or a magical weapon in one hand and a focus in the other.

#### Armor

*is worn on the entire body; a character*  

can only wear one suit of armor at a time.

#### Amulets

*are designed to be worn around the*  

neck. Some amulets are designed as jewelry, while others are full-sized capes or cloaks. A character may only wear one amulet at a time.

#### Belts

*are also designed to be worn over the*  

body, usually around the waist. A character may wear one belt at a time, and may wear a magical belt and magical armor simultaneously.

#### Boots

*are designed to be worn on the feet, and*  

always come in pairs (or sets of four, for fourlegged creatures). A character gains no benefit from wearing less than a full set.

#### Crowns

*are designed to be worn on the head.*  

Some crowns are incorporated into full helms, meant to be worn with a matching suit of armor. The helm’s effect will be separate from the armor’s.

#### Rings

*are designed to be worn over the fingers,*  

although some “rings” are actually shaped as full-hand gloves or gauntlets. A character may wear at most one ring on each hand. Certain ring items are paired; wearing only one ring of a pair might impart a lesser effect, or no effect at all, depending on the referee’s judgment.

Finally, Wondrous Items are a catch-all category for magic items that are neither worn nor wielded, but are used in some other fashion.

## Creating New Magic Items

Most magic items are relics of the Old Empire, passed down from generation to generation due to their utility. Some can be duplicated by modern alchemists, while others are priceless heirlooms worth a kingdom's ransom.

Wizards, druids, and dweomersmiths may attempt to craft new magic items. The crafter must have the desired item's blueprints, as well as all the necessary ingredients.

Dweomersmiths may also construct rapid prototypes of arcane magic items called enchantments in a matter of minutes and from mere scrap; these items operate identically to normal magic items, but require their creator’s constant maintenance to continue to function. In order to hold a permanent enchantment, a weapon, armor, or other device must be a masterwork item before it is enchanted; any enchantment bonuses will replace its innate masterwork bonus with their own effect. The object must also be made with some kind of compartment or gemstone-like setting, which will hold the materia that will power its magic. Almost any arcane spell or primal talisman can be woven into a magic item by including materia that corresponds to the effect’s element; this materia may be refined and perfected into a dragon pearl, which is a nugget of condensed and purified materia that resembles a small bead or gem, to allow the item to recharge without expending fresh materia. Without a dragon pearl, magic items have a fixed number of charges, which must be replenished with fresh materia by a skilled alchemist once they run out.

In addition, orichalc and feysilver alloys must be incorporated into the design - these are woven into the sigils and runes of the effect’s arcana, which channel the materia’s energy into the item’s effect. Approximately ⅛ oz worth of orichalc and feysilver must be woven into a common artifact, about ¼ oz into an uncommon artifact, or ½ oz into a rare artifact. Finally, the physical substance of the item must be made from high-quality materials capable of withstanding the spell’s energies, which usually means hides, woods, or other materials harvested from creatures with magic-like elemental powers that match the elemental aspect of the channeled forces.

The crafting process itself takes an amount of time depending on the rarity of the object being constructed. Each day, the crafter and each assistant make a Finesse check, with a difficulty based on the item's rarity. Each success on the part of the crafter accumulates +1d6 build points towards the construction, +1 additional build point per successful assistant. A common item requires 5 build points to complete, an uncommon item requires 25, a rare item requires 100, and a legendary item might require a thousand or more.

At the end of the project, the referee makes a Science check on behalf of the crafter vs. a difficulty based on the item’s rarity. If this roll succeeds, the item works as intended; if it fails, on the other hand, the item appears to function normally but bears a curse.

### Cursed Items

Cursed items tend to activate their powers at the least convenient times and use them in ways that hinder rather than help the creature using them. Some cursed items bind to the user and cannot be removed without Remove Curse or Dispel Magic; successfully casting either on such an item detaches it from the wearer and deactivates it, but does not uncurse the item — it must be disassembled and rebuilt from scratch to remove its flaws.

### Magic Item Maintenance

Arcane magic items must be maintained, or their performance will begin to degrade. An arcane magic item that has been actively used needs approximately ten minutes of active maintenance per day from a dweomersmith or wizard, or its quality will reduce from good to fair. An arcane item in fair quality requires an easy Science check for a magic-user to properly activate or maintain it; activating it also becomes one step harder for non-magicusers. An arcane item in poor quality requires a normal Science check for a magic-user to properly activate or maintain it, and cannot be activated by a non-magic-user; on a failure, the item becomes cursed.

Primal magic items do not track quality, but instead have rudimentary personalities of their own, tracking their attitude in a similar manner as henchmen or animal companions.
