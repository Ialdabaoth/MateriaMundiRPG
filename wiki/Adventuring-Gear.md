## Adventuring Gear

#### Potion Belt

A potion belt is a simple leather belt or sash with six padded pockets sewn into it. It allows the wearer to hold up to six small items in a protected fashion, leaving each item readily available to be drawn and used as a single action.

As the name implies, potion belts are most often used by alchemists to store brewed alchemy bottles or small tools and supplies, but magic-users and scouts also find them extremely useful for holding bottles of magic ink, unprepared raw materia, prayer strips and brushes, lockpicking tools, throwing knives, and so on.

#### Rucksack

A rucksack is a heavy canvas and leather bag with shoulder straps, designed to evenly distribute weight. It allows a medium-sized mortal to carry up to 50 lbs of gear with minimal encumbrance (1 encumbrance point), or 100 lbs of gear with moderate encumbrance (2 encumbrance points). Any more weight risks damaging the bag.

Small rucksacks can also be made, which allow a small mortal to carry up to 25 lbs of gear with minimal encumbrance, or 50 lbs of gear with moderate encumbrance.

#### Toolkit

Many different kinds of craftsman’s and tradesman’s tools exist. Each type of tool requires familiarity to use properly. Some toolkits, such as jeweler’s tools or lockpicking tools, are small enough to fit into a single pouch of a potion belt, while others, such as blacksmith’s tools or carpenter’s tools, are each the size of a medium weapon, and might even be serviceable as such in a pinch.

#### Survival Gear

While armor is primarily designed to protect from violence, other clothing is designed to protect from the elements. Different kinds of survival clothing protect against cold, heat, sun, rain, and so on; an unprepared adventurer will find various saving throws and Survival tests harder in hostile environments.

#### Fine Clothes

Well-tailored clothing that is appropriate to an occasion can mean the difference between a good impression and a disastrous one in a social setting. Without the right attire, most Charm and Wit actions in social situations that expect proper dress are much harder.

#### Decanter

Most mortals require around 32 ounces of fresh water per day to stay healthy. A proper canister for drinking water will hold around this amount, and will be designed to not break or tear in rough situations. Some decanters are lined to help safely hold fluids other than water.

#### Rations

Dried meat and fruit, nuts, biscuits, and seasoned preservatives make for a hearty - if dry - meal that won’t spoil on the road. Each day’s worth of rations for a medium mortal weighs approximately one pound.

#### Ammunition

A quiver of 20 arrows or crossbow bolts fits into a single container approximately the size of a medium weapon, with a strap that allows it to sling over a character’s back.

#### Dungeoneer’s Kit

Various items have become traditional “required gear” for seasoned explorers. A typical dungeoneer’s kit contains a 10 ft pole of hardened wood with an iron ring on one end that doubles as a quarterstaff, 100 feet of hemp rope, a small oilcloth tent, a lantern with a clasp that can affix to the ring on the end of the pole, a flask of lamp oil (enough to keep the lantern lit for 8 hours), two pairs of climbing spikes (one pair for the boots and one pair for the hands), two palm-sized silver mirrors, and a 5-lb lead plumb weight with a loop to thread a rope through. The whole package weighs around 20 lb, and (other than the pole) fits neatly into a rucksack with some room to spare for treasure and rations.

Finding absurd new ways to use these items has become a tradition among adventurers, who swap tavern stories of their exploits in exchange for drinks and renown.
